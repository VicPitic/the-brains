## Playbook: building a niche directory site in about 8 hours

John built a directory around a hot topic (Next.js boilerplates) and monetized the SEO traffic. His sequence:
1. Spot a hot topic.
2. Check Google search volume.
3. Find a high-traffic keyword.
4. Buy a domain on GoDaddy.
5. Build the directory.
6. Get SEO traffic.
7. Earn from sponsors, ads and referrals.

He says it took him 8 hours in total and that "anyone can do this."

## Picking the topic, keyword and domain

- John saw hype around Next.js boilerplates. He searched the term in Google Keyword Planner and found low-competition keywords with good traffic.
- Keyword Planner is his idea source: search the hot topic and look for low-competition, decent-traffic terms.
- He searched for the domain on GoDaddy and always starts with .com. After his tests, ".com beats crap out of everything else."
- He bought nextjsstarter.com, a domain that matches the keyword.

## Building the directory fast

- He built the site in under an hour from a template using his own builder, Unicorn Platforms (a directory template).
- He made the logo with ChatGPT's DALL-E.
- He took the top 10 keywords from Google Keyword Planner and placed them on the home page.
- To fill it, he scraped dev tools from Product Hunt and DevHunt and found every boilerplate listed there. He also searched Hacker News, dev.to, GitHub, Google and X.
- That gave about 50 items. Authors then submitted about 100 more through a form.

## SEO content and indexing

- He turned on SEObot to generate 100 blog articles. The articles came out really good, and he learned a few things reading them.
- He turned on IndexRusher to get all 250 pages indexed. He notes a new domain can take months or longer to get indexed otherwise.
- He launched the site first and let it warm up for 2 months. The blog went live in month 3.
- Traffic grew day after day.
- Lesson: "Whenever I tried launched many pages at once, I failed at growing the site."

## Monetization

- Decent SEO traffic plus click traffic from a few viral posts and blogs made the site attractive to Next.js boilerplate authors.
- His main revenue was selling "featured-spot" placements to those authors. He also lists sponsors, ads and referrals as revenue sources.
- Because the audience is the boilerplate authors' target buyers, the directory sells them exposure directly.

## Next steps John planned

- More blog articles around Next.js "how-tos."
- Programmatic SEO (he says he has "a crazy cool idea" for it).
- Run ListingBott to grow domain rating by getting 100 more backlinks pointing to the pages that already have traffic.
