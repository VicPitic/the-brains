## Validating ideas and building what users want

- John says he "wasted at least 5 years building stuff nobody needed." His first rule is to validate the idea before building.
- Ego is the enemy. "It's not about me, but the user." He must want what the user wants, not what he wants.
- He sells features before building them. He asks existing users whether they want a feature, and runs DMs with 10-20 users every day to chat about his ideas and the features he wants to add. He sees what resonates most and only builds those.
- He does not hold on to a bad project for long, max 1 year. Projects usually fail because the idea is so wrong it can't be pivoted, or because the team is good one by one but can't make it as a team. He says don't drag this out for years.

## Team, hiring and process

- Never hire managers. Hire only doers until product-market fit (PMF).
- Hire only fullstack devs. He says "There is nothing less productive in this world than a team of developers." His model is one full stack dev building the whole product.
- Hire only people you would want to hug. A mentor told him this in 2015. If he doesn't want to hug someone, he dislikes them, even if he can't say why, and sooner or later they will have a conflict and break up.
- Outsource nothing until PMF. Startup work needs to be done in a slightly different, more creative way that is integrated into the vision. Outsourced members get no love and no case for the product, so it is just another assignment in a boring job.
- "Scrum is a Scam." A team that has to be nagged every morning like kindergarten children will eventually fail. His good work came with grown-ups who managed their own stuff, syncing on goals and plans over chat.

## Funding: bootstrap and chase users, not investors

- He says to bootstrap. He raised more than 10 times (preseed, seed and series A), and each round was a 3-9 month project with weekly meetings and lots of distraction.
- He could have afforded to bootstrap but went the VC-funded way anyway. He says he didn't know bootstrapping was something he could do or that anyone does.
- "Chase users, and then investors will be chasing you."
- He avoids working or partnering with corporates. They seem like amazing opportunities, with promises of millions of users, but every time none of it happens. You talk to regular employees who waste time, destroy focus, shift priorities, and bring in no users or money.

## Distribution: SEO, global market, Twitter and landing pages

- Do SEO from day 2, as early as possible. He ignored it for 14 years and calls it his biggest regret.
- Go global from day 1. If product and marketing are good, it works on the global market too; if bad, it won't work locally either. Going global gives a 100x bigger upside if it works.
- Post on Twitter daily. He started in March 2023, and it is his primary source of new connections and traffic.
- The landing page is the least important thing in a startup. Pick an average template and edit the texts. His reasoning: 90% of users reach your site from a blog article, social media post or recommendation, so they already have intent and there is no need to "convert" them again.
- Tech conferences are a waste of time. They cost money, energy and time, and you never really meet anyone. Most attendees are corporate employees sent as a perk, with very few fellow makers.

## What to build: B2B, not consumer

- He says don't build consumer apps, only B2B. Consumer apps are like a lottery, where "just 0.00001% make it big."
- Even with many users, monetization is a further challenge. He spent 4 years in consumer apps and regrets it.

## Where to put your money and attention

- Invest all your money into your own startups and friends' startups, not crypto, stock market or property.
- His math: if he had kept investing all his money in his friends' startups, he would have about 70 investments, and 3 of them eventually became unicorns. Even 1 would have made the bank. Since 2022 he has put all his money into his products, friends and network.
- Don't get distracted by hype such as crypto. He lost 1.5 years of his life to it and met the worst people along the way: scammers and thieves. Some close friends turned into thieves because that was so common in that space.
