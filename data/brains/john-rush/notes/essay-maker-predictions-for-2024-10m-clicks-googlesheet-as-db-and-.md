## John's 2024-2026 prediction: makers multiply and build many products

- He predicts exponential growth in the number of indie makers. Almost every corporate dev is building a side project or planning to.
- Example from uptime bots: 10 in 2020, 20 in 2021, 30 in 2022, 60 in 2023. He projects 200+ in 2024 and 1000+ in 2025.
- Reason: "1% of tech is complicated rocket science, but the rest is an easy 2 week project for a good productive developer who uses the right tools."
- Analogy: popular singers launch brands, act in movies and make commercials because they have a distribution channel and can push almost anything to a loyal audience. He says tech is entering the same world.
- There is a limit to how perfect one SaaS can get. After that comes overengineering: users are happy but the maker keeps adding things nobody asked for. So makers build more tools for the same audience instead of endless features.
- By end of 2024 he expects very few makers doing a single product. Most will run many, some more than 10, some more than 100.

## Distribution beats dev force: corporates and VCs lose

- He is "totally bearish on corporates" in the new world. They have a large dev force and little distribution force. "In the new world, distribution is 90% of what one needs to win."
- Corporates will launch new brands that pretend to be standalone, not products under their own brand.
- He calls the old VC-backed startup world a thing of the past that won't come back. Money no longer helps growth, so what everyone needs is distribution channels.
- He expects 99% of VC money to go into "platforms" that few devs can build. Tools, sites, SaaS and apps will be bootstrapped, self-funded or funded by "maker labels".

## Maker + influencer collabs and "maker labels"

**Collabs**
- Influencers with over a million followers no longer make good money. He says the value of a single follower dropped 100x because the number of people with over 100k followers grew 100x, and brands and ads pay little.
- He predicts influencers will partner with indie makers to build a product for their audience and split the profits. Those who do it first "gonna make millions".

**Maker labels**
- Like music labels, movie studios and sports agents, almost no famous star is on their own. Labels handle groundwork, contracts, negotiation and distribution so the artist focuses on the craft.
- The same will happen to indie makers through alliances, startup studios and holdcos.
- Benefit 1: makers focus on product, UX, talking to users and product-led promotion instead of everything else.
- Benefit 2 (the bigger one): distribution. If an alliance has 50 makers, their audiences cross-promote. Fully independent makers will struggle against those in alliances.
- He notes maker "groups" already support each other's tweets and Product Hunt launches, and says he respects them.
- Final prediction: this leads to several monopolies taking most of the audience. He cites Peter Thiel: "competition is for the losers", and the winner makes a monopoly.

## Google Sheets as the database for public directories

- After 15 years of using databases, in 2023 he switched his public projects and directories to Google Sheets.
- Origin story: he launched osssoftware one night and posted on Hacker News, dev.to and X. It went viral overnight with over 20K visitors. Hundreds of comments came in about data quality, corrections and suggestions. He couldn't keep up, so he made the sheet public so everyone could comment.
- Result: osssoftware.org became a crowd-sourced directory of open-source projects. He says building comment-moderation software would have been a waste.
- Benefits: free regardless of traffic or users, and "100x easier" to edit and maintain from user feedback than a conventional database.
- His directories run on unicornplatform.com plus Google Sheets: allgpts.co, osssoftware.org, nextjsstarter.com, topwebsitebuilders.org.
- Next planned on the same stack: auth, ratings, reviews, bookmarking, comments, upvotes, all on publicly open Google Sheets.
- Transparency angle: anyone can verify the data is real, since the sheet has public revision history. He calls this "decentralized immutable storage" that people hoped to get from blockchain.

## Forced Google indexing (IndexGoogle) and SEObot quality

- He underestimated forced Google indexing. He turned on indexgoogle.com for internal testing 2 weeks earlier and was pleased with the results.
- Getting the Google Indexing API to work for an unlimited number of pages was hard because of limits. Google granted a large API quota, so he planned to launch the following week.
- Waitlist invites go out at 10 a day. SEObot (seobotai.com) users get it included in the "PRO" subscription.
- On SEObot he is "100% bullish" on generating articles that humans enjoy reading and Google ranks high. "We're not in the garbage content business."
- Quality approach: he spends almost a dollar per article, because each goes through numerous cycles of generation, improvement and research.

- He stops building directories ("It all works now") and plans 90 more by the end of 2024.
- New experiment: 15 basic SaaS tools, each doing one thing clearly, with a one-button user experience. Domains are chosen purely from SEO research.

## Directory promo network: 10M clicks, $0 revenue, and the plan

- His directories (allgpts.co, devhunt.org, osssoftware.org, nextjsstarter.com, topwebsitebuilders.org) drove 10 million clicks to third-party projects. Revenue so far: $0.
- Plan: build a directory promo network. ListingBott was the first step, but he realized convincing directory owners to join a new project from a no-name would be hard.
- Workaround: launch 100 successful directories, each with Domain Rating above 30 and over 10k visitors a month. Then build the promo network on top of them. Once it has clients and revenue, it is easier to recruit other directory owners.
- How it works: an indie maker or small startup with low DR or traffic enters a URL and loads $100. The tool finds the most relevant directories and features the product there until it reaches the click target (for example $100 buys 100 clicks), then removes it from all featured sections. Buyers can try it for as little as $5 for 5 clicks.
- Why: most directories monetize badly (Google Ads, Carbon Ads, irrelevant banners). He knows owners with millions of visitors making just a few thousand dollars a month.
- Best monetization is selling "sponsored" spots, but finding a buyer is hard and buyers can't judge directory quality. Software and AI would match promoted products with relevant directories.
- He said he was open to partnering with other directory founders and expected more in January.
