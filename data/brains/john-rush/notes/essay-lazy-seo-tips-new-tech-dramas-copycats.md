## John's view that a big X following replaces Y Combinator

- John says YC "isn't 'a must' anymore."
- In his view, 20k followers on X gives a moat similar to being in a YC batch.
- Two benefits he names:
  - Successful founders answer your DMs.
  - You get large inbound interest from VCs.
- He says he had no followers a year before and hit 40k by October 2024. His newsletter bio claims 20k followers and 30 million views on X, plus over 8,000 Substack subscribers.
- He treats audience-building as the founder's substitute for an accelerator network. He publishes startup playbooks, SEO and directory guides, and case studies like "From a Failed App to a Successful Vertical SaaS" to feed it.

## John's take on the SEO industry and his "lazy SEO" playbook

- John says SEO is "living its chatgpt moment." Ahrefs and Semrush trash-talk each other publicly and compete by buying other firms.
- He chose not to join the fight. While they did, he published a "lazy SEO playbook" as an X thread on 2024-10-17 (https://x.com/johnrushx/status/1847272685463621937).
- The tactics are in the thread, not in this essay.
- He also runs a directory guide, "DirectoryVerse" (johnrush.me/directory-guide). He claims a "maker mafia" is going to take over the directory world.

## Copycats: what happens when a product gets traction

- Someone cloned John's product ListingBott (listingbott.com), "even the name."
- He describes the indie hacker scene as turning into a reality show: clones, vanishing makers, and hate tweets.
- He argues that people who can't create value try to go viral by tweeting hate at successful makers.
- He notes that Zuckerberg wins by cloning successful products and even a successful look, and is "like water, adapts to any surface."
- Cloning is a standard play at every scale, so expect to be copied once you work.

## Presale scams and buyer trust

- John cites a maker who ran a boilerplate presale and disappeared.
- Early backers had bought 143 lifetime deals (LTDs) on AppSumo in August alone, and are now suing their bank for chargebacks.
- The case shows why presales and lifetime deals depend on trust. A maker who takes money and vanishes damages the whole indie scene.

## Acquisitions as a way to control a market

John groups three 2024 deals under "everyone wanna be a monopoly":
- **Webflow acquired Greensock.** His read is that the tool will now work only for Webflow and stop serving its competitors.
- **Semrush acquired Third Door Media.** His read is that startups are buying news media to control the narrative.
- **Stripe acquired a crypto startup.**

His reading is that companies buy the dependencies, media and distribution around them.

## Consistency beats excuses

- John points to a creator who got 65 million YouTube subscribers in 2 months.
- His challenge to readers: "What stops you from doing the same? What's your excuse? Just keep showing up for 2 months and u win."
- It is the same bet as his own X growth: post consistently and let the audience compound.
