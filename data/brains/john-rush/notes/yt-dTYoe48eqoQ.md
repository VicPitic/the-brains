## Why directories are an SEO game and why most fail

- John has built about 50 directories (he says "20" at first, then "a lot more like 50"), and about 12 are doing pretty well. He claims over 10 million in traffic and has sold one.
- He runs a product called Listing Bot that finds hundreds of new directories every day, so he watches what others build.
- He says about 99% of directories fail, as with startups generally. It looks easy, but the market is open and crowded.
- The number one reason they fail is too little keyword research.
- A SaaS can find product-market fit by pivoting, because the pain was real to start with. A directory is purely an SEO game.
- People never remember or bookmark a directory. They land on it from a Google search.
- Bad keywords (low traffic, high competition) never win, and he has never seen an exception.

## Keyword research with Google Keyword Planner plus Google search

John tried Ahrefs, Semrush and others. He always uses Google itself, in combination:

1. Start in Google search and let it help you phrase the keyword. A keyword is always more than one word, and more words make it easier to win.
2. Use Keyword Planner for traffic.
3. Use Google search to judge competition.
4. Check year-over-year growth. Fast growth suggests the space isn't crowded yet, because it wasn't popular a year ago. No growth may mean it's oversaturated.
5. Ping-pong between the two tools, narrowing from the broad term to a sub-term.

Demo: "best tent" showed 10K to 100K searches. "Popup tent" showed 900%+ year-over-year growth. The results were paid ads, a Cambridge dictionary page and e-commerce sites, with no directory and no specialist blog.

- Ignore "high competition" in Keyword Planner for purchasable items. That figure is competition for ads, and people scroll past ads.
- Searchers read URLs closely. A URL like "best popup tents" stands out against unrelated ones.

## Picking a domain

- Often the keyword is good but the domain is gone, and you have to give up on it. In the demo, a premium domain was listed at 20K. John never pays more than $9 for a domain.
- A .org domain is an advantage when competing with commercial results. You look like Wikipedia, a place for information rather than transactions. He called the .org version of popup tents "just amazing".
- His rule is 80% of a directory's success is keyword research and domain, 15% is data curation (good items) and 5% is technology and design.

## Building fast with no-code

- Most people reverse the 80/20 rule and spend weeks coding or polishing design. John says to go no-code, using his Unicorn Platform or any other.
- No-code tools give you the ongoing work (promotion, updates) out of the box and ship with templates.
- Nobody cares about brand identity. The plainer and less salesy the site looks, the better, because you are convincing people you're there to inform them and not to take their money. Craigslist is the example of an ugly site that works.
- A Unicorn Platform directory template with big images works for tents. The database is a Google Sheet, and you replace the demo rows with real items.
- Sourcing data depends on the niche. He recommends building for topics you understand.
  - Scrape Amazon or other listing sites.
  - Use Twitter advanced search with a minimum likes filter (he used 30) to find viral posts, since viral items are probably better than unknown ones.
  - He builds one scraper per source (Product Hunt, Twitter, LinkedIn, Reddit, Quora). For one directory he fetched 100 items from Product Hunt into the sheet.
- If building takes more than a day, you're going the wrong way. Finding the idea can take weeks. A first-timer can give themselves a week to a month for research and validation.

## Validation checklist before building

John's checklist:

1. Search the keyword in Google, so Google helps phrase it.
2. Check there is real traffic. No traffic means nobody cares, or you spelled the term wrong.
3. Check for high growth.
4. Check that you understand the topic, because you need to judge and curate the items.
5. Check that user-generated sites (Reddit, Quora, forums, Twitter, LinkedIn) have active discussion. Use `site:reddit.com popup tent` and repeat for each site, then check views and engagement.
6. Have a promotion plan before you build.

In the demo, the keyword had high traffic, growth, a good domain, and a subreddit with 5 million members. All the boxes were checked.

## Launching and getting backlinks

- Best launch tactic: find questions on Reddit, Quora and similar sites and say "I built a directory that helps with this".
- Nonprofit directory links rarely get hated, unlike a SaaS with sign-up and pay buttons. Directories are often the only URLs allowed on some subreddits. Be honest about your story, ideally one that comes from your own hobby or interest.
- Reddit gives strong exposure and backlinks. A Reddit post can rank on Google within a day, whereas your own domain takes months or years.
- Example: for his open source software directory he posted on a subreddit with a viral post. The next day he had tons of backlinks, as people wrote blog posts linking to it.
- Two ways to earn backlinks:
  1. List the directory on other directories (directories of directories), forums and blogs, hundreds of places.
  2. Find every article on the topic and drop the URL in the comments. These are nofollow links, but Google still follows them with lower weight.
- Make the data so good that influencers link to it. Update it every month. Most directories stop after launch.
- Pick a niche you love, since you'll update it monthly and already know where the data is.

## Why directories beat blogs and where the opportunity is

- John says there are too few directories and only about 1% exist that could. He advises against crowded niches like AI tools. Go where nobody is building, such as unsexy niches whose users (campers) aren't developers or founders.
- Programmatic SEO applied to such a niche: scrape everything, create many category pages, and beat affiliate blogs and e-commerce sites.
- He is bullish on directories and bearish on blogs. AI will dominate answers, so people will not open a blog post for something AI already summarized.
- AI will not dominate judgment. If AI says "this popup tent is best, according to the popup tent directory", people will want to open the directory and judge for themselves. He thinks directories will survive the post-AI search era.

## Monetizing directories

He has a guide with 20 ways and covers the first five:

- **Claim your listing**: email the owners of listed items and offer to update their data, pictures and contact info. Include analytics ("your listing was viewed 100 times this week"). He calls it a no-brainer because you bring them traffic.
- **Affiliate links**: convert every outbound link. It works for high-ticket items. His directory MVP Wizards (people building MVPs) has tickets of about 5K, with 20% commission, so one transaction brings about 1K. His movies directory had millions of clicks on iTunes affiliate links but earned about $100, because iTunes pays very little.
- **Sponsors**: he gets inbound sponsor requests via his social following. Without an audience, use Hunter.io or Find My Mail to find sponsor emails. Look for audience overlap. For popup tents, approach smaller makers where you can reach the founder, not the huge ones. A single sponsor can sometimes pay more than many, and may become a near co-owner.
- **Boosted listings**: charge to be sorted higher, possibly via bidding or an auction for the number one spot.
- **Featured listings**: pay to be featured at the top. On his open source directory he features Listing Bot at the top, which gets a lot of clicks. Buyers come back because you bring growth, and traffic compounds.
- Sponsor retention is possible. For his Next.js starter directory, sponsors pay on a monthly or weekly subscription "infinitely".

## Why start with a directory instead of a paid SaaS

- John says everyone should have a directory as a side project. Directories need way less talent than SaaS. About one year is enough for an amateur to succeed with at least one directory, but expect to try 10 or 20.
- Benefits: you build confidence, learn idea validation, keyword search and simple promotion.
- Promoting a SaaS gets you slapped in the face, because nobody cares and people hate it. With a directory, people say "cool, nice job".
- Never start with a traditional paid SaaS. Start with a directory, an information site or a free tool.
- A directory in a space you like gives you a ready traffic channel for a later SaaS, so the growth part is covered.
- He offers a Directory Guide (page by page with examples) and a Discord called "directory makers" for buyers of the guide.
