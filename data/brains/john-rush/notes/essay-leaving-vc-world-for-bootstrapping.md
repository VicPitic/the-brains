## Why John left VC-backed startups for bootstrapping

- John felt he was the only one focused on users and the value they get. In his view, everyone else in the VC startup world was obsessed with conferences, the next funding round and media press.
- He had moved with his family to a Turkish village, and his VCs kept pushing him to travel to their office. It felt like he was an employee and they were his bosses.
- He argues this is the reality for most founders of VC-backed startups: "You can't say no to VCs. You have to pretend their jokes are funny."
- He then found indie makers on Twitter and followed them for a while. Their approach resonated with him far more than the VC approach, which led him to bootstrap.

## MarsX: a holding company of bootstrapped products

- MarsX is a "universe of products": a holding company that aims to build a full ecosystem of tools to ideate, build, grow and monetize startups.
- The core product is Mars IDE (marsx.dev). It builds SaaS, apps and directories using nocode, lowcode, AI and full code.
- John calls that mix "perfect" because it makes the whole process ultra productive. He says it lets him ship a few products every month.
- The interview frames the goal as building 1,000 tools by 2025. John's answer on how is not in the available text.

## Acquisition context: Unicorn Platform

- John acquired the website builder Unicorn Platform for $800k. The interviewer cites this as how he came onto their radar.
- He is now building a holding company of bootstrapped startups (MarsX) rather than a single venture-funded company.
