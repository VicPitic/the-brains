## John's low-budget SEO routine for busy founders

John says SEO is not dead and drives a lot of traffic for him. His routine for a busy founder:

1. Launch the website and blog on Unicorn Platform.
2. Check keywords with Ubersuggest on the top 5 competitors, then edit the home page based on that.
3. Submit the sitemap to Google Search Console (GSC) and wait 1 month.
4. Activate listingbott.com to build up domain rating.
5. Turn on seobotai.com to produce 1 article a day.
6. Turn on indexrusher.com to index those pages.
7. After about a month, check GSC, manually pick the top-performing articles, improve them, and spend a full day on the 5 top articles.
8. Check the top GSC queries for the home page and edit the meta tags and h1/h2 to reflect them.
9. Spend 1 day a month repeating steps 7 and 8.

- He admits it is "not as good as if you pay an expert", but the total budget is below $1k and it takes you from 0 to 1.
- Once you are rich and successful, hire a pro to go from 1 to 10.

## How John did SEO for Unicorn Platform

- A year earlier he knew very little about SEO. Instead of picking one strategy, he tried nearly all at once.
- He looked at keywords the site already ranked for and optimized those pages to win more traffic.
- He looked at competitors and wrote articles for the same keywords.
- He worked on domain rating until it reached almost 80.
- Half of his pages were not indexed, so he used an indexing tool.
- He wrote many articles on non-related topics for the same audience.
- He then built 3 tools to automate the work: Index Rusher (indexing), Listingbott (backlinks) and Seobot (articles).

## Directories as a traffic channel

- John saw traffic from directories about 2 years earlier on another project (marsx), so he pushed hard to list Unicorn Platform everywhere. Most directories were not popular yet, and he got a good share of traffic as they went viral.
- He automated this with Listingbott, which keeps listing his tools on new directories as they appear.
- He owns over 20 directories, some very successful. He had a gut feeling about directories 3 years earlier, but friends said "Directories are out of hype by now.." and he dropped the idea.
- In November he launched the AllGpts directory, which went viral with millions of visits. After that he built a few new directories every month ("directory lord").
- He also sponsored a few other directories to place his banner. He likes it: there is traffic, ROI seems good, and he wants to do more of it, especially on directories visited by busy founders.

## Cross-promotion from his own product portfolio and partners

- John has 24 products. The other 23 drive traffic to Unicorn Platform through footer links, banners and occasional email recommendations. About 30% of Unicorn's traffic comes this way.
- He partnered with Senja by adding their testimonial block to Unicorn's third-party blocks. Unicorn users add Senja blocks in a few clicks, he drove traffic to Senja, and Senja posted about the integration on social media and sent some traffic back.
- Possible next steps: each company emails its user base about the other.
- Next integration was RapidForms (multi-step forms, including payment steps), launching the following week.
- He invites other products that fit to collaborate, since both sides grow their user base.

## Social, Reddit, Product Hunt and word of mouth

- **Social media:** He shared his actual steps, not just MRR numbers. Many posts went viral and helped other makers. X became a top 4 traffic source for sign-ups.
- **Reddit:** He had never used it, tried it in November, and the first attempt went viral. He mentioned Unicorn Platform in the text and had it as a link in his profile. Traffic went from 0.7 visitors a day to 7000 (his words: 1000% growth overnight). Reddit is evergreen, so traffic never really stopped.
- **Product Hunt:** Launched in July, took 2nd place with over 1000 upvotes. A win has a long aftershock because you end up in newsletters and blog articles.
- **Word of mouth:** He tracks it via social media mentions and calls it his most important metric, which together with SEO leads to product-market fit (PMF). In January the year before, viral "best landing page builders" posts never included Unicorn Platform. After the above work, 90% of mentions come organically.
- **Affiliates:** Running for 8 months. Partners bring sign-ups that convert to paying users. Easy to set up and no effort once running.
- **Paid ads:** He tried and failed. He thinks he may need to learn more, or "maybe it never works".

## Talking to users to cut churn and create referrals

- John emailed every paid user asking for feedback and started a discussion. He talked to at least 300 of them.
- It surfaced low-hanging fixes to keep users, and created a strong network effect: many new users said a friend recommended Unicorn Platform.
- He now helps users every day, for free, for at least an hour a day: feedback on whatever they ask, tips and advice. He once connected a user raising a round to an investor.

## Redesign that cut churn and the $300k ARR result

- Unicorn Platform reached $300k ARR on 2024-05-20, with 128,328 users having built sites, directories, job boards, launchpads and landing pages.
- He went through 1000+ support tickets one by one and boiled them down to 100 frequent requests. Fixing them all would have taken a year.
- After sleeping on it for a few weeks, he realized about half could be solved by changing the whole UX from page-based to canvas-based, like Notion: one canvas with a page, and everything else in popups.
- That erased 80% of the backlog tasks. The remaining 20% were then tackled (not all done yet).
- Churn dropped from double-digit to single-digit.
- He also hired a designer to create many new templates and components, and was working on an update meant to 10x Unicorn's visual power.
