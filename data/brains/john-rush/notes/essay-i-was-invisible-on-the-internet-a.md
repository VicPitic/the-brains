## Context and results behind John's audience-building playbook

- John says he was invisible on the internet a year before writing this essay (dated 2024-05-08): no followers, no views.
- By then he had 50 million views and 40k followers across all platforms combined, and had reached 140,000 users with Unicorn Platform.
- His playbook for founders building an audience comes from that experience.

## Pick one platform and learn to write on it

- He puts 100% of focus on one platform. Until you reach several thousand followers and good content quality, creating content, engaging with people and understanding the algorithm take hours daily.
- He says "Learn to write by writing." It is not about hacking the algorithm or copy-pasting memes.
- Good writing is an asset that helps win every other platform later, so the first platform is a training camp.
- He suggests Twitter as the first platform. It has the shortest feedback loop, and you can create 10 daily posts to practice.

## Measure followers, not views

- Follower count is his critical KPI. He says to focus on followers, not pure views.
- Users follow when content creates respect for the author. A random meme reposted from another platform does not make people interested in you.
- Avoid negative content. It gets views quickly, but he wants "new followers" as the measure of content quality.

## Stay on one topic and know more than the average

- He says to "hit on the same target until you crack a hole there."
- A common mistake is throwing out all sorts of topics hoping one goes viral. Even if one does, you cannot reproduce it later.
- Once you pick a primary topic, add your own takes, experiments and experience. Reposting known theory is not enough; personal practice makes the information valuable.
- This takes thousands of hours digging into the topic to become one of the top 0.1% experts. He says there is no other way, unless you want followers for short cat videos or dancing TikToks.

## Not every follower counts for B2B

- A B2B founder's goal is eventually a distribution channel over social media. People who consume short entertainment content will not help.
- Example: a friend with almost a million followers launched SaaS tools and got fewer than 10 sales, because that audience didn't care about them.
- John launched all his products via social media and got far more than 10 sales instantly, with 20x fewer followers than that friend.
- Produce content for your followers, not for everyone else.
- Learn from creators who have the audience you want. For a B2B audience you cannot learn from Mr.Beast; you may end up doing the opposite of him to win.

## Anatomy of the ideal post or article

- High effort.
- A mix of theory, practice and facts.
- Grabs attention from the start and keeps the reader to the end.
- Easy to scan instead of read.
- Has a clear audience.
- Intersects with popular topics or views.
- Relevant to your persona. Don't talk about VC money if you never raised it.
- Has a killer headline.

## What to avoid when building an audience

- Talking about things you have no proven track record in.
- Being negative.
- Memes, unless you actually invent them (he cites Dago).
- Low-effort posts. If a post takes less than an hour, it will be bad in 99.9% of cases.
- Random topics such as JS, politics or dogs.
- Talking about yourself, your habits or your opinions.
- Asking questions.
- Asking friends to add fake likes and replies, or buying fake engagement.
