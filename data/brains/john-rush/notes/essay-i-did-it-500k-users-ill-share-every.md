## Growth trajectory to 500k users on Unicorn Platform

John took Unicorn Platform from 0 to 500k users in stages:
- 4 years for 0 to 25k
- 2 years for 25k to 250k
- 6 months for 250k to 500k

He tried about 22 channels. Many failed, and he shares the failures as openly as the wins. His biggest lever in the end was the product itself: "Most users come via word of mouth, and people only recommend products they love."

## Channels that worked best

- **Directories (his own):** "One of the best channels I have." They bring traffic on autopilot. He sometimes thinks he should "quit marketing and just build directories." He published a guide on ideating, building and growing web directories at johnrush.me/directory-guide/.
- **Listings on other directories:** Another passive channel. He lists his products on new directories every month and built listingbott.com to automate it.
- **LinkedIn:** He ignored it for a while, then started about a year before this post. He calls it boring ("everyone behaves like AI bot"), but it drives really good traffic and has the best conversion of all his channels.
- **Reddit:** Huge traffic. He placed no links in his posts; people went to his profile and found the products. Reddit bangers bring more traffic than anything else, even Twitter bangers. His guess: Reddit users are on PC, Twitter users read on phones.
- **Sponsoring:** He sponsored hackathons, teachers and web directories. All three worked well and are easy to scale. He plans to do much more and is building tinyadz.com to simplify the process.
- **Internal cross-promo:** At least 10% of all users came from his other products. He says multiple side projects, even free ones like directories, are a good idea.
- **Product quality:** New features, improvements, customer support and empathy for users helped growth the most.

## SEO and social media

- **SEO:** He didn't touch it until April 2023 and calls that his biggest mistake. He now mixes AI-generated blogs (made with his own seobotai.com) and human-written articles.
- **Social media:** He hired someone and invested in building her brand, but a competitor headhunted her. He started again himself and reached over 100k followers. He thought he'd never pass 10k, but reached nearly 100 million views in the year.

## Affiliate program: price it high enough

- Implementing it takes less than a day.
- He launched at 15% commission and got no interest.
- He raised it to 33% and it picked up. His lesson: the commission has to be attractive.
- He uses Tolt (@ToltHQ) and is very happy with it.

## Paid and outbound channels: ads and cold email

- **Paid ads:** He wasted lots of cash with no results. Targeting always brought a nonrelevant audience. Ads for a product itself never really work; you need a freebie or be "genius at banners."
- **Cold email:** He hired a pro and spent money and time with no results. He applies the same logic as with ads: you need to hide the product behind freebies. He dismisses "skill issue" replies from people who can't show proof.
- **Newsletter and banner ads:** He tried Product Hunt and many others. He feels they are built for VC-backed startups that want clicks at any cost. Small and tiny newsletters worked well.

## Influencers, podcast and conferences

- **Influencer marketing:** Not bad overall, but 90% of influencers are fake and so are their followers. With a real influencer, ROI was pretty good at small scale (below $1k).
- **Own podcast:** It built brand and trust well, but cost around $40k. He wouldn't recommend it to solo founders; it works if you have a team and spare hands.
- **Conferences and speaking:** He joined many programs as a mentor and promoted Unicorn Platform. It brings users but drains energy, so he stopped.

## What didn't work

- **Incubator partnerships:** He signed deals with most famous incubators to list Unicorn Platform as a "perk." He thinks he didn't get even one paying user from it.
- **Cross-promo with partners:** He put lots of effort in, but founders aren't good at collaborating ("high on EGO"). His only successful collab is with HuntedSpace (by Dan).
- **Guest posting (HackerNoon, dev.to and similar):** It brought traffic only when the article went viral, and nothing otherwise.
- **YouTube:** Decent effort, nothing out of it. Video production is hard. He plans to try again in a few months.
- **Instagram, TikTok, Facebook:** A waste of time. Their audience doesn't overlap with B2B no-code builders.
- **Giveaways and campaigns:** They didn't work and he says they damaged the brand.
- **LTDs (lifetime deals):** The worst outcome of everything he tried. "Never doing them again."
