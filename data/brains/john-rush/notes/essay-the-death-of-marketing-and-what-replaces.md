## AI agents become the main customer, and human-targeted marketing playbooks lose their audience

John argues AI is already the biggest user of the internet. Most emails are read first by AI, most websites are crawled by AI before humans, most API calls are software-to-software, and most content is parsed and summarized by LLMs and served back as a short answer.

- He predicts that within five years the share of decisions made by AI goes from 70% to 99%. Humans set goals and AI figures out how to achieve them.
- His framing: "Your customer is now an AI agent with a task, a budget, and zero brand loyalty."
- Every classic playbook (SEO, paid ads, cold outreach, content marketing, brand awareness, funnel optimization) assumed a human in the loop. He says that person "doesn't give a sh*t anymore."
- Optimizing landing pages, content calendars and conversion rates is, in his view, optimizing for "an audience that no longer exists." Founders who see this early "will own the next decade."

## John's 2020 vs 2030 marketing spend forecast

John sketches how marketing spend shifts by 2030:

| Channel | 2020 | 2030 |
|---|---|---|
| SEO | 60% | 2% |
| Paid ads | 25% | 10% |
| Brand | 10% | 23% |
| AI placement | 0% | 65% |

He says four channels survive: government-mandated purchases, AI picking the genuinely best product, human identity purchases, and paid placements inside AI. Only two are realistic for bootstrapped founders (the best product for a niche, and a personal-brand audience).

## Channels indie founders should skip: government lists and paid AI placement

**Government-mandated purchases.** John expects governments to create approved vendor lists for medical, defense, education and infrastructure, with AI agents required to pick from certified products.
- He calls it "the oligarch channel": you need lobbyists and connections to get on the list.
- Data he cites: US federal contract spending was $755B in 2024 (source: GAO), with defense and weapons systems the largest category at 42%, then IT and cybersecurity at 18%, and drugs and biologicals at 12%. There are 12,600+ pre-approved vendors on the GSA Schedule, and you need certification to even bid.
- His advice: ignore it unless you are playing that game.

**Paid placements inside AI.** He calls this "the new Google Ads."
- He expects OpenAI, Google, Anthropic and Meta to build ad layers into their agent ecosystems, so an assistant's SaaS recommendation will be paid for, and the user won't know. He describes it as lobbying inside LLMs: training data sponsorships and preferred vendor deals with AI platforms.
- Data he cites: the AI advertising market is $8.6B in 2023, $11.2B in 2025, $36.3B in 2030 (CAGR 26.7%) and $81.6B in 2033 (source: Business Research Co.). Gartner projects AI search handling 50% of all queries by 2028.
- His view: it will be pay-to-play. Companies spending millions on Google Ads will move those budgets to AI placement, so bootstrapped founders are priced out.

## AI picks you because you are genuinely the best

John calls this the channel that matters most.

- **Today:** AI recommends products based on reviews, ratings, comparison articles and Reddit threads. The obvious move is gaming this with fake reviews on Capterra, Trustpilot and Reddit. He says it will work "for a few more years."
- **Next:** models get smarter and sign up for free trials themselves, run real evaluations, and test your product against 50 competitors for a specific use case while measuring outcomes.
- **Example:** an AI agent picking a cold outreach tool signs up for 10 of them, runs the same sequence on all 10 for a week, and picks the one that landed the most replies.
- **Short-term tactic:** be listed everywhere you can, on all launchpads, directories, listicles and ratings.
- **Endgame:** "the best product wins. Every time. For every specific use case."
- **Niche strategy:** go after long-tail niche markets to reduce competition and aim to be the best, or one of the best, among a few. Being genuinely the best at one specific job is, to him, the best marketing strategy in an AI-first world, and gaming reviews is a short-term play that dies fast.

## Human identity purchases and the "T-shirt" effect

John says some purchases are identity decisions, not product comparisons.

- People use Notion because they are "a Notion person," or Linear because they identify with its aesthetic. Others are Mac, Linux or iPhone people.
- People also follow a founder on X, admire their journey and use their product to support them. He calls this wearing the founder's "T-shirt."
- He describes it as pure human tribalism and the one channel where personal brand absolutely dominates.
- Founders who build in public, share real numbers, tell real stories, or are simply remarkable and interesting people create a "gravitational pull" that AI agents will never replicate.
- Data he cites for the creator economy: $20B in 2020, $100B in 2023, $250B+ in 2025. His conclusion: "People buy founders."

## The founder playbook for bootstrapped teams

Stripping out channels that need lobbyists or ad budgets, John leaves two moves for indie founders and small bootstrapped teams.

1. **Build the best product for a specific niche.**
   - AI agents will run real evaluations, so genuine quality beats gamed reviews.
   - Pick long-tail niches to cut competition and be the best among a few.
2. **Build an audience that buys YOU.**
   - Personal brand is "the strongest moat that's left."
   - His claim: the founder with 100K followers who shares their journey will outsell the faceless SaaS with a bigger feature set "every time."

Everything else, including traditional SEO playbooks, content calendars, conversion rate optimization and paid acquisition funnels, was built for a world where humans do the searching, and he says that world is ending soon.
