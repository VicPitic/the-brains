## No easy, proven, or risk-free path to startup success

- John says no one can offer an easy, quick, risk-free, proven way to succeed, and "Everyone who gives this promise lies."
- He states that 99% of makers fail on their 1st, 2nd and Nth try.
- He warns against being fooled by rare stories like an 18-year-old college dropout on TechCrunch. In his view those odds are "lower than a lottery."
- He says every "overnight" success took far more time than it looks.
- Example: he could start a product today and reach $30k MRR in 6 months, but he says he can't teach that to someone else. It comes from 20 years of building startups.

## Startups demand above-average skill across many areas, learned only by doing

- John says there is no magic bullet. A founder must be above average in ideas, design, UX, coding, sales, marketing, presentation, copywriting, content, customer support and more.
- His analogy is a soccer player: you can't win if you run like a snail or shoot badly.
- He argues the only way to learn these skills is by doing.
- Reading, watching YouTube, taking courses and bookmarking tweets don't help unless you apply the knowledge right away.
- His rule is to be in "action" all the time, every hour of the day.

## Focus ruthlessly and don't mix fun with learning

- John tells founders to stop wasting time. Ask whether you want to succeed with your startup or share opinions on politics that nobody cares about.
- He advises removing everything from your life except one focus: "Just a gladiator. Forget hobbies, parties & fun. Focus, Focus. Focus."
- He watches X profiles of people who seem eager to do things but whose feeds are full of nothing: nothing shipped, nothing learned, only senseless comments.
- He says not to mix fun and education. It kills your bias for action, because you start getting dopamine from consuming rather than from acting.

## Step 1-2: Pick a narrow niche and become a top 1% expert

- **Pick one topic and bet on it.** Don't spread across wide audiences. Choose a narrow niche where you have some connection to the audience, or can understand and emulate their thoughts, pains, tastes and desires.
- **Learn it very deeply and aim for the top 1%.** John says this takes reading, trying and experimenting for 1000s of hours.
- He says this time isn't wasted: "I've never yet seen any true 'expert' being poor & unsuccessful."

## Step 3-4: Educate the audience, then build small tools from the conversations

- **Educate the audience.** Share things that save them time so they don't have to spend 1000 hours learning it. Content like this earns follows.
- You can also launch newsletters and info directories out of your expertise.
- **Ideas follow expertise.** If you have a crisis of ideas now, he says you'll have too many ideas after completing these steps.
- Discuss those ideas with your audience and build little tools to demonstrate them.
- He notes that often the person doesn't even make a tool, but instead "they are the tool."

## Step 5-6: Iterate with micro MVPs manually and treat it as a marathon

- Spend months iterating with micro MVPs and doing things manually for your audience.
- Inevitably, one of the tools or services will gain more attention, and you'll clearly see where to put more time.
- Don't expect it to take weeks or months. John says to enter the game as if it were a marathon.
- If you're not prepared for a marathon, he says to go back to being employed, or "try dropshipping biz, I heard kids make millions there with no effort" (sarcastic).
