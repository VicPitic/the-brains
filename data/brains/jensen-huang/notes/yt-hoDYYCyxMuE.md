## AI as a platform shift and why it is different from earlier software

- Jensen reasons from first principles about what changed in the computing stack. He calls AI a platform shift, like the PC, the internet and mobile cloud. In each shift the stack was reinvented and new applications were built on top.
- ChatGPT is itself an application, but new applications will be built on top of ChatGPT and on top of Anthropic's Claude.
- Old software was "pre-recorded". Humans typed the algorithm or recipe, and it worked on structured information (names, addresses, account numbers) retrieved with SQL. He calls SQL "the single most important database engine the world's ever known".
- AI is the first computer that understands unstructured information (images, text, sound). It processes context in real time, reasons about the user's intent, and performs the task. Prompts are how intent is described.
- Because AI is software you teach rather than write, he says "You don't write AI, you teach AI."

## The five-layer cake of AI and the infrastructure buildout

- Jensen describes AI industrially as a five-layer cake, from the bottom up:
  1. Energy, because intelligence is generated in real time.
  2. Chips and computing infrastructure (the layer he lives in).
  3. Cloud infrastructure and services.
  4. AI models, where most people think AI is.
  5. Applications (financial services, healthcare, manufacturing), where the economic benefit ultimately happens.
- Models improved enough last year that the application layer is now taking off. He cites 2025 as one of the largest VC years ever, with most funding going to AI-native companies in healthcare, robotics, manufacturing and financial services. He gives "over a hundred billion dollars" worldwide.
- He calls this "the largest infrastructure buildout in human history", with "a few hundred billion dollars" spent so far and "trillions of dollars" still to build.
- Examples he gives of the buildout across layers:
  - TSMC announced 20 new chip plants.
  - Foxconn, Wistron and Quanta are building 30 new computer plants with Nvidia.
  - Micron has started investing $200 billion in the US. SK Hynix and Samsung are also doing very well.
- He describes three kinds of factory being built: chip factories, computer factories and AI factories.

## Three breakthroughs in AI models last year

- **Agentic AI.** Models hallucinated less and became better grounded. They can research, reason about situations they were not trained on, break problems into steps, make a plan and perform tasks.
- **Open models.** DeepSeek, the first open reasoning model, was a huge event for industries around the world. Open reasoning models let companies, researchers, educators, universities and startups build domain-specific or specialized systems.
- **Physical AI.** AI that understands nature: proteins, chemicals, fluid dynamics, particle physics and quantum physics. He treats proteins as essentially a language. His example is the Lilly partnership, where AI can understand protein and chemical structures well enough to "talk to the proteins like we talked to ChatGPT".

## Jobs: purpose versus task framework

- Jensen argues AI is creating jobs, and expects labor shortages rather than mass displacement.
- The buildout creates trade jobs: plumbers, electricians, construction and steel workers, network technicians and equipment installers. He says salaries in the US have nearly doubled, to six figures for people building chip, computer and AI factories. "You don't need to have a PhD in computer science" to make a great living.
- His test for AI's impact on a job is to separate the **purpose** of the job from its **tasks**. Watching him and the host on camera, you would think they are typists, but typing is a task, not their purpose.
- Radiology example:
  - Ten years ago it was predicted to be wiped out, because computer vision was the first superhuman AI.
  - AI now permeates radiology completely, yet the number of radiologists has gone up.
  - The purpose of the job is diagnosing disease. Faster scan reading frees time for patients and clinicians, so the hospital sees more patients, revenue rises and it hires more radiologists.
- Nursing example:
  - The US is 5 million nurses short.
  - Nurses spend half their time charting. AI charting and transcription (he names Abridge, a partner) frees them to see patients.
  - Hospitals then do better and hire more nurses.

## Advice for developing countries and individuals

- AI is infrastructure, like electricity and roads, and every country should have it. Countries can import AI, but he urges them to build their own.
- Training is "not so incredibly hard" now, and open models plus local expertise let countries build models for themselves. Language and culture are a country's fundamental natural resource, so countries should develop AI on them and make national intelligence part of their ecosystem.
- AI is "the easiest software to use in history", which is why it is the fastest adopted, nearing a billion people in two to three years. He expects it to close the technology divide.
- Everyone should learn to direct, prompt, manage, guardrail and evaluate an AI. He says these skills "are no different than leading people". In future companies will manage digital, silicon AIs as part of the workforce alongside carbon-based ones.
- Anyone can be a programmer now. If you do not know how to use AI, ask the AI how to use it. If you want a website, it asks questions about what you want and writes the code.
- He praises Claude, which Nvidia uses across the company for coding and reasoning, and says any software company should get involved. He calls ChatGPT probably the most successful consumer AI in history.

## Advice for Europe

- Nvidia works with every AI company in the world because it sits low in the infrastructure layer.
- Europe's strong industrial and manufacturing base lets it "leap past the era of software", which the US led. He says to fuse industrial capability with AI and move into physical AI and robotics, "a once in a generation opportunity".
- Europe's deep sciences can use AI to accelerate discovery.
- Europe must get serious about increasing its energy supply to invest in the infrastructure layer and have a rich AI ecosystem.
- Europe's skilled trades workforce is an advantage that the US lost over the last 20 to 30 years.

## Why he says this is not an AI bubble

- His test is GPU rental demand. Nvidia has millions of GPUs in every cloud, and renting one is "so incredibly hard".
- Spot prices for GPU rentals are going up, including for GPUs two generations old.
- Demand comes from the growing number of AI companies and from companies shifting R&D budgets. Lilly's R&D, which was once all wet labs, is moving toward AI, including a big AI supercomputer and AI lab.
- Investment is large because every layer above has to be built.
- He urges investing in energy, "land, power and shell" and trade workers.
- He argues pension funds and ordinary savers should take part in the infrastructure investment so they are not "watching it from the sidelines".
