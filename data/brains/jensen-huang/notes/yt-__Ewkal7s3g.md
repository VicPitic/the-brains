## Company purpose: helping great minds do their life's work

- Jensen opens with a story about Dr Chang at National Taiwan University (NTU). Chang built a homemade supercomputer out of Nvidia GeForce gaming cards in open PC motherboards, on metal shelves, cooled by oscillating fans. He did it after his son in Silicon Valley told him about CUDA.
- Chang told him: "Because of your work I can do my life's work in my lifetime."
- Jensen says this "perfectly capture[s] our company's purpose": to help the "Einstein and da Vinci of our time do their life's work."

## Story 1: Admitting the wrong strategy and asking for help (Sega and Riva 128)

- Nvidia was founded to create accelerated computing. Its first application was 3D graphics for PC gaming.
- It used an unconventional approach called forward texture mapping and curves, which was substantially lower cost. It won a contract to build Sega's game console. That attracted games to the platform and funded the company.
- After one year of development, Jensen realized the architecture was the wrong strategy:
  - It was technically poor.
  - Microsoft was about to announce Windows 95 Direct 3D, based on inverse texture mapping and triangles.
  - Many companies were already building 3D chips for that standard.
  - Finishing the console would have meant inferior technology, incompatibility with Windows and no way to catch up.
  - Not finishing meant running out of money. Either way, Nvidia would be out of business.
- He told Sega's CEO that the approach was wrong, that Sega should find another partner, and that Nvidia could not complete the contract. He still needed Sega to pay in full or Nvidia would fail. He was embarrassed to ask.
- The Sega CEO agreed. His understanding and generosity gave Nvidia "six months to live."
- With that time Nvidia built Riva 128. It shocked the young 3D market, put Nvidia on the map and saved the company. Demand for the chip took Jensen back to Taiwan to meet Morris Chang at TSMC, starting a partnership that has lasted 25 years.
- Lesson: "confronting our mistake and with humility asking for help saved Nvidia." He says these traits are the hardest for the brightest and most successful people.

## Story 2: Creating a new computing model with CUDA

- Nvidia announced CUDA, GPU-accelerated computing, in 2007. The aim was a programming model to boost applications from scientific computing and physics simulation to image processing.
- Jensen calls creating a new computing model "incredibly hard and rarely done in history". The CPU model had been the standard for 60 years, since the IBM System/360.
- The chicken-and-egg problem:
  - CUDA needed developers to write applications.
  - Developers needed a large installed base.
  - A large installed base needed customers buying new applications.
- Solution: use GeForce, the gaming GPU with an already large market of gamers, to build the CUDA installed base.
- The cost was very high. Profits took a huge hit for many years. Market cap hovered just above one billion dollars. Shareholders were skeptical and preferred a focus on profitability.
- Nvidia persevered because it believed the time for accelerated computing would come:
  - It created the GTC conference and promoted CUDA "tirelessly" worldwide.
  - It worked with each developer to write their algorithms and achieved large speedups.
  - Applications arrived one science domain at a time: seismic processing, CT reconstruction, molecular dynamics, particle physics, fluid dynamics and image processing.
- In 2012, AI researchers discovered CUDA. AlexNet, trained on GeForce GTX 580, started the "Big Bang of AI."
- Nvidia recognized deep learning as a whole new software approach and "turned every aspect of our company" toward it. Jensen says it "risked everything", and a decade later the AI revolution began.
- He says the journey "forged our corporate character to endure the pain and suffering that is always needed to realize a vision."

## Story 3: Strategic retreat from mobile phones to create robotics

- In 2010 Google wanted Android to become a mobile computer with excellent graphics. Nvidia's computing and graphics expertise made it a fit. It entered the mobile chip market and was instantly successful, and its business and stock price surged.
- Competition swarmed in. Modem chip makers were learning to build computing chips while Nvidia was learning to build modems. The phone market was huge and Nvidia could have fought for share.
- Jensen instead sacrificed the market. His reasoning: Nvidia's mission is to build computers that solve problems ordinary computers cannot, so it should dedicate itself to its vision and a unique contribution.
- By leaving, Nvidia "opened our minds to invent a new one." It imagined a new type of computer for robotics, with neural network processors and safety architectures. At the time this was a "zero billion dollar market."
- Today Nvidia has billions of dollars of automotive and robotics business and started a new industry.
- Principle: "strategic retreat, sacrifice, deciding what to give up is at the very core of success." Retreat does not come easily to the brightest and most successful people.

## View on AI as a new computing era (2023)

- Jensen calls AI the start of a major technology era, after PC, internet, mobile and cloud. He says it is "far more fundamental" because every computing layer has been reinvented, from how software is written to how it is processed. He calls it a rebirth of the computer industry.
- Within the next decade, he says, the industry will replace over a trillion dollars of the world's traditional computers with new accelerated AI computers. He calls this a golden opportunity for Taiwanese companies, which he describes as the foundation and bedrock of the computer industry.
- AI automates tasks in the world's multi-trillion dollar industries, including healthcare, financial services, transportation and manufacturing.
- Companies:
  - Agile companies that take advantage of AI will boost their position.
  - Companies that don't "will perish."
- Jobs:
  - AI will create new jobs such as data engineering, prompt engineering, AI factory operations and AI safety engineering.
  - It will obsolete some jobs and change every job.
  - Programmers, designers, artists, marketers and manufacturing planners will be supercharged.
- Advice: learn to work with an AI co-pilot. "Someone who is expert with AI will" take your job, not AI itself.
- He notes that every earlier era produced new industries from new entrepreneurs, and expects AI to do the same.

## Advice to graduates

- Success is a risk: "today is the most successful day of your life so far," and Jensen says he was also successful until he started Nvidia. There he met big, humiliating, nearly fatal failures, and those failures define the company.
- He argues timing is relative. He graduated in 1984, the year of the IBM PC and Apple Macintosh, and calls it a perfect year. He predicts 2023 will be as well.
- He tells graduates they are at the starting line of AI, where every industry will be "revolutionized, reborn" and ready for new ideas.
- Closing: whatever you create, "run after it like we did. Run, don't walk. Remember, either you're running for food or you are running from being food."
