## AI as a new industry: factories, electricity, data and offtake

- Jensen frames AI as a general purpose technology driving an industrial revolution that affects every person, company and country.
- He also calls it a new industry in itself, because "AI is manufacturing." It has to be generated in real time for every use and cannot be pre-computed, so it needs a new type of factory.
- NVIDIA builds the computing systems that power these AI factories.
- AI factories consume natural resources: electricity, data, and offtake.
- Offtake comes from leadership inspiring young people, students, university scientists and industries to engage the AI economy.
- He endorses the "five-layer cake" as a framework for a national AI strategy, and says Kazakhstan has the assets to build all of it.
- Advice to a resource-rich country: instead of only exporting energy, "transform the energy into intelligence and export intelligence."
- He notes a planned 1 gigawatt data center valley in Kazakhstan. With telecom interconnection, he sees it as a compute and AI factory hub for Europe.

## Sovereign AI: a country's data is its natural resource

- Jensen argues a country's data encodes its culture, history, humanity and values. That data belongs to the country, "just as your natural resources belong to you."
- He calls building national AI capability "a national security concern," not just a good idea.
- His reasoning:
  - Intelligence is foundational and general purpose, and it touches every citizen, company and industry.
  - You cannot export something as fundamental as your culture and values in order to import intelligence from someone else.
  - Countries should control their data and the delivery of intelligence to their people.
- Analogy: countries have their own electric grid, internet and telecommunications. In future they will also have their own "intelligence grid."
- Foreign companies and countries can supplement it, but it cannot be delivered completely from abroad.
- On how to build it: inventing the core technology is hard, but adapting it is "not so difficult" (though not easy). A country with strong mathematicians, scientists and programmers can take open-source models and apply them to its own language and culture, then run its own intelligence infrastructure.
- He praised Kazakhstan's Kazakh LLM as an example of turning national data into national intelligence.

## Physical AI as the next wave and the largest industry

- Jensen calls physical AI the next wave and "the largest industry of all," because most of the world involves interacting with and understanding the physical world.
- AI starts with cognitive ("soft") intelligence and extends all the way to physical AI.
- Physical AI is broader than robotics. He lists seismic understanding, mapping mines and wells, geography and geology (where and how best to mine), material science, and chemical processing.
- For a country with mining, natural resources and agriculture, every stage from extraction to turning minerals into new materials can benefit.
- He says everyone is at the beginning of this journey, which makes it a chance to help shape the industry.
- Prediction: just as there will be artificial general intelligence for cognition, there will be "artificial general robotics." He extends this to artificial general farmers, miners, scientists and engineers in every field.
- He says NVIDIA is already working with robotics companies in Kazakhstan.

## Ambition is what decides who AI lifts

- Asked about jobs, Jensen says "The only thing that matters is do you have ambition?"
- Examples of ambition plus AI:
  - A farmer uses robots to farm, then expands the operation, horizon and business.
  - A scientist's AI assistant expands the universe they can explore.
  - An engineer's AI engineer helps explore better designs.
- The same logic applies to children: if a child wants to learn more, AI helps them learn more. If the child wants to do less, AI can help them do less.
- He says it is the job of parents to instill "hope, ambition, aspiration" and help children dream bigger.
- It applies equally to adults, companies and nations.
- He says leaders, whether country leaders, CEOs or parents, should instill ambition in their people, families, companies and nations. He cites Kazakhstan's president declaring the country an AI nation as an example.

## How NVIDIA decides to invest: people, not companies

- On NVIDIA's investment in Higgsfield AI, Jensen says: "you invest in people, not companies." He calls this "the first thing and the last thing."
- Why the Higgsfield founders stood out:
  - They are visionary and had a hard idea to execute.
  - They compete globally against well-funded, formidable rivals, many in San Francisco.
  - Their team of about 600 people in Kazakhstan has an average age of 25, and many are deep tech engineers and computer scientists.
  - They bridge the culture and expertise of two nations, with offices in Kazakhstan and the United States.
- His rule: when you invest in a company, you are really investing in people, so you have to select the best people.
- He joked that he starts work at 5 a.m., and that day he started at 4 a.m.

## Building and deploying AI responsibly

- Jensen says the world can only enjoy AI's benefits if it is developed, advanced and deployed responsibly. Safety must be built in from the very beginning, as with self-driving cars and mining.
- He says creators like NVIDIA and Higgsfield carry that responsibility.
- Safety technology is foundational and has to be invented and engineered.
- NVIDIA created OpenShell. He describes AI agents as "essentially self-driving cars or robots inside a computer." If given access to files and tools, they will freely use them to do their job.
- OpenShell's approach:
  - Contain agents so access is exactly what is wanted.
  - Limit privileges to what the agent needs for its job.
  - Monitor it at all times.
- He advises everybody to use it.
- On whether his view of AI changed in the past year: no. He is more enthusiastic than a year ago. Agentic systems made AI useful, and he cites generated video going from short clips to long, temporally and physically consistent stories, which he says is not possible without agentic reasoning systems. He also names software coding, robotics and drug discovery as fields where it has become very useful.

## Advice to an 18-year-old: become the world's best programmer of AI

- Jensen says he envies being 18 today, because technology has caused a complete reset.
- His own generation was defined by the creation of the personal computer. In his 43 years of work, all of that effort led to AI, "a complete reset of everything that we've done."
- Shift: "Instead of programming the computer, you have to learn how to program AI."
- Because AI is new for everybody, he says he would "dedicate myself to be the world's best programmer of AI."
- AI can automate, partner with and assist people, but as with working with a human assistant, you have to learn how to interact and "program each other."
- He says you need both technical understanding and the human skill of interacting. The best programmer of AI understands how AI works: its fundamental principles, strengths, weaknesses, how it is developing and where it is going.
- Analogies: "The best user of a machine is someone who understands how the machine works. The best driver of a race car is someone who understands how the car works."
