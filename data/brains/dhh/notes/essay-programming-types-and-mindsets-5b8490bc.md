## Types are a matter of mindset, not correctness

- DHH says the static vs dynamic typing schism has run for his whole career, and "very few" of the million arguments from either side ever convinced anyone.
- He calls these arguments "rationalizations masquerading as reason" in matters of faith: "The rider will always justify the way of the elephant."
- Converts tend to "shout the loudest rationalizations of all": "The shorter the faith, the brighter the flame."
- Functional vs object-oriented programming falls in the same category. Poles on both axes have delivered excellent software over the decades (and awful stuff too).

## DHH's own position: dynamic typing and Ruby

- He is "unashamedly a dynamic typing kind of guy", and that is why he loves Ruby.
- Ruby takes full advantage of dynamic typing to allow the "poetic syntax that results in such beautiful code".
- Ruby with explicit, static typing would be "like a salad with a scoop of ice cream. They just don't go together."
- He does not enjoy static typing, and objects animate [his] mind's eye.
- His line in the sand: he never wants to "soil [his] Ruby with type hints" or write all front-end code in TypeScript.

## Why he stopped evangelizing

- He admits he used to hold the evangelical position for dynamic typing, to the point of a "One True Proposition affliction".
- He read lack of enthusiasm for dynamic typing as a sign of missing education, experience, or competence. He calls that "folly".
- He compares it to convincing an introvert they would love parties if they loosened up, and that shouting in crowded rooms for hours is actually fun.
- He describes himself as a "recovering solutionist" who now smiles at people incredulous that anyone could prefer JavaScript over TypeScript, because he recognizes that zeal from his own past.

## The case for multiplicity in programming

- He has come to appreciate "the magnificence of multiplicity". Programming would be an "awful endeavor" if everyone were confined to one paradigm.
- Human nature is too varied to accept such a constraint on creativity.
- His analogy: imagine all visual art in cubism or realism, or all novels in Hemingway's short, direct style. It would be a bore.
- Uniformity would "ruin the magic of programming", which he calls a unique fusion of art and engineering.

## Limits of the relativism and coexistence

- Not every programming disagreement is equal-but-different mindsets. He says "there are limits to this relativism", but static vs dynamic typing and functional vs OO sit within them.
- People can express themselves equally well from either end, but being ambidextrous is rare. The evidence is the "utter disbelief" each side expresses that the other might hold a reasonable position.
- He accepts that others "illuminate their creativity with just as much intensity" using functional constraints and explicit types.
- His coexistence deal: as long as he never has to add type hints to Ruby or write all front-end code in TypeScript, he can happily coexist with people who adore Go or can't stand JavaScript. "Vive la différence!"
