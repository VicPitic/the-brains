## DHH's reaction to the TypeScript backlash after Turbo 8 dropped it

- DHH describes the response to 37signals dropping TypeScript from Turbo (Sept 2023) as a "full-on meltdown" and "open source hooliganism".
- He separates two groups: active contributors and users of Turbo who had legitimate technical objections, whom he is happy to debate in good faith, and people with no stake in Turbo who showed up "spoiling for a fight".
- The second group carried most of the debate. They framed anyone who dropped TypeScript as incompetent or malevolent, defending the "honor" of the language.
- Behavior he cites:
  - Pull requests to add TypeScript back
  - A pull request to delete the Turbo repository
  - An issue to remove him from the project
  - Pile-ons of emojis, rockets and "LGTM"s
- He points out that 37signals founded and funded Turbo and remains its primary maintainer.

## Why he saw the backlash as unjustified

- The decision was one project, mostly overseen by one company, removing TypeScript from its own code and explaining why.
- It left ample room and spirit for others to reach different conclusions for their own work.
- He says a reaction like this might have had a sliver of justification only if the decision had been a tirade against TypeScript, a wholesale denigration of its users, or a petition for Microsoft to shut it down. It was none of those.

## Evangelist versus dogmatic crusader

- DHH smiles at people with passionate preferences. He sees it as good that people care enough about their craft to extol their favorite tools.
- He draws a "fine line" between a passionate evangelist and a dogmatic crusader.
- Crusading for technology is old: he recalls Newsgroup flamewars over the Sony PlayStation versus the Sega Saturn, fought by overzealous nerds citing "The Facts".
- He admits he used to dance that line himself in the early days of Ruby on Rails.
- Even so, he says he would have found it "absolutely inconceivable" to show up in someone's open source repository to deface its "collaboration organs".
- He calls this a new variety of open source advocacy, closer to hooliganism than to 1990s console flamewars, and "entirely unbecoming for a profession like ours".
- He expects most people who genuinely care about TypeScript to look back on it with ambivalence or shame.

## TypeScript is JavaScript, and the bikeshed

- DHH calls the animosity "a weird exhibition of self-loathing in the choice of technology".
- His description of TypeScript: "a superset that sprinkles an appearance of type safety on top of a weakly- and dynamically-typed language."
- He sees the rage toward people who use the plain JavaScript subset as an example of the ferocity and viciousness of minor differences.
- He invokes the bikeshed parable: when stakes are low, debate gets nastier.
- Whether to use compiler-based type checking is, in his words, "so ultimately unresolvable" that it invites hooliganism. It lets people drop inhibitions for a match, repeated season after season. He calls it "the exercise of the Id".

## His position on tool preference and open source culture

- He is glad if people found their calling in TypeScript, and calls himself an "unapologetic superfan of Ruby".
- His ask: find another way to express enthusiasm than defacing collaboration spaces or slandering people who "JavaScript differently" with accusations of incompetence or malice.
- He frames this as a matter of open source culture and professional conduct, not a verdict on whether static typing is right for everyone.
