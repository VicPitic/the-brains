## Programming should be enjoyable, and DHH wants to keep writing the code

- DHH argues that part of the appeal of "vibe coding" (leaning back and prompting AI through a whole project) is that many development environments are deeply unpleasant to work with.
- Programmers stuck with cumbersome languages and frameworks naturally want to give up the coding part of software development. DHH says: "If I found writing code a chore, I'd be looking for retirement too." He doesn't find it a chore.
- He used to treat coding as a necessary but inconvenient step toward getting programs. That changed when he learned Ruby and built Rails.
- His view is that programming "should be a vibe", meaning fun, and should resemble English closely enough that line noise doesn't obscure the underlying ideas and decisions.
- Code should allow a richness of expression that serves the human reader instead of favoring the strictness the computer prefers. Ruby does this.

## Ruby's premise of programmer happiness, and why it matters more with AI

- Ruby's entire premise is "programmer happiness": writing code should be a joy.
- Historically Ruby traded run-time performance, memory usage and other machine sympathies for that happiness.
- DHH says that trade-off has largely gone away: after thirty years of constant improvement, Ruby is "now incredibly fast and efficient, yet remains a delight to work with."
- He sees disgruntled programmers finally realizing that an escape is possible from "nasty syntax, boilerplate galore, and ecosystem hyper-churn". He says that ethos "couldn't shine brighter now."
- He holds that the instinct behind vibe coding is correct: "Programming _should_ be a vibe! It should be fun!"
- The wisdom Ruby was founded on, and a big part of AI's appeal to programmers, is that programming should "favor and flatter the human."

## Why AI hiding the unpleasant parts is not a fix

- DHH says the appeal of AI is that it hides away the unpleasantness of bad languages: syntax, boilerplate and framework churn.
- He compares this to "cleaning your room by stuffing the mess under the bed". The mess doesn't go away.
- The better answer is to use an environment (Ruby and Rails, in his case) where the mess isn't there to begin with, rather than papering over a painful stack with AI.

## Why he won't hand AI the keyboard

- DHH has no interest in giving up writing code. It isn't the unpleasant part he wants AI to take off his hands.
- He rejects the idea of becoming "a project manager for a murder of AI crows". He has had the option to move up the manager ladder for most of his career and has steadily refused, "because I really like writing Ruby! It's the most enjoyable part of the job!"
- He says he'd retire before permanently handing AI the keyboard to drive the code.

## How he does use AI: as a pair programmer

- DHH converses and collaborates with LLMs all day long.
- His uses: looking up APIs, clarifying concepts, and asking stupid questions.
- He calls AI "a superb pair programmer", with him still driving.

## Where he thinks AI could go

- DHH allows that wanting to write code might one day become a quaint concept, like tending to horses for transportation in the modern world: done as a hobby but devoid of economic value.
- He says he doesn't think anyone knows how far the intelligence and creativity of these "insatiable token munchers" can be pushed, and he wouldn't bet against their advance.
- Even so, his position for now is that programming should stay enjoyable for the human doing it.
