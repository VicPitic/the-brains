## Origin: Extreme Programming and the agile movement

- DHH calls himself "a programming child of the agile software movement." Kent Beck published *Extreme Programming Explained* in 2000, just as DHH was starting out.
- Having had just enough exposure to Big Upfront Design and waterfall to appreciate the shift, he saw XP as a monumental break from the traditional approach. Beck's methodology "x-rayed the ills of the traditional approach."
- What attracted him most was Beck's "irreverent insistence on practicality": plenty of vision and principles, but all of them lead to running code.
- He sees XP as one expression of a larger paradigm shift. In 2001 several of his programming heroes published the Agile Manifesto: Ward Cunningham, Martin Fowler and Dave Thomas among them.
- The manifesto points that still excite him: "individuals over processes" and "working software over comprehensive documentation."

## The two mottos that drive all his programming work

- Beck made the lofty agile ideals more practical still, with two mottos:
  - "the simplest thing that could possibly work"
  - "you aren't gonna need it" (YAGNI)
- DHH says that to this day these are "the mottos that move all my programming work forward."
- Their job is to be pithy mental antidotes. It is easy to get lost in sentiments like "but wouldn't it be nice if..." and "we'll probably also need...", and you need a counter ready.
- He admits sticking with this intent is always a struggle. Human nature seems primed to dwell on all potentialities rather than activate the instinct for action, so the mottos help.

## Pairing the mottos with cultivated ignorance

- DHH argues the mottos pair well with a "cultivated strain of ignorance" when you want to tackle existing problems from first principles.
- His examples of being an outsider to the incumbent paradigm:
  - An outsider to J2EE, which let him pursue Ruby on Rails.
  - An outsider to JavaScript, which let him chase Hotwire.
  - An outsider to Kubernetes, which let him sail with Kamal.
- His conclusion: "It's easier to break the paradigms if you're not enmeshed in them on the daily."

## Going from nothing to working software in days

- Even when you're not busting paradigms, he calls the mottos paramount.
- They are how you go from nothing to working software "in days, not weeks or months."
- The tactic is cutting straight to the chase for the epicenter of the solution (he links Basecamp's *Getting Real* chapter on epicenter design).
- He reports seeing this in action on a new product at 37signals (March 2023), marveling at how quickly the team went from concept to code.

## Why he recommends reading Beck, and the payoff

- DHH strongly encourages reading Beck's *Extreme Programming Explained* if you haven't.
- Whether XP as a complete methodology is right for you is irrelevant. "The mere audacity of the prescriptions will expand your horizon."
- The benefit is trained eyes: you learn to look for the simplest thing that could possibly work.
- His claim: "Few changes in perspective will unlock that hallowed 10x potential like this."
