## Rails 7 as "The One Person Framework"

- DHH calls Rails 7 "the version of Rails I've been longing for," the one where "all the cards are on the table" and there are "no more tricks up our sleeves." He frames it as the culmination of years of progress on five fronts at once.
- The ideal he is chasing is The One Person Framework: "a toolkit so powerful that it allows a single individual to create modern applications upon which they might build a competitive business. The way it used to be."
- He sees this release as the closest Rails has come to that ideal.
- He says it still makes him giddy: more than twenty years into web development, a big release "still makes me giddy like nothing else."

## Why the conventional path is too heavy for individuals

- Becoming an expert in all the latest tools and techniques takes a huge amount of learning.
- He calls the conventional path, "as paved by solutions extracted from giant tech companies," a journey "akin to The Oregon Trail": "You might well die of dysentery before you ever get to your destination!"
- His framing of the fight: "You simply can't play by the same rules against an opponent exponentially stronger than you."
- Rails 7 is meant as "the wormhole that folds the time-learning-shipping-continuum," letting you travel grand distances without knowing "all the physics of interstellar travel." The goal is to give "the individual rebel a fighting chance against The Empire."

## Conceptual compression as the engine

- The key mechanism behind the One Person Framework is conceptual compression.
- His analogy: a video codec that throws away irrelevant details so you can download the film in real time rather than buffer for an hour.
- He dedicated an entire RailsConf keynote to the idea.
- In practice, a developer gets powerful capabilities without having to master every underlying layer first.

## Hotwire and import maps as Rails 7 defaults

- Stimulus and Turbo, together known as Hotwire, are integrated directly as the new defaults in Rails 7.
- Import maps arrive alongside them, which means "you no longer need to run the whole JavaScript ecosystem enchilada in your Ruby app."
- Turbo's roots go back to 2012 with the first release of its predecessor, Turbolinks.
- Stimulus originated in a Christmas spike he did almost exactly five years before this essay, out of frustration with the many different ways Basecamp was doing JavaScript sprinkles at the time.
- This long-running work is "now finally ready for the big stage of being Rails defaults."

## Backend upgrades: encryption

- The backend gets "really nice upgrades," especially the encryption work done for HEY.
- This lets data be encrypted while it is live in the database. 37signals calls it "at-work encryption" on the HEY security page.

## Swimming against industry specialization

- He acknowledges uncertainty: "Will it succeed? Who knows."
- The headwind is "so many vested interests in the subdivision of expertise." Specialisms are getting ever narrower, and the One Person Framework deliberately goes in the opposite direction.
- Still, he argues that if there was ever an opening to "at least tilt the direction of the industry, now is it."
- His mood: "What a glorious time to be working in web development."
