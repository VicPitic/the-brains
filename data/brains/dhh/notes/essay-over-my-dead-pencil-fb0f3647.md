## Rails World 2026 message: put down the pencils

- At Rails World (two weeks before October 6, 2026), DHH told programmers it is "time to put down the pencils."
- His claim: the vast majority of code will no longer be written by hand. Coding agents are now good enough that having humans type out lines of Ruby, Rust, or C++ is "not an economically viable enterprise going forward."
- He polled the room on who still writes a material amount of code by hand on a weekly basis. Only a handful raised their hands.
- His read on the reaction: the room already knew this, but many were still shocked to hear it said out loud.

## AI agents as new coworkers, not just another tool

- DHH argues AI "isn't just another tool." He compares it to "gaining some new coworkers" who are exceptionally good and fast at a wide range of tasks.
- These coworkers still need help with some things, and you may occasionally disagree with their choices.
- He calls them "clanker colleagues" and says it is fine to be skeptical about the areas where they still get things wrong.
- Treating agents as collaborators, not as an instrument, is the framing he recommends for working with them.

## Why programmers feel threatened

- DHH acknowledges the shock: programming has barely been a professional career for a generation, and it is being completely transformed.
- He says that is unsettling even for people who have watched technologies come and go.
- The threat is partly about status. Programmers were treated as "precious priests" whose incantations were needed to get even the lowest-hanging fruit out of a computer, and that was barely "five minutes ago."
- The perceived risk of disintermediation, with agents appearing as competition, is hitting some programmers hard.

## The optimistic case: a bloom in software development

- DHH does not think anyone willing to lean into the future needs to worry much. The condition is to make the most of the "intelligence explosion" and apply existing skills to what comes next.
- He predicts "an absolute bloom in software development" as the price of development plummets.
- His reasoning: everyone will realize how much automation is still left to do in the world, so demand for software grows as the cost falls.
- Closing line: "Don't go down with the pencils. There's so much to build. We need you."

## Who should actually worry

- DHH says the only programmers who need to worry are those who refuse to embrace the progress of the age of agents, or who drag their feet on it.
- Skepticism about where agents still get things wrong is acceptable to him.
- Refusing collaboration with agents is, in his words, "not a viable career path for almost anyone."
