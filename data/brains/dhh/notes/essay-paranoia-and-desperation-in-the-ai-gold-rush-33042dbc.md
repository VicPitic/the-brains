## DHH on the AI gold rush: fear-driven desperation and privacy transgressions

- In May 2024, DHH wrote that he had "never seen so much paranoia in technology about missing out on The Next Big Thing as with AI."
- He says companies seem "less excited about the prospects than they are petrified that its going to kill them."
- He leaves open whether that fear is justified ("maybe it's not"), but calls the desperation it produces "incontestable."
- His case in point is Slack, which Salesforce owns.

## Slack's default opt-in training on private messages

- Salesforce announced it would train Slack AI models on customers' private messages, files and other content.
- Training is on by default. Opting out required sending a specially formatted email to feedback@slack.com.
- DHH mocks the "feedback" address as the kind of process that invites a quip about "knuckle-sandwich feedback to their face."
- His reading of the motive: executives in a brainstorm decided that the way to catch up to big players like OpenAI is to ignore privacy concerns altogether.
- The logic he lampoons: if you can't beat OpenAI at scanning the sum of public human knowledge, scan the confidential conversations instead (new product strategies, unannounced layoffs, private 2025 financial projections).

## The autocomplete-for-layoffs critique

- DHH imagines a CEO typing an announcement of a 30% layoff and Slack autocompleting it with "the most anodyne distillation from five competitors doing the same."
- The CEO would only edit out a competitor's name (his example is Asana) and save "at least 8 minutes typing out the corporate slop yourself."
- He treats this as the product vision such training buys: bland, averaged corporate text built from other companies' confidential communications.
- Whether that vision looks bright or dystopian, he says, depends on how well one's "inner compass is tuned to the kind of AI KPIs pushing product managers in charge of acquired chat products at large tech companies."
- The point implied: AI metrics driving acquired-product PMs, not user trust, are what produce decisions like this.

## Prediction: more trust and privacy violations in the race

- DHH's broader point is that this reflects "the broad paranoia and desperation in the AI gold rush."
- He expects "more such flagrant transgressions of trust and privacy" because things are moving fast.
- The trigger he describes is low: any "sliver of a chance" that a data grab gives an edge in building a better chatbot.
- He closes with "Buckle up!"
