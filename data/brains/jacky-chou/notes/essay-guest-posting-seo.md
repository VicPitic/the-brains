## What guest posting is and what it does for SEO

- Jacky defines guest posting (guest blogging) as writing and publishing articles for other sites, usually under your byline as a "guest author" or "contributor".
- Payment varies by publication. Some pay, and others give non-monetary rewards such as a link to your site or social accounts.
- He lists these SEO and growth benefits:
  - More organic traffic, because guest posts lift domain authority and rankings.
  - More brand visibility, from exposure to a larger audience on popular industry sites.
  - More backlinks, via the author bio or links inside the post body. He calls backlinks a crucial factor in search ranking.
  - Stronger topical authority, when posts on topics related to your site link back to relevant pages. Google evaluates a site's relevance to specific keywords to decide how high it ranks.
  - Higher-quality traffic, because people who click through from a guest post are more interested in what you offer and more likely to convert.
  - Relationships with other thought leaders, plus a promotional boost when the host shares the post with their network.

## Setting goals and finding guest post opportunities

- Step 1: set the goal before you pitch. Goals include leads or sales, backlinks, visitors, and brand awareness.
- Not every guest post has to be about backlinks. One article can build awareness and sales and also strengthen your backlink profile.
- Step 2: find opportunities in four ways:
  - Contact influencers to see if they'd want your guest post.
  - Look for blog posts relevant to your industry.
  - Search online for sites that accept guest posts.
  - Check competitors' backlinks and try to secure guest posts on the same sites.
- He suggests Google search operators built on phrases like "accepting guest posts", "guest post", "guest post guidelines", "guest article" and "guest author".

## Qualifying guest posting sites

Jacky says to vet a site before pitching it. His checks:
- High traffic.
- Good domain authority.
- Well-written, informative content.
- A site built for more than accepting guest posts. If every published post carries outbound links to random websites, it was most likely designed to sell links, and he recommends avoiding it.

He also warns against writing for unrelated websites. Their readers won't click through to you and the post won't help your rankings.

## Pitching and writing the guest post

**What a pitch should include:**
- The type of article you want to write.
- A quick overview of the proposed article.
- Why you want to blog for that site.
- Why the article fits the site.
- How the site owner and readers benefit.
- How you plan to promote the piece if it's accepted.

A pitch that is well-crafted and customized to the site is more likely to be accepted.

**Writing rules:**
- One link to your domain in the author bio.
- A few contextual links to your site, thematically relevant to the pages they point to.
- The right anchor text, with no keyword stuffing.
- Fit the site, and use well-researched, high-quality content. He says the article reflects your brand and you want it to rank for its keywords.

## Promoting, measuring and iterating

- Build backlinks to the guest post itself. This raises the post's PageRank and so the link equity it passes to your site through contextual links.
- Track results in Google Analytics:
  - Sales or leads generated from the guest post.
  - Visitors who reach your site from the article.
  - Changes in page authority and domain authority.
  - Changes in search rankings for the linked pages.
- Use the data to adjust future guest posts. Two warning signs he names: the post drives lots of visits to the host's site but little direct traffic to yours, or the lead quality differs from what you expected.
- Each new post should move you closer to your goals.

## Guest posting practices that risk Google penalties

Jacky notes that guest blogging has been heavily manipulated and some practices have become spammy. He flags these as penalty risks:
- Posting repeatedly on the same few sites. Leaning too much on them can look spammy.
- Posting on low-authority sites, such as the flood of low-quality "news" sites that have poor editorial standards and sometimes accept weak posts for payment.
- Putting a half-dozen backlinks in the post body.
- Over-optimizing keywords in anchor text.
- Spinning content: slightly rewriting an already published piece to place it elsewhere for more backlinks.
- Writing weak content with little value, made only to get a link.
- Writing on a subject you don't know well enough to deliver value to the site's audience.

## Is guest posting black hat? Paid links and hosting guest posts

- He says guest posting isn't black hat in itself, but newcomers can repeat black hat habits.
- Google doesn't support paid guest writing. He says you'll be penalized if Google finds that a guest blogger was compensated for your backlink.
- Links from gambling sites and other questionable sources can hurt SEO performance. He recommends learning Google's guidelines first.
- A backlink is a link from one site to another. A guest post is a post you write for someone else's site. Backlinks matter because they signal to Google that your site is valuable to others.
- He considers guest posting worth the effort because it reaches an established pool of readers who may visit your site for more.
- Accepting guest posts on your own site is, for him, a way to expand the site with little effort. Guest authors share and promote the content, which brings free promotion, social shares and referrals.
