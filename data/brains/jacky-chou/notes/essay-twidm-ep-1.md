## AI content for SEO: bulk-publish, then rewrite what ranks

- Jacky uses AI for content creation and runs a couple of pure AI-automated sites that publish "people also ask" content.
- His reasoning: hiring writers gets very pricey at scale, and AI lets him test keywords at a scale that wasn't possible before.
- He uses a custom-built service that auto-posts to WordPress. He can publish thousands of blog posts a day and push them through indexing services.
- One of these sites was at about 1,500 views a day at the time. He calls the results "pretty surprising" and says he is "seeing some crazy success."
- He declined to name the service ("I'm gonna gate keep this").
- His observation on detection: AI sites that get publicly identified on Twitter get destroyed immediately. Sites that stay quiet and aren't doing huge numbers yet probably won't be found. So far Google isn't catching up, even though mainstream marketing outlets keep saying it will.

## AI content workflow: post in bulk, rank, then rewrite with humans

- Current process: bulk-post AI content, see what ranks, then have a human writer rewrite it, then optimize it.
- AI content is not great for buyer-intent content right now, so he doesn't rely on it there.
- Its strength is building topical authority very quickly.
- Example: write a thousand posts on how to cut various things, then target "best chef's knives for cutting." That is much easier to rank than starting from scratch.
- He notes a full affiliate site made only of "best XYZ knives" pages wouldn't work anymore. The informational cluster is what carries it.

## Performance plateau: why paid direct-response stops working

- Jacky describes the performance plateau as mainly relevant to large DTC and consumer brands, not niche site builders.
- Running paid direct-response ads for too long eventually stops converting. He cites Airbnb as saying it hit this plateau.
- The fix is branding campaigns. You saturate the people reachable on Facebook and Instagram, so you need to reach people who aren't on those platforms as often.
- Example he gives: Coca-Cola's expensive product placements in movies and on Netflix exist to extend brand reach once performance ads are saturated.

## Creator-led brands

- He calls creator-led companies a popular topic right now. The biggest example he cites is MrBeast Burgers.
- The playbook: grow an audience first, then launch products easily because the audience is large.
- His view: building a brand around your own identity tends to make customers a lot more loyal.
- He expects a seismic shift in this space over the next couple of years.

## Why big tech is laying off, and what display ad RPMs signal

- He cites Meta laying off 11,000 people, Twitter cutting about 50%, and Stripe layoffs, and says he periodically checks layoffs.fyi.
- His plain explanation of the cause: during Covid, online shopping and social media use spiked, and companies built projections off that usage. After Covid usage dropped, which they didn't anticipate. Amazon, Facebook and Stripe had to re-run projections, and the numbers looked bad, so they cut headcount to avoid running out of money in two years.
- A leading indicator he watches: display ad RPMs (he's seen it via Mediavine-style display ads) are much lower than a year earlier. His read is that advertisers are seeing less money come in, so they spend less on ads, so publisher payouts fall. He says the slowdown in consumer spending is "pretty alarming."
- Lesson he endorses: diversify. He says diversification is "nonexistent" in the niche site community, where many people rely on a single income source like display ads.
- He also notes Stripe's valuation was still around 40 or 50x annual revenue, which he calls absurd but plausible for a software business.

## Twitter, Meta and short-form video

- On Twitter: he said he would invest as of then ("100%"). He calls Elon Musk probably the number one executor and operator among tech company leaders, with Mark Zuckerberg not far under.
- On Zuckerberg: he says people don't give him enough credit for keeping Meta relevant for years by copying competitors, for example Snapchat. He calls VR a Hail Mary and says "I wouldn't bet against Zuck right now, but I'm not betting for him." His stance: "I'd rather be late than wrong."
- Platform shifts he sees: kids aren't using Instagram, and Facebook skews older. He cites a poll saying 35% of Gen Z have TikTok, then Snapchat, then Instagram. He's more bullish on Snapchat.
- TikTok experiment: he ran a challenge posting two short-form videos a day and spent an hour a day on the app to learn what works. His advice is to use the app for about an hour so the algorithm learns what you want.

## FTX collapse and holding crypto

- His summary of the collapse: a rumor spread that FTX was insolvent while crypto was falling. Binance's CEO (CZ) announced Binance would sell its FTX stake, worth about 2 to 3 billion, held in BUSD and FTX's own token, FTT. Selling on the open market (not over the counter) drove the price down. That created a domino effect, and FTX halted withdrawals. He says several friends lost millions.
- He notes FTX US and Binance US are separate entities from the international exchanges because of US regulation, and reports suggested FTX US users could withdraw.
- He cited Sam Bankman-Fried's net worth falling about 95%, from roughly 16 billion to 1 billion.
- Lesson he endorses: "not your keys, not your crypto," meaning trade on exchanges but move holdings off. He admitted he still held a significant amount on Binance and trusted it.
