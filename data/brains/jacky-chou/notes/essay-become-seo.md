## How Jacky frames what an SEO specialist does

- SEO is fine-tuning a website to rank higher in search results and attract more targeted visitors.
- He cites that over 93% of internet activities begin with a search engine, and that 95% of search traffic comes from the first page.
- He says a good SEO doesn't just stuff keywords into content. They pick keywords that reach the intended audience while staying consistent with the searcher's intended query.
- He argues Google updates prioritize content that meets users' needs, so user experience matters.
- Core job responsibilities he lists:
  - **Keyword research:** classify user intent as informational, navigational, transactional or commercial, then use it to shape strategy or improve existing pages.
  - **Content strategy:** a long-term plan combining topics that satisfy intent with keyword research, including how content is distributed, promoted and measured, then repeated and improved based on results.
  - **Pay-per-click:** often managed alongside SEO. It does nothing for organic rankings, but the SEO tracks which terms convert and feeds that into the SEO plan.
  - **Website improvement:** reporting on campaign results, competitor sites and new SEO developments, with recommendations tied to company goals and resources.
  - **Performance review:** never create content or make changes without monitoring results.

## How search engines work (the foundation)

- Search engines send "spiders" that locate pages and crawl their source code.
- Crawled pages are added to the index, a cached collection of pages. Google results come from this index, not real-time, which is why results return in milliseconds.
- Google's algorithm weighs hundreds of factors to return relevant results.
- Jacky says this understanding is a fundamental skill for any SEO.

## The three ranking factors to get right first

Jacky warns that many people waste time perfecting minor factors. His advice: "Ensure you control significant elements before worrying about page speed and title tags."

- **Relevance:** the page must match what the searcher expects, not just the topic. Example: someone searching "how to make peanut butter" wants instructions, not products, which is why page one has blog content and no shopping results. Optimizing for search intent means answering the "why" behind the search.
- **Authority:** Google treats links as votes of confidence. The number of quality backlinks correlates with rankings. The best links come from sites with authority in their field and relevance to the linked content. His answer to low authority is mostly "building more links."
- **Value:** matching intent isn't enough. The content must deserve the top spot. Study the leading pages to learn from their successes and mistakes.

## The four SEO categories

He compares SEO to a jigsaw puzzle where practitioners need to understand how the pieces interact.

1. **On-page SEO:** optimizing pages for rankings and organic traffic. Examples: keyword modifiers in title tags, engaging meta titles and descriptions, descriptive image alt tags, optimizing for search intent.
2. **Link building:** accumulating inbound links from other sites. They raise rankings and organic traffic and can also bring referral traffic.
3. **Technical SEO:** making the site good for users and easy for search engines to crawl and index. Examples: fix broken internal and external links, improve page performance, use canonical tags to avoid duplicate content, add hreflang tags for multilingual content, tune robots.txt for crawl efficiency, avoid thin indexable content.
4. **Keyword research:** finding the terms people search on Google. His rule of thumb is to target keywords with traffic potential (they drive traffic if you rank), business value (they can bring in clients) and low competitiveness. The best keyword strategy combines high search volume, click-through rate, business value and minimal ranking difficulty.

## Learn by doing: start your own website

- His analogy: reading about golf helps, but you only win tournaments by picking up clubs and practicing. Theory matters, but "nothing beats getting your hands dirty."
- Steps:
  1. Pick a subject you're passionate about.
  2. Get hosting and a domain.
  3. Build a simple website.
  4. Create content.
  5. Apply your SEO knowledge: optimize content, build a few links, and make the site technically sound.
- The more you repeat this, the more SEO you learn.
- Writing content takes the longest of all the tasks. Outsourcing it is fine if you have the resources, but give writers specific guidelines so the output is optimized for search.

## Specialize and build proof

- **Be T-shaped:** SEO is too broad to master everything. Link building and technical SEO have so little overlap that improving one doesn't improve the other. A T-shaped SEO is strong in one area with broad knowledge of the rest.
- You can still make a living offering SEO to all kinds of companies, but he says you'll have more success specializing. Choose a niche that can at least break even and still holds your interest.
- **Courses:** certification isn't required for most SEO roles, but fundamentals (how search engines work, HTML, key ranking factors) help, and they prepare you for advanced work in keywords, content marketing, link building and technical optimization. Free or low-cost options he lists: UC Davis SEO Specialization (Coursera), HubSpot's SEO Certification, and Google's Fundamentals of Digital Marketing Certification.
- **Portfolio:** compile your work, including course projects, into a portfolio that accurately reflects your abilities. Show the results of the SEO tactics you tried and include stats and numbers so hiring managers can see where you excelled.

## Why learn SEO yourself

Benefits he lists:

- **Traffic and rankings:** you can use increased rankings and search traffic to make money blogging or build an online business.
- **Less agency reliance:** in-house SEO expertise lets you grow without outside help.
- **Cost savings:** bloggers and solo operators save significant money by doing SEO themselves.
- **Better hiring:** knowing SEO lets you judge what's in an agency's packages and whether their recommendations will genuinely help rankings.
- **Not optional:** sites not built with SEO in mind struggle to survive in a competitive internet. He calls SEO and search engines "the best investment you can always make" for your business and personal skills, since both are "here to stay for years."
