## How Jacky bought and flipped a towing directory for $190K

- Jacky bought one of the top-ranking towing directories in the US when he was flipping a lot of niche sites.
- The seller (from Lithuania) was making only about $200-$300 despite heavy traffic. Jacky says he couldn't close clients himself, partly because his accent put off towing operators who hung up on him.
- Deal: the seller kept a small minority stake (Jacky says he doesn't remember whether it was 15% or 30%). Jacky bought the rest for $10K.
- He sold it for $190K (he shows an escrow screenshot). He did not own 100%, because of the seller's retained stake.
- His thesis: lots of traffic, poor monetization, so he could out-monetize the previous owner.

## The playbook: hire a closer, sell one slot per city

- Step one was to "immediately hire a cracked sales guy" (a closer based in Utah) to work the existing inbound leads the seller couldn't close.
- Inbound leads and calls came from the site's traffic. Cold leads were generated on top of that with Facebook ads.
- Once a client closes, they get the number one slot on the directory for their city. Jacky's line: "number one in the country".
- Only one client per city, so exclusivity is the selling point.
- Calls are delivered with call forwarding: once a client signs, all calls are forwarded to them.
- The most he charged for one city was $2.5K per month, justified by the site's traffic.
- The business also inherited some Google Business Profiles (GMBs), which he calls the main sticking point of the asset. They were still live when he recorded this.
- He calls this a rank-and-rent / directory play that anyone can run, even for a single location.

## The Facebook lead ad campaign

- One single campaign, objective called "Meta Leads" (Facebook lead ads with an instant form), at about $25 CAD per day. He describes setup as easy.
- Offer in the ad: "guaranteed 50 calls per month", positioned as "the largest towing directory in America".
- He says 50 calls per month is easy in towing. If a client didn't hit 50, they refunded the difference.
- Terms and conditions sat behind an asterisk on the ad.
- The form's description promised a free trial. Fields collected: email, full name, phone number and city, plus a privacy policy link.
- Sales flow: after a lead signs, they get a free trial, meaning leads are forwarded to them. If they close, great. If not, the lead moves on to the next person.
- He says the same Meta Leads approach could be applied to other local niches (he mentions plumbing as an example), with a closer on the other end, as a case study.

## Local Rank pricing change (his SEO software)

- Jacky announced a plan change for Local Rank (local rank.so): the Pro plan moved from unlimited locations to 10 locations.
- Reason: unlimited citation locations were "too OP", users were abusing it on the back end, and throttling everyone's speed was needed. Limiting locations would make everything faster.
- Pricing: the Pro plan at $297 with unlimited locations, rank tracker and lead credits, plus 50% off Local Boost, was available only on the day of the announcement. Unlimited citation locations afterwards cost $447.
- This was a time-limited grandfathering offer to drive same-day sign-ups.
