## What link buying is and the forms it takes

Jacky defines backlinking as getting other websites to link back to your site. It can be black hat, white hat, or somewhere in between, depending on how it is done, who does it and why.

Forms of buying backlinks he lists:
- Guest posts. He recommends Rhino Rank, Outreach Pete and FatJoe.
- Sponsored posts.
- Niche edits (link placement in existing content). He recommends Indexsy, which he says ranks #1 on Google for "niche edits".
- Product reviews.
- Paying for a link to be published on another site.

## Why brands buy backlinks

Jacky gives three reasons: save time, get faster and better results, and keep up with competitors.

**Saves time.** Doing link building yourself through outreach is a long chain, even in the best case:
- Create high-quality content optimized for search.
- Prospect for opportunities and vet whether you qualify.
- Build rapport with each prospect and request the link.
- Wait for responses and customize content per site.
- Test different outreach approaches.
- Create content for approved sites, hiring a freelance writer if you have no in-house writers.
- Monitor and repeat.

If SEO, content and outreach are not your core business, this pulls you away from your primary work. He recommends outsourcing it to a provider devoted to link building.

**Faster, better results.** Outreach companies already have relationships with reputable bloggers in almost every niche. They have tested link building strategies and know which work for which industry. You then focus on content ideas, creation and optimization, and combined effort gets results faster.

**Competition.** If competitors buy high-quality links to rank, you may need to do the same to level the playing field. If you avoid it, they will be "miles ahead", rank better and grow their customer base. If your industry demands buying links, keep up with the trend.

## Is buying backlinks safe?

Jacky's position is nuanced:
- Buying links is safe when the provider follows a white hat approach and industry best practices. A skilled provider will protect your safety and privacy.
- He also states plainly that buying links is black hat SEO because it goes against Google's TOS. Because the line is thin and SEO is increasingly competitive, he still urges getting links only from reputable brands.
- Avoid low-quality, cheap, irrelevant and non-niche-specific links. Links from PBNs (private blog networks) hurt your profile, are easily detected by Google and can jeopardize your site.
- Trust only providers with proven track records and integrity who help you gain an edge without compromising your site.

## How to vet a link seller

Jacky's checklist for a link selling site or vendor:

1. **Clear information on link types.** A good vendor explains the types of links in layman's language, explains how to stay within Google's TOS, and has a support team to answer doubts. If all you see is sales hype about being number one in search results, be careful. You may be duped and they may not follow best practices.
2. **Quality backlinks.** Check the vendor's own site: DR, DA and Google rankings. Check whether links on their sites look natural or forced. Read their policies and terms. A good brand talks about quality links rather than spam links and offers a guarantee.
3. **Long-term safety.** Black hat tactics may lift rankings in the short term, but reputable vendors do not use them. They emphasize practices that raise rankings steadily and keep your site safe over time.
4. **White label.** Prefer white label vendors, whose links and reports are unbranded and customizable. This matters for long-term well-being and for transferring or reselling.
5. **Affordability.** Compare prices and commit only to what you can afford. Do-follow and no-follow links do not cost the same, and paid links on high-authority sites cost more but can lift organic traffic and rankings. He adds that backlinks are not the only determinant of ranking.

## Where links come from and what you are actually buying

Sellers of paid links include:
- Manual outreach
- PBNs disguising themselves as real sites
- Brokers
- Webmasters and contributors

What they sell:
- Guest blogging opportunities
- Features or interviews
- Contextual links on webpages
- Backlinks to existing content on the site
- Community posts
- PBNs

## What drives backlink pricing

Jacky says there is no simple answer on cost. Six factors drive it:

1. **Link quality.** Older, high-domain-authority sites charge more and limit placements per month, often using higher prices to limit applicants. If you are a beginner without a big budget, they are not the place to pitch.
2. **Content.** Sites with excellent, optimized content have no trouble finding link partners, are picky, and their partners can be pricey. If your site is new, use SEO tools to measure it and hire excellent writers, which brings you closer to guest-post opportunities.
3. **Industry.** Niches where money flows fast cost more. Finance links are pricier than gardening links.
4. **Creative control.** Specifying rules, such as an exact anchor text, adds work to placement, so you pay more.
5. **Brand strength.** A renowned blog with many social shares and comments gets its outreach emails answered sooner and gets more offers. A new, unknown site waits longer and pays dearly for placements on high-quality domains.
6. **In-house costs.** If you skip freelance writers, staff time goes into content and outreach. Factor employee time into the true cost of links.

## Do backlinks still work, and how to make them work

Jacky says backlinks still work, citing Moz, whether paid or natural. Quality is what matters. A backlink is a vote of confidence signaling authority in your industry, and unnatural links can bring a Google penalty that hurts rankings significantly.

His rules for making links work:
- Get high-quality links from high-authority sites in your niche.
- Pair links with high-quality content optimized for search engines and your audience.
- Use links naturally, with informative anchor text.
- Check the authenticity and quality of vendors before buying.
- Understand the link types and which ones are likely to improve your profile and organic traffic.
- Watch trends in link building services and follow them.
