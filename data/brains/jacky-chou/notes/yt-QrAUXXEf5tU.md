## Cloudflare as the default stack for local business sites
- Jacky says Cloudflare is "non-negotiable" for local clients in 2026. He called Squarespace "freaking nasty".
- Local businesses don't need a blog. "Just Cloudflare and chill."
- The site is just served HTML, so it's fast, never goes down, has no bloat and is easy to optimize. The owner can still have code written for their own additions later.
- Migrating an old site to Cloudflare Pages is roughly a one-shot job for Claude, about 10 minutes.
- Example: a Newark, NJ vet clinic on Squarespace was already ranking #1 organically. It had a partial-match domain and the keyword plus location in the title. It was weak on Google Maps.

## Local SEO is mostly a set of "why not" on-page basics
- Jacky argues local SEO is easy because most competitors do almost nothing. Doing the bare minimum should make a site "fly" in a low-competition niche.
- Schema (including on the Google Map embed) is "not part of the 80/20". It's a "just do it" easy win, not a big mover.
- Adding a landmark or neighborhood in the first paragraph is a cheap tip: "I just do it cuz it's so easy." With Claude or Codex you tell it to do it, so there's no reason to skip it. He doesn't claim data proves it helps.
- He endorses a page template used for sub-service and service-area pages. This is the framework Tristan walked through:
  - Front-load the target keyword in the title tag and H1.
  - Intro copy says who the business is, with a landmark or neighborhood.
  - A big contact form above the fold.
  - A process section for unique content.
  - Sideways internal links to the same service in nearby cities.
  - Links to other services in the same city, with the pillar page first.
  - A "why choose us" section with team and real reviews.
  - A Google Map embed.

## Site structure: pillar pages, sub-services and service-area pages
- Jacky calls the structure "fantastic" and says it works for any industry, from low-competition vets to roofers and locksmiths.
- Core service pillars (for the vet: veterinarian services, animal hospital services, veterinary pharmacy) target the seed keywords.
- Sub-service pages hang off each pillar to capture long-tail searches, such as sick pet visits, wellness exams, puppy and kitten care, and flea, tick and heartworm meds.
- Each core page gets a service-area page for every nearby town. This expands the radius beyond what the Google Business Profile can capture.
- Every sub-service page links back up to its pillar page. This signals the hierarchy to Google.
- Expect 100-150+ new pages. Unique content on each matters so they don't look boilerplate.
- Branded content like process, team and reviews is the biggest asset because nobody can copy it.
- Watch for cannibalization. A new pillar page targeting the same keyword as the homepage could compete with it, so check the data first.

## CRO fixes on local service sites
- Jacky audited the vet site on desktop and mobile and found it painful on both. Mobile was worse.
- A call to action must sit above the fold on mobile on every page. Book appointment should always be visible, so use a sticky book button.
- If a site like this ran paid ads, its conversion rate would be terrible.
- He prefers card-style internal links over in-content text links. He argues "ain't nobody clicking those" text links, and clicks and behavior signals from Chrome matter. "If your links aren't clicked, might as well not have a link."
- Poor click data matters less in a low-competition space with a partial-match domain.

## Citations, browser blast and the single-variable test
- On organic, the vet's higher-value keywords were being outranked by Yelp and S6. Jacky thinks they can be beaten.
- His hunch is that "browser blast" (behavior signals) alone could outrank them, since those pages are already well optimized and likely just need more behavior signals.
- Local rankings will take more work. The plan was to build citations and run a browser blast, then re-check rankings a week later.
- If the owner is slow shipping on-site changes, that makes a clean single-variable case study. Citations alone go first, before any on-site changes.
- He dismissed a 40-citations-for-$20 purchase as "NGMI". With Local Rank he says one click gets 1,000 citations.

## Reviews and directory-site rankings
- Ranking on directory sites like booking.com comes down to revenue and reviews. Jacky says it's "100% just revenue based and review based".
- The vet's local Maps competitor had about 700 reviews. A separate video was planned for the review game plan.

## Positioning a local SEO agency
- Jacky says to carve out one industry and go laser-focused, such as being "the roofing marketing girl in your city". Don't be a general marketing consultant ("NGMI").
- He suggests starting where you already have contacts.
- A niche lets you build systems that get easier as you scale.
- Hyper-focusing also lets you buy an exact or partial match domain. "SEO agency of Houston probably prints cash."
- When starting out, take any lead you can get. A proper brand helps, but stay dialed into one practice.
- Fast results come from people who are "dialed" and "super hungry". You can tell early who will make it quickly.
