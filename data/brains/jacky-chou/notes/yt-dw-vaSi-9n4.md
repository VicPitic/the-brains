## Local SEO basics: the three ranking levers for the map pack

Jacky defines local SEO as ranking in the Google "map pack", the block between the ads and the organic results. Most people click there because the business is near them. Typical queries are "realtor near me", "dentist near me" and "gyms Vancouver". He treats it as any query where the searcher intends to drive or walk somewhere.

His 80/20 for ranking #1 in a city:
- **Google reviews.** Get a lot of them.
- **Citations.** Brand mentions listing name, address and phone number (NAP). Consistency of NAP across the internet is a local ranking factor.
- **Click-through rate.** The number of people visiting and driving to the Google Business Profile.

He says that as of August 1, 2025 local SEO is "stupid easy" and "super abusable". Some listings rank in seven days, which he says is impossible in normal organic search. Google can only draw on a limited set of signals because it is essentially just Google Maps. Hypothetically, if he started a plumbing business, he would hand out fives on the busiest street and expect to rank within 30 days.

## Gray-hat tactics for reviews, citations and CTR

Jacky is open that these are gray hat. He says they work, and he does not know how long they will keep working.

**Reviews**
- If he were 18 and broke, he would stand on a street corner or a university campus and pay people $5 for a five-star review, writing the review for them.
- Different phones and local IPs make the reviews hard for Google to trace. He includes target keywords in the review text.

**Citations**
- One-click citation services exist now. With zero budget, he would hand-register about 50 directory listings himself, or buy them on Fiverr.
- To check that citations are live, search the exact-match business name in quotes on Google. It is a quick check, but imperfect for exact-match domains.
- Citations tell Google the business is real. They can also help a new Google Business Profile stick if it has a missing-place listing.

**Indexing the citations**
- He uses SpeedyIndex, a Telegram-bot indexer. He says it traps Google crawlers in a loop of sites and forces them to follow an outbound link to the submitted URL.

**CTR**
- Take an Uber from a few blocks away to the business, with the location set to the Google Business Profile. Tip the driver $5 for a five-star review.
- Jumper Media: his team tested it on one location. In 30 days the business went from outside the map pack to inside it. They piggyback on mobile app ad data to make it look like people travel to the location. They have a non-compete per niche in an area, so a second HVAC company would not be signed.
- His idea: partner with free step-counting or walking apps (Sweatcoin-style) and gamify walks to locations whose owners pay for CTR. Hundreds of people walking to one location a day should lift its ranking. He is looking for a developer to build it.

## Selling local SEO: niches, offers, pricing and outreach

Jacky says fulfillment is the easy part and sales is the hard part, so the community teaches mostly sales.

**Which niches**
- Target high cost-per-click service businesses: personal injury lawyers, roofing, pest control.
- Method: look at the service categories on Thumbtack, find the highest CPCs in your geography, and pitch those companies on ranking higher on Google Maps.

**Offer**
- The offer has to be strong because the market is competitive. A ranking guarantee works.
- He notes Jumper's trick of guaranteeing a tiny radius (about 5 km), where the business probably already ranks. Many SEO companies guarantee long-tail terms.

**Pricing**
- Price off the customer's average order value (AOV). A roofing company in Vancouver can pay up to about $400-500 a month. A restaurant might be about $300 a month, with an actual cost of about $50.
- Start high and drop the price until they say yes, but leave margin for the fulfiller.

**Outreach**
- Do what overseas competitors cannot: cold calls (he says a local accent helps) and cold walk-ins. One community member bought $100 of donuts and closed the client the next day.
- Cold email: niche down (for example "easyroofing.com", not a generic SEO brand name) and send high volume. Put a naked, unclickable domain in the signature. He says it gets about a 10% visit rate, so 100 emails a day brings about 10 direct visits.
- Send the first email with no links and the second a day later with a link, to protect deliverability. He uses cheap SMTP servers. He cares about opens and traffic to the site, not reply rate.

## Local Rank's community funnel and TAM creation

Jacky's product, Local Rank, sells to people who run local SEO agencies. He says the local SEO tool market is tiny because few people offer local SEO, so the company has to educate people and "build the actual TAM".

**Funnel**
1. Organic content on X: free swipe files, such as a 65-point local SEO checklist, are teased in a long post. The CTA is to comment "local rank" and get the file by DM. The post is in a "boring local SEO" style.
2. The email is required to receive the template. After sign-up: a VSL right away, heavy retargeting, and a roughly 40-email flow.
3. Direct-response ads retarget into the paid community, Local Rank Academy (the pitch: first client in 90 days). Everything taught uses Local Rank.
4. The software is upsold from the community.

**Numbers and lessons**
- The community did about $5K MRR in its first month. He runs it at break even to offset the paid ads budget, and treats the software as "the gravy".
- An enterprise client paid about $20K for the year, which he says came through the community and would not have happened without the education.
- Pushing people straight into the software did worse after heavy testing, because it is too hard to use without education.
- One month on X: roughly half a million impressions, which he equates to about $20K in Meta CPMs.
- His moat is that his team does local SEO for clients themselves, so they find small problems that copycats cannot.

## Channel results: YouTube, Meta, Google, Reddit

- **YouTube:** he says about 90% of the roughly $15K MRR at launch came from YouTube, from a coupon he gave out on YouTube and X. He had talked about local SEO on the channel for about half a year to a year before launching. His advice: "go all in on YouTube" and dominate an industry, then launch. Demand is built first and then captured.
- **Meta ads:** about $120 CAC, payback in two to three months, churn maybe 10-15%. Profitable but worse than he expected.
- **Organic X:** the most profitable channel for him, via the funnel above.
- **Google and YouTube ads:** did not work. CAC was about $400 and he says they burned roughly $20K learning that.
- **Reddit ads:** about the same as Meta, targeting SEO subreddits.

## Reddit parasite SEO and moving search volume

**Reddit parasite SEO**
- Parasite SEO means piggybacking on another site's domain authority.
- SOP: search the target keyword (for example "best creatine gummies"), open the top Reddit result, and leave a soft-sell comment on that thread with no link ("I tried XYZ creatine and liked it"). Buy about 50 upvotes and 10 comments. He says it can rank in about a week for roughly $10 of upvotes, versus years with a new site.
- He says it also feeds AI answers, since ChatGPT and Perplexity pull heavily from Reddit.
- He says upvotes and downvotes can steer a thread's direction, and that buying large subreddits from their mods (he guesses about $5K) is an opportunity.

**Lifting branded search for "local rank tracker"**
- He says Local Rank is #4 for "local rank tracker" on Google about a month after launch, above Semrush. Competitors have existed for years.
- Tactics: put "search local rank tracker on Google" in cold email signatures and in job listings.
- He used GSA to send about 70K contact-form fills in 7 days. The message names the brand and the search term, with no URLs, so his domains are not blacklisted. The goal is search impressions, not conversions.
- Result: Search Console shows about 6.5K searches a month versus roughly 1,900 in Semrush.
- He turned the idea into a product, Browser Blast, which mimics virality to push rankings. He says driving traffic and positive signals to one URL can lift rankings two to three pages at a time.

## AI SEO: what he sees and what he distrusts

- He says no agency has figured out how to rank in LLMs consistently, and he would monetize it if he had. He warns buyers to be careful with agencies that claim to.
- He says the pattern so far is brand mentions: LLMs pull from popular subreddits and page-one-to-three listicles, and Google AI Overviews pull from press releases.
- Press releases and listicles that name you first (for example "best Vancouver SEO company") tend to get you into AI Overviews. A #1 spot in the biggest subreddit on a topic tends to get you into ChatGPT. He has not cracked Perplexity.
- His case study for a Vancouver physio clinic got press releases mentioned for "best physio Vancouver 2025", but not consistently.
- Idea he is testing: build your own subreddit, buy about 100K members, and simulate activity to see whether LLMs treat it as a major source.
