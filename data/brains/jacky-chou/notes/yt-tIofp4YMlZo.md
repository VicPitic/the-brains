## Affiliate partnership model: Amazon rate card with a 20% cut

- Jacky runs an Amazon affiliate account that partner sites attach to. Partners get onto his Amazon rate card and he takes a 20% cut.
- Amazon pays out net 60, so partners receive 80% of their earnings about 60 days after the sale.
- He says the cut is worth it for partners because "we ran the numbers and the uplift is more like 40% in most cases."
- Example: one partner's site produces 19.1k a month, and Jacky's 20% is 3.8k a month "from just existing."
- He added a couple of new sites to the Amazon account in this update.
- Day snapshot: about 11.2k total daily revenue, made up of Amazon 6,920, Mediavine about 3,000, FanFuel and Cell Health about 200, and OnlyFans about 1,059. Amazon ordered revenue that day was 128.2k, multiplied by 54 to get to the Amazon number.

## Revive pricing and the bundled indexer

- Revive is his content refresh product. The refresh workflow is to rewrite the first paragraph of each article, change the publish date, and resubmit to Google for indexing. He calls this a strong freshness signal for Google and says they have had "incredible success."
- He built an indexer as part of the Revive plan. It went out as a tag-on to the "baller" plan at no extra charge.
- Competing indexer tools such as Tag Parrot and URL Monitor do the same thing for up to about $100 a month. Jacky "ripped the best features of the top three indexers" and included them in the baller plan.
- It wasn't hard to develop, and it pairs well with Revive because resubmitting refreshed pages is the main use case.
- He plans to raise Revive prices possibly the following month, depending on how things go.

## Cold email benchmarks and follow-up

- Cold email tooling mentioned: Instantly, after 20 days of warm-up.
- Good response rate: 2 to 5% is decent. Under 1% means "your copy sucks."
- Once a subject line like "quick question Jacky" works, you don't need to keep checking open rates.
- For prospects who haven't replied, keep following up. "Don't be ashamed to follow up," and you can keep going "until they block you."
- He says US prospects are generally more open to being sold to, while Europeans (he gives Germans as the example) react worse to repeated follow-ups. He frames this as cultural.

## CTR manipulation tools: his test results

- He tested CTR manipulation tools (SERP Clicks, Agency Assassin, CTR tools like CTR RFI) and is now cancelling the subscriptions.
- SERP Clicks: 5 clicks a day, about 400 credits (a couple hundred dollars, he doesn't remember exactly). His verdict: "I don't think so," meaning it did nothing noticeable.
- A free 7-day test (12th to 19th) at 10 searches a day for "physiotherapy East Vancouver": ranking went from 16 on the 12th to 6 by the 19th. After the campaign ended it trickled up, and he isn't sure it did anything.
- Agency Assassin on a client site (Movement Room) ran a month starting the 12th. Rankings for physio Vancouver went up a lot, so it might have done something, but he is unsure and says it "doesn't feel like it did much."
- Overall he can't attribute results to CTR manipulation and found some of the tools hard to use. He discovered one campaign had been paused the whole time.
