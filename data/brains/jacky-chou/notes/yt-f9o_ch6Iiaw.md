## Software launch and validation approach

- Jacky pivoted toward software after seeing "the writing on the wall" for SEO and niche sites. He now focuses on local SEO, Reddit marketing and link building.
- He launches fast. Local Rank launched in about 2 weeks and Tracking's in about a week.
- Validation is revenue-based: he checks conversion rates and whether there is audience alignment with the product.
- Benchmarks: Tracking's did about 20K in its first week. Local Rank did 10K in its first month.
- If there is product-market fit, he goes all in. If not, he either kills it or puts it on autopilot.
- Examples of duds still making money on autopilot: a WordPress hosting service and a couple of WordPress plugins at about a grand a month each. They take zero bandwidth.
- He does not look at TAM. He launches in mature spaces and checks whether competitors exist.
- His tactic is bundling, "the Adobe approach": everything under one subscription at the same price.
  - Local Rank bundles GBP posts, review boosting, citation building, a lead source and an audit.
  - The rank tracker is slower than competitors (about double the time) and is given away free as a bonus.
  - "We're not like the best product in any category, but we're just like the only one that's like good enough."
- He works with a co-founder who ships software quickly, and he now always partners with someone who executes.
- On his software money sites (e.g. Local Rank) he does no link building and no AI content spam. Local Rank has about three blog posts.
- He says people blasting thousands of articles a month "have not seen pain before."

## Risks of black hat SEO and public income reporting

- Jacky reports his numbers publicly every month and has not missed one in about 3 years.
- He deliberately stopped reporting his organic SEO income after a public hit. He had 15+ partnership sites with legacy newspapers (affiliate subdirectories, like forbes.com/advisor but for smaller papers), making upwards of 500K/month.
- Google found them and destroyed the subdirectories overnight, dropping revenue from about 500K to about 80K per month in roughly 2 weeks. He had blasted thousands of articles a day for months.
- The Search Console properties of all 15 were hit. Client sites where he did not own Search Console were not hit.
- He pulled the plug rather than risk the newspapers' core businesses.
- His lesson: when something big works, "sit on my hands" and make as much money as possible instead of announcing it. Announcing brought partnership flywheel inbound, but wins can disappear within a week.
- On haters: they usually never stuck with one thing and were burned by gurus. He says that if anyone sticks with something for 90 days they make 10k/month, and that 30 minutes a day of cold calling for 90 days would replace a full-time job. He also claims posting every day for 100 days changes your life.

## Link building tactics by risk level

- **White hat (digital PR):**
  - Take public government data and make it visual in Canva.
  - Post it to r/dataisbeautiful with your URL as the citation, and buy upvotes.
  - Pick a controversial angle, e.g. Canadian housing: median wage vs housing prices, "30 years to get your first down payment." Post it to a Canadian subreddit with about 500 upvotes and reporters pick it up the next day.
  - He admits it is arguably gray hat.
- **White hat (citations):** hand-register citations (Crunchbase, Yelp, Foursquare) for any new site, even non-local ones.
  - His service uses VAs with AI, because sites like Yelp have hurdles. Some sites are 100% AI, some need minimal intervention.
  - He hand-wrote a 65-point local checklist before ChatGPT.
- **Black hat:**
  - PBNs still work. Tiered links are great.
  - Anyone saying "PBNs don't work" or "citations don't do anything" gets written off entirely.
  - Canonical or hreflang buffer: build backlinks to alternate-language sites (e.g. Spanish and German) that point to your main English site, and don't link to the English one. Canonicals and hreflangs don't pass on penalties right now, as he sees it. Used in his private community SEO contest (made-up keyword, prize about a couple thousand dollars).
  - Unclappable high-DR domains: Amazon, Facebook, Reddit. He says Google doesn't penalize DR90+ sites.
    - Build your own subreddit: about 5 posts a day with about 10 comments per post, bought, to look active.
    - Facebook groups are starting to rank like Reddit.
    - Redirect penalized or deindexed domains to a subreddit and republish the old articles there. He says penalties are domain-side, and content that once ranked still ranks.
  - Amazon listings are the easiest thing to rank on Google. Send traffic and build backlinks.
- **Parameter indexing:** when a site has no noindex on search pages, you can force-index a search-result URL with an anchor-text-laden link and make it ranking (the Disney Plus example). This is common for selling OnlyFans or phone numbers.
- **Negative SEO:** he won't do it, and says disavow only if you know a penalty exists. If he had to, he'd churn and burn by pointing about 100 PBN links with exact-match anchors at the target page, which rises then drops.
- He thinks Google engineers are focused on AI products, so organic algorithm changes are few right now.

## CTR manipulation and Browser Blast

- Jacky still does CTR manipulation. He says Google gets its data from Chrome (already confirmed), so Chrome signals still move rankings.
- Traditional proxy-based CTR software no longer works.
- What works now is cheap real human traffic, e.g. from adult sites, sent directly to the URL. He calls his service Browser Blast.
  - It imitates virality: about 10k sessions from 10k users worldwide.
  - It is direct referral traffic, not branded searches.
  - He has tested it across many niches, including casino.
- DIY test: run Meta ads to the specific URL, targeting Chrome users, for 10k sessions. If the page is in the top 3 pages, he says rankings go up. He claims Browser Blast is cheaper than Meta ads.
- AI mode and citations: Google AI mode pulls citations from top-ranking Google articles, so ranking still matters even if clicks fall.

## Rank and rent, retail rank and rent, and where SEO still works

- **Rank and rent:** rank a site (e.g. a Vancouver tree services site) and sell leads at about 100 per lead, or rent it for about 1,000 a month.
  - SERPs now show 4 ads, an AI overview, the map pack, then organic. Little traffic remains except in rural areas without AI mode or ads.
  - He only recommends it if you can get a Google My Business profile. Ranking GBP is "infinitely easier" than organic, and you can rank local within a week.
- **Retail rank and rent:** rank "best protein powder" (e.g. 100k searches a month) and sell the #1 sponsored slot to supplement brands, who reach out inbound.
  - At peak it got up to 30-40K a month for one page. Now the most is about 2K a month per page because traffic dropped.
  - Leave the buyer margin to run the page profitably: with about 50k traffic a month, run conversion-rate math. He prefers 2K a month for 2 years over 10K for a single month.
- **Where SEO still makes sense:**
  - Restricted niches (casino, cannabis, adult, health, gambling) where AI mode doesn't trigger and SERPs are still 10 blue links with zero ads.
  - E-commerce: paid ads first, SEO as a supplementary budget.
  - SaaS: he wouldn't invest in SEO. Ads are the unlock. At most he does Reddit parasite and "best X" listicles targeting head keywords.
  - Niche affiliate publishers: he saw HouseFresh go from about 50k visits a month (2023) to about 1,000 after the HCU to about 200,000 now, 4x its pre-HCU high. He thinks it is part Google "putting their thumb on the scale" and part great marketing. He notes friends' sites, with real experts and millions of YouTube subs, are still in the gutter.

## Getting cited in AI answers (ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Perplexity)

- Jacky's method: check what each model currently cites, then replicate those citations.
- Top cited per his tracking tool: Wikipedia for ChatGPT, YouTube for Gemini and Perplexity, Yelp for Claude. ChatGPT no longer openly cites Reddit.
- Wikipedia play: get a page with citations that list your product as #1 (e.g. Local Rank as the top local SEO software). Wikipedia links are nofollow but still valuable because ChatGPT reads the page context. He believes Wikipedia and ChatGPT have a direct data stream.
  - You can pay someone to get a page in, or get enough unpaid, controversial press to be prominent. He tried to make one for himself 10 years ago and was not prominent enough. After the Search Console wipeout press, he was.
- Listicles across PR sites that consistently list you #1 help, because the model reads the consensus.
- He says to only quote him as "at this very moment, this thing works right now."
- To filter guru advice, test the tactic across about 10 sites. If it fails, write off that person's future claims.

## Landing page conversion rules

- Call to action above the fold, always. No carousel above the fold.
- Put a VSL on every page that gets significant traffic, including SaaS and local pages. Few people watch it (e.g. 100 of 10,000 visitors), but those who do convert about 10x higher.
- Paid ads need a dedicated landing page, never the homepage.
  - His Local Rank ad pages are noindexed replicas with no menu and no footer nav. The only footer links are legal ones (privacy and terms). Visitors can only convert or leave.
  - He believes removing the nav and footer can 2-3x conversion (e.g. 1% to 2%).
- The best paid landing pages are long. He cites info-product pages of about 20,000 words, 40 testimonials and a VSL above the fold. They have spent millions perfecting the format, so copy it.
- Design is not conversion. A designer-made redesign tanked his conversion rates, and he says a design agency can convert worse than a ClickFunnels page.
- Use simple language, not sophisticated wording. Sell the result and the transformation in a headline of about six words, not the features.
