## Refreshing publish dates to trigger recency boosts in SEO

- Jacky changes the publish date on his blog posts to the most recent date about once a month or once every two months.
- He does it on sites he has owned for a while, and on sites he wants to push from rank 5-10 up to the top 5.
- When he buys a website, he immediately slaps on a nice theme, designs it well, and changes all blog posts to the most recent date. That "typically gives it a jump". He then optimizes the posts after the jump and builds from there.
- When he is lazy, he changes only the date and no content, and the rankings pop immediately.
- He says this works extremely well for tech niches because "tech's all about recency".
- He notes that if articles are good enough, there is always an excuse to go back, update small bits and change the date again.

## Recency and Google reviews in local SEO

- Jacky points to an agency study that found a correlation between local SEO rankings and the recency of Google reviews. The finding: the more recent reviews a business has, the better it ranks.
- He cautions it may be correlation, not causation. A business ranking number one gets more traffic, so it gets more recent reviews.
- He sees a plausible mechanism: a business that consistently earns five-star reviews over months signals to Google that it is good, so Google keeps sending it traffic.
- He says Google reviews are easy to abuse and that the market rate for bought reviews is about $2 to $10. He says he has never done it. The risk is losing your Google My Business location.
- His advice: if there is any way to convert something into local SEO, do it.

## Original images for review content at scale (UGC-style visuals)

- For reviews, he says the standard approach is to take your own photos of the product and post them so Google sees original content.
- At scale, or for products you cannot buy (he says you cannot buy every MacBook), he describes an "unethical hack":
  - Take stock images or Amazon product images.
  - Remove the background in Canva and add your own background, such as a photo of your table.
  - Flip the images so Google cannot find them in reverse image search.
- The result is new, original-looking images. He frames it openly as a shortcut, not a best practice.

## YouTube as a self-reinforcing loop for a site that already ranks

- Jacky is "pretty bullish on YouTube right now". His team is editing long-form YouTube videos for an architecture site.
- One video was at 23K views after about a week and a half.
- His loop:
  - Organic YouTube views come in.
  - Embedding the video on a website that already ranks pushes more views to the video.
  - More views lift the video's rank on YouTube.
  - The video then pushes traffic back to the site.
- In the architecture niche he is also running a challenge of two TikToks a day for three months. It is going well for follower growth on Instagram and YouTube but is not yet driving much direct traffic to the site. TikTok itself has not been cracked yet.
- A Far & Away TikTok flopped, while the same content did OK on Instagram at about 15K views.

## DTC collapse, Made.com, and what it signals for niche sites

- Jacky's account of Made.com, a DTC furniture brand: it was valued at about $1 billion about a year earlier and listed on the London Stock Exchange. It raised about $135M and did about $500M in annual revenue. It went bankrupt and sold its brand and domain for $3M.
- He also cites Touch of Modern, which did $120M in annual revenue and sold its assets for $7M.
- His diagnosis: online shopping surged during COVID, consumers did not return as projected, and the companies ran projections off the surge. Revenue dropped, burn rate was too high, and leadership did not cut drastically to a slow burn.
- DTC logic is to burn cash on ads to grab market share in hopes of profitability later. He expects a lot more pain for DTC and e-commerce aggregators, especially in Q1.
- Implication for niche sites: slowing consumer spending trickles down to who pays for display ads and affiliate commissions. He expects to feel it around Q2-Q3.
- His plan: offload some niche sites in January at whatever multiples he can get, and hold the big ones for a long time.

## Search engines, Bing traffic, and platform shifts

- Apple search engine: he thinks it is a smart play, comparing it to Apple's iOS privacy update that hurt Facebook before Apple launched its own competing product. He flags possible antitrust risk. He agrees Google needs competitors even though his revenue depends on Google traffic.
- Bing: some of his sites get 15-20% of traffic from Bing. Funnily, the AI sites get a lot of it. He credits the Rank Math Instant Indexing API (instant index to Bing) and recommends installing it.
- Mastodon: he sees it as growing fast after Elon bought Twitter, but expects it to die within months. Open source and server-based instances make finding friends hard, and it is "not built to scale". He compares it to Clubhouse, which blew up and then faded.
- TikTok: he watches Chinese TikTok (Douyin) to see what TikTok will look like in the US in about two years. Examples are integrated one-click shops with a hovering buy-now button during live streams, where people buy off the streams as a daily routine. He also sees opportunity in live streams of people making products by hand, such as pouring candles, and selling them.
- He notes TikTok copied the BeReal format with a "now" feature.
