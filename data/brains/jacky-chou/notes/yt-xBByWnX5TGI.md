## Affiliate SEO outlook for 2024: what still works

- Jacky is "very, very bullish" on affiliate. He expects affiliate revenue to stay around.
- He calls display ads "clapped": third-party cookie removal plus Google SGE is "the perfect storm". Anyone bullish on display ads "needs to check themselves."
- He calls informational content dead within about two years. His view: a query like "can dogs eat lettuce" gets answered by SGE. He puts it at 90% certainty that a site fully reliant on Google informational traffic loses its revenue within five years.
- Content that needs nuance and has health or money impact survives better, e.g. "best testosterone boosters for men over 50", where people want to read reviews.
- He does not recommend expired or auction domains in 2024: Google catches them quickly. He has never seen one live more than two years in recent years. They are only worth it for short-term gains.
- Parasite SEO is here to stay for a while but is a cat-and-mouse game. A page on Outlook India might work for three months, get clapped, and then the play moves to Google Groups or LinkedIn Pulse. Parasite is for short-term gains.
- For long-term gains, he says to build from scratch properly and move toward partnerships.
- Link building needs more variety in 2024: citations, niche edits, guest posts, "a bit of everything". Treat the site like a real business rather than a niche-edit-only play.
- Niches:
  - Home is evergreen and needs no content updates.
  - Supplements require catching trends. If Huberman mentions something, publish immediately. One term went from about 100 searches a month to about 100k after a celebrity mention.

## The partnership (rev-share) model

- Origin: a health-tech startup founder with a DR6 site (at the time) and a couple million sessions a month messaged Jacky. They were running out of runway and could not monetize the traffic. Jacky took a risk on a rev-share deal, which he had never done before.
- Deal structure:
  - Low share on display ads.
  - 50/50 on affiliate commissions.
  - No cut of the partner's core product revenue (e.g. their online TRT service). He only takes commissions from affiliate content on their blog.
- Result: that first partnership scaled from 0 to $300K a month in under three months.
- Why it works: he piggybacks on the partner's existing authority (built through VC funding, PR and a big team). The partner is struggling to monetize and needs cash.
- Why partners don't do it themselves: they lack the expertise in ranking, keyword research and industry outreach to thousands of brands a week, and they have full-time jobs.
- Jacky's view of the competition: niche sites have been clapped because the SERPs are owned by big companies and private-equity groups. His response is to "fight fire with fire" and partner with one of those big sites rather than compete with a DR5 niche site.
- He rolls up partnerships, but saturation hits fast. With 20 partners in one niche you have already dominated page one, so the next step is new niches or your own brand.

## Retail rank and rent on partnership sites

- Plain Amazon affiliate articles on a ranking site might earn about $50k a month.
- The step further: once ranking for a term like "best protein powder", pitch the top brands directly. The offer is "pay us 20K a month, we'll put you on number one, and 50% commissions on top."
- It is a no-brainer for a brand with around 90% gross margin, since they still profit and are seen as number one.
- He calls these "retail rank and rents". They make up the majority of partnership revenue.
- This requires heavy sales: three full-time salespeople sending outreach emails.

## Finding your first partnerships

- Start small. A DR90 software company doing $10M a year will not talk to a nobody.
- Think creatively about who you know. His example: a well-known downtown Vancouver restaurant he frequents, whose owner he knows, that is DR50. That could become a kitchenware affiliate site or a recipe site.
- Smaller-DR sites also work and do well, depending on the niche.
- E-commerce partnerships are another route. His pilot was Far and Away, his e-commerce company with a business partner (Albert):
  - He did a two-year proof of concept, scaling slowly before showing the partner revenue.
  - Informational and affiliate content irrelevant to the store now adds a flat $10-15K a month.
  - E-commerce runs at 10-20% net margins at most, so extra free cash flow is valuable. Far and Away did multi six figures in November with 20-30% net margins.
  - Paid ads to the brand also send positive brand-search and social signals to Google.
- He is bullish on e-commerce partnerships, especially with brands spending seven figures a month on marketing.

## Agency and landing the first client

- Jacky is not taking traditional retainers. His minimum retainer has been raised to about $10K a month because he does not want to deal with them. Most of his growth now comes from partnerships.
- First-client tactic for a beginner: rank-guaranteed offers.
  - Rank for a low-competition local term, like "best Vancouver realtor" (about 100 searches a month). He says if you cannot do it, "maybe you should question yourself".
  - Example: he used a parasite asset on Outlook India to rank number one for "best Vancouver realtor", then did outreach: "I ranked you at first place." If they don't pay, move to the next realtor.
- Local clients pay at most about $2K a month. This is fine when starting out, but five of them at $10K total means five people breathing down your neck, which is tiring.
- Directories are a good source of local clients. Once you rank for a term, others ask to be ranked too.

## Directory-style rank and rent

- Jacky defends James Dooley's rank-and-rent: it works at scale in high-CPA niches. For example, a job worth $20-30K at $10K a month per asset per city across multiple UK cities makes the math work. He says people should research before hating.
- His own approach differs: add directories to partnership sites. For a job-board partner he would build an SEO agency directory (e.g. jobboard.com/agency), copying the Clutch.co model.
- Clutch does about $30M a year selling Yelp-style ads to SEO companies. Jacky has spent $20K a month with them and says the deals were incredible.
- The idea: "Clutch for dentists, Clutch for daycares, Clutch for real estate agents".
- Closing is the hard part. Most won't pay, so he often takes ad revenue instead.
  - Easier in high-risk niches: in Canadian cannabis, he messaged two people and got "all right we're in for 2K a month".
  - Harder with Vancouver real estate agents, who close multi-million dollar deals but won't pay $500 a month.

## Closing the loop and diversification

- 2024 goal: "close the loop" by launching his own products rather than only rolling up partnerships.
- Reasoning: he owns four or five slots on competitive supplement keywords. Keyword tools show a term at about 50 searches a month, while the Amazon volume he pushes is about 3K a day, so he has insight into what is selling that tools miss.
- Options: pass the insight to supplement partners and take equity, or launch the product himself. His Amazon logic: if he pushes $1K a day to a product via Associates, he could launch on Amazon, get about 10 reviews, take a 50% haircut and still make $500 a day.
- He does not chase virality-dependent traffic such as Pinterest or Google Discover. It is inconsistent and hard to project. He has sites with thousands of Discover visits a day and doesn't know why, so he finds Discover courses make no sense.
- Paid ads on Far and Away are his diversification beyond Google.

## Community and personal brand

- He is putting more focus on his paid SEO community (Indexsy-style). It dropped to about $1,500 a month when he ignored it. Since he started focusing on it, it has grown to over 100 members who are active and refer each other.
- His audience skews toward people purely focused on making money, not in-house SEOs talking about schema.
- He is bullish on continuing to post on Twitter (most active there) and YouTube. His view: you can make a lot with few viewers if you dial in the content. Friends can't sit through his videos, but those who do have SEO expertise and a minimum income level, so the audience is valuable.
- He directs people to marketingletter.com for his net worth and newsletter.
- Last month's business revenue was an all-time high of $500K, with profit in the high 30s percent.
