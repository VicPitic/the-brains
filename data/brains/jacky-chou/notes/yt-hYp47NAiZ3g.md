## Setup of the $10,000 backlink case study

- Jacky (founder of Indexsy, nearly a decade in SEO) spent about $10,000 across 10 link vendors to see which had the biggest effect on rankings.
- He split the budget across vendor types: niche edits, PBN providers and guest post providers.
- Spend per vendor:
  - Control domain: $0
  - Fat Joe: about $1,000
  - Rank Authority: about $1,100
  - Hatred PBN: about $1,000 as budgeted (he ended up spending only about $200, see the results section)
  - Loganix: about $1,000
  - Rhino Rank: about $1,100
  - Growth Resolve: about $1,000
  - Grindstone SEO: about $1,000
  - Upttops: about $1,100
  - Indexsy (his own agency): about $1,100
- He discloses the bias of including his own agency and tells viewers to exclude it from their own read if they want.

## How he controlled the variables

- 10 different domains, all aged exactly 12 months. They were registered and sitting idle, never used for PBN content or anything else.
- The same 800 informational keywords were targeted across all 10 sites, e.g. "can dogs eat lettuce", "can dogs eat apples".
- Keywords had over 100 searches a month in Ahrefs and keyword difficulty under 20.
- All articles followed the same structure and were generated with an auto-blogging service, published directly to WordPress. He says it costs less than a dollar per article.
- Caveat he states himself: these are low-difficulty keywords, so results would likely differ for more competitive terms.

## Results ranking (worst to best)

- **10th, Upttops (Vietnamese PBN seller):** popped up early, then tanked and never recovered. He had seen great results from them before, so he suspects a bad batch.
- **9th, Grindstone SEO (post-that-rank / niche-edit style service):** the owner warned that his links aren't the right pick for a brand-new site because they look unnatural. Part of the budget went to foundational links, but it didn't work. DR was around 8 to 13.
- **8th, Loganix (niche edits):** links looked good and they had the best ordering process and account management of all the vendors, with proactive updates. They let you pre-approve links. Jacky picked domains with the most traffic rather than the most relevant, which may be why it didn't move rankings. No penalty, just flat.
- **7th, Fat Joe (guest posts):** early growth then sideways. Solid links, weaker DR, no penalty. He used to resell their services to agency clients.
- **6th, "Powerhouse" (homepage PBN links, found on Black Hat World):** reached DR 45 on Ahrefs with about 500 referring domains and about 200 traffic after six months. He suggests it could suit content-farm builders.
- **5th, Growth Resolve (Michael Johnson):** 3 links for $1,000, on domains with 30k to 100k traffic. About 32 referring domains, mostly spam. Steady climb. The vendor knew he was in the study.
- **4th, control domain (zero links):** about 1,000 indexed keywords and nearly 500 traffic a month from content alone.
- **3rd, Indexsy:** instructions were just "20 referring domains, 100+ links". Affordable.
- **2nd, Rhino Rank (niche edits):** 22 links for about $1,150 (price has since risen to $1,425). Low DR, but rankings went up, stayed up and reached about 670 traffic a month.
- **1st, Hatred PBN:** about 1,200 traffic a month after six months. He wanted to buy 50 posts but the owner refused to protect his network and refunded about $800, so Jacky spent only about $200. It had the best results and best cost efficiency. The owner blocked the crawler, so link quality can't be inspected.

## Takeaways Jacky draws

- Many vendors showed an early uptick followed by a drop that never recovered.
- The cheapest result (about $200 PBN) beat the pricier niche-edit and guest post services.
- A no-links control beating several paid vendors on low-difficulty keywords suggests that paid links are not always worth the money there.
- DR is a poor predictor: Rhino Rank had low DR but good rankings, while Powerhouse had DR 45 with modest traffic.
- Vendor relevance and link selection matter. He thinks he may have picked the wrong domains at Loganix.
- Vendors that limit how much they sell you (like Hatred) are protecting their network, which he reads as a sign of quality.

## Limits he acknowledges and his challenge

- He admits the study can be criticized: tiny keyword difficulty, a single run per vendor, and vendors who knew they were being tested.
- He says Hatred's owner pointed out where the study went wrong.
- His challenge to critics: run a better case study. Doing so takes money and action. If someone's is better, he will share it.
