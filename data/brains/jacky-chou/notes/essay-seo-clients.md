## Getting SEO clients: free audits as the opener

- Jacky argues clients need to understand what they're buying before they pay, which is why a free audit works. It breaks the ice, introduces the services, and shows the depth of the agency's capabilities and experience.
- He notes the average SEO client knows they need help but not how badly or how urgently. The audit shows them, and gives the agency a natural moment to explain how it would fix things.
- Target businesses that neglect SEO, especially local businesses that rarely update their websites. Find them, run a free audit, and send it.
- When these clients see results, he says they come back each time they need SEO services and refer other companies.

## Positioning: niche down to stand out

- Jacky says SEO is competitive and many agencies sell the same services, so an agency needs a strategy to show it delivers what a prospect is looking for.
- His main answer is to narrow the niche. Pick a specific industry based on your expertise or where you see profitable opportunity.
- If the agency is strong at technical SEO, make that specialty clear on the website.

## Finding clients where they ask for help

- Jacky recommends going where prospects describe their SEO problems and helping there, which shows expertise and builds trust before any sale.
- Example: someone asks how to rank for a keyword. Take that keyword and offer to bring the site to the top. If they get results, the lead is far more likely to convert to a paying client.
- Places he lists:
  - SEO and business forums: BlackHatWorld, SEO Forum, the Moz community. Most people there aren't looking for paid services, so don't expect immediate money. Contribute in discussions and someone will eventually reach out.
  - Quora: you may not find paying clients at first, but good answers bring requests for help. Quality answers can also rank on Google and bring in more leads.
  - Facebook and LinkedIn groups, subreddits, YouTube channels: participate so the target audience recognizes you and contacts you when they need help.

## Building authority: content, social, LinkedIn and speaking

- Content: post educational content regularly. He says this gives audiences new content, keywords, backlinks and social engagement. Basics he lists:
  - Keyword research, using the industry's key and trending terms in titles, subheadings and body.
  - H2, H3 and H4 headings so content is scannable and search engines understand it.
  - Links to other articles and sites, to improve usability and pass link juice.
  - Clear formatting so the value is easy to take in.
- Social media: prospects check whether an agency is active. Post useful SEO content, memes, opinions on the latest Google update, or videos explaining services.
- YouTube: he suggests a channel with informative videos, since videos can be optimized to rank and attract clients.
- LinkedIn: use it to educate, entertain and inspire. For outreach, he says to send "non-scammy" messages: mention something personal at the top, then give value in the first message.
- Conferences: speaking at marketing conferences isn't instant, but he calls it effective for the long term. After a talk, one or two people usually ask whether you offer SEO services. Meeting face to face builds trust fast.

## Partnerships and directories

- Partner with design agencies and digital marketing agencies. Web designers often get asked about SEO by their clients, so a partner will refer those clients to you.
- List the agency on directory sites. Fill in as much information as possible and get customers to leave reviews on them. Some directories aren't worth the time, but most can be useful. Examples he names: Agency Spotter, Digital Agency Network and SEOBlog.com.

## Pricing and social proof

- Jacky recommends offering monthly SEO packages. Most customers ask about pricing before starting, and transparent packages improve the odds of being chosen over competitors without them.
- Gather testimonials from current clients. He cites research that 72% of clients say positive reviews and testimonials help them trust a business, and 88% trust online reviews as much as personal recommendations.
- Video testimonials are best because they're more engaging than written ones. Written testimonials still give "social proof" if video isn't possible.

## Email outreach

- Jacky calls email outreach one of the most reliable ways to generate leads, and says email remains one of the most practical ways to stay in touch with existing and potential clients.
- Cold email works well for some businesses, while others get better results from an opt-in approach. Opt-in means you're dealing with people who have shown active interest in your work.
- His closing advice: pay attention to every detail, deliver great work, and educate prospects about SEO with evidence. The more clients you want to onboard, the more work that takes.
