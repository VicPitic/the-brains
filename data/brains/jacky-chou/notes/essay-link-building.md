## Why Jacky says link building matters and what a link-building service is

- He calls link building "among the most important SEO tactics" and one of the heavily weighted Google ranking factors.
- His reasoning: Google ranks sites that show authority, trust and expertise. When other sites link to yours, Google reads it as an authority signal and rewards those pages with higher rankings.
- He calls it one of the hardest parts of an SEO expert's job, so he recommends outsourcing it to link-building experts who build a custom strategy.
- A link-building service is a digital marketing service from an SEO agency or consultant that speeds up the number of backlinks to your site.
- White-hat methods he lists: guest blogging, broken link building, and manual outreach campaigns that show people your content.
- His warning: genuine, quality providers are hard to find. Many get low-quality links through spammy tactics Google dislikes.
- He says he "spent $10k on backlinks" and published the results (the details are not in this piece).

## How Jacky says to evaluate and hire a link-building company

**What a good company does:**
- **Discovery session:** it learns your business needs first and doesn't jump straight to a proposal. A tactic that worked for one site won't automatically work for yours.
- **Research and strategic recommendations:** delivered by email or on a call.
- **Contract with deliverables:** deadlines, resources, deliverables and budget spelled out in detail.
- **Client onboarding:** you share your website, blog and analytics, and they set how the project is managed.

**What to check before hiring:**
- **Competitor analysis:** do they study how many and what types of links successful competitors build?
- **Pricing:** shop around. Prices that look too good to be true versus other services are a red flag for low-quality links.
- **Bespoke strategy and roadmap:** tactics relevant to your industry, with a structured campaign plan.
- **Samples:** always ask for link samples and case studies to judge quality.
- **Team size:** his view is that large link-building companies decline in quality as they grow, because the mindset shifts from building quality backlinks to scaling the business.

## Link types Jacky says are effective

- **Guest blogging:** pitch authoritative owners in your niche. The link should sit in the body of the content or the author byline.
- **Blogger outreach:** it can go well or fail badly because it resembles spam. The difference is personalization: target specific blogs and journalists and write personalized emails explaining why they should link. A variant is to create the best content on a topic, find sites linking to your competitors, and ask them to link to yours instead.
- **Resource page links:** find resource pages listing useful sites in your niche and ask to be added. Ideally find a page linking to a dead site and offer yours. Only works if your site is useful and offers new insight.
- **Citation links:** list your business with its NAP (name, address, phone) in business directories. This works best for local businesses and adds geo-relevancy. Citations cost about $5 each, while other links can cost from several hundred to a few thousand dollars each.
- **Broken link building:** find broken links on popular sites in your industry, create better content on the same topic, and suggest the owner swap it in. It works because dead links hurt user experience and webmasters want them gone.
- **Brand mentions:** find sites that mention your brand without linking and ask for a link. The author already knows your brand, so these tend to go well.
- **PR links:** create a unique angle on your business, write a press release and distribute it to PR syndication sites.

## Link-building tactics Jacky says to avoid

His warning: link schemes may give short-term ranking gains but end in penalization, because Google is now better at spotting inorganic links. A respectable vendor shouldn't offer these.

- **Low-quality article directories** (ezinearticles.com, goarticles.com) with thin spun content. This stopped working with the 2011 Panda update.
- **Volume-based "link building packages"** that focus on link count over quality, built from one low-impact technique or a mix of link schemes.
- **Private blog networks (PBNs):** a black hat tactic of owner-controlled sites built to link out. He calls it an obvious attempt to manipulate rankings, and says most PBN providers only rent links that won't stand the test of time.
- **SEO press releases:** written for SEO rather than newsworthiness. The frequency voids the effort.
- **Spammy blog comments:** generic comments with links. Commenting is fine only when you genuinely engage with the blogger and readers.
- **Web 2.0 platforms:** setting up blogs to piggyback off domain authority. It is outdated, and providers spam many links, which doesn't work.
- **Low-quality directories:** directories users rarely visit. They bring no results and can take months to publish a submission.
