## Background and portfolio (as of Nov 2022)

- Jacky has an electrical engineering degree. He found SEO by googling how to make money online the night before a failed final exam, and landed on Black Hat World.
- Career path: digital marketing jobs, then director of marketing at a Berlin startup, then his own agency. His reasoning: "why are you doing SEO for clients when you can do it for yourself" if you are confident in it. That led him to buy and sell websites.
- Portfolio: 40+ content sites, several e-commerce businesses, and various lead gen sites.
- Revenue: six figures in e-commerce, six figures in content sites, five figures in lead gen.
- Team: a director of ops, many writers and editors, and an operator for lead gen and the agency side.
- Content site monetization: display ads, Amazon affiliate, VPN affiliates, web hosting affiliates. Roughly 10+ sites were started from scratch and many more were bought.
- He is always buying affiliate sites, especially Amazon affiliate sites, and says he closes deals within two days.

## Dropshipping: how he scaled it and the biggest mistake

- First attempt: he dropshipped manually before Oberlo, fulfilling orders with his own credit card. He spent about $1k on ads and made about $15k in revenue in two days. It died from ad fatigue because he had no idea how to run ads or make creatives.
- Second attempt: two years later his business partner proposed trying again. Each put in $5k of their own money, treating it as money to burn. They were down to about $1k of budget when one creative became a winner.
- Result: scaled to roughly $200-300k a month in revenue within a couple of months. The business later had a private equity exit, and he says they got burned on it and learned a lot.
- Biggest mistake: they kept dropshipping when they should have switched to private labeling immediately. He says it cost "hundreds of thousands of dollars" because competitors entered with faster shipping while he was content taking home about $100k a month.
- What he would do now:
  - Test ads with dropshipping.
  - When a product wins, source it, ship it in bulk to the market where the winners are, and hold it in a warehouse.
  - Customers then get products within a week, and you can build a returning customer base.

## Sourcing: 1688, 3PLs and finding products

- 1688.com is the Chinese-language version of Alibaba, for Chinese buyers. He says prices are 20-30% cheaper and most of the Western world doesn't know about it.
- Three ways to access it:
  - Use Alibaba itself.
  - Use a sourcing company (his own has two people).
  - Hire a Chinese speaker, even a local exchange student, to find the same product on 1688 and negotiate.
- Ship the goods to a 3PL and fulfil from there.
- 3PL tip: the top Google results for 3PLs are expensive and "absolutely rip you off." Look on page two of Google, and those smaller 3PLs give better courier rates in exchange for your business.
- Product ideas: use dropshipping spy tools for inspiration, find a cheaper version on AliExpress, pick products with nice creatives, shoot your own video, and launch as many ad variations as possible.
- Store: use Shopify, not WordPress, for dropshipping. He says they tested it and Shopify is easier and more scalable even though WordPress is better for SEO. He recommends the paid Prestige theme for conversions.
- He says "dropshipping is dead" claims usually come from people whose stores look terrible, so store quality matters.

## Ad channels after the iOS changes

- Facebook is very tough after the iOS changes.
- What works for his DTC company now: Google Shopping, and TikTok ads.
- TikTok creative needs a raw, ugc, non-polished feel. Aesthetic, Instagram-style content doesn't land. His younger brother, who is TikTok-famous, helps with it.
- Organic TikTok: he says viral reach involves manual curators, so content that looks too commercial or brand-led gets pushed down. Listicle-style content has a better chance.

## Amazon affiliates: selling your own products

- Shopify vs Amazon test: sending affiliate traffic to a Shopify store converted at about 1.5%, versus about 30% on Amazon for the exact same product (supplements). He says it wasn't even close, because "people love buying on Amazon."
- His idea: successful Amazon affiliate marketers control the traffic but stop there. They should launch their own private label products on Amazon FBA and recommend those.
- Caveat: check local laws on referring your own products (FTC in the US), and add a disclaimer.
- FBA cost and cash flow:
  - Door-to-door from placing an order to the Amazon warehouse is about three months.
  - That means ordering about six months of inventory up front, paid up front.
  - So he says it suits established affiliate marketers who have flipped a few sites and have cash, who love the niche.
  - His team has a couple of FBA businesses and is still figuring out the formula.

## Link building and starting new sites

- Delegates keyword research (Ahrefs).
- New site setup: connect socials with IFTTT so each published post also goes out there, and buy cheap Fiverr citations (about 50 for $5). He says most are nofollow but he is convinced they do something.
- Anchor text: for the first six months on a new site, build all homepage links with naked or branded anchors. He says exact-match anchors no longer move rankings, and many sites he sees have aggressive anchors and too many links to inner pages.
- On inner pages he uses branded anchors rather than naked URLs, which look odd in a blog post.
- He calls link building "an art": you keep testing and watch how Google reacts.
- Outreach:
  - He builds mostly niche edits, about 6k links last month across clients and his own sites.
  - Emails are two-liners that offer payment up front: "we want to link here, please let me know the price."
  - Everything, including follow-ups, is automated, and the back and forth is mostly price negotiation.
  - Minimum traffic on target sites is about 1k organic (or 500).
  - He pays about half of what he sells the link for, roughly $20-30, and uses scale as leverage ("if you accept this right now we will give you 30 other clients").

## Buying sites, expired domains and fixing penalized sites

- Expired and auction domains:
  - His only metric is the number of Ahrefs ranking keywords. A DR2 with lots of keywords is a buy, because "Google likes them."
  - He says backlink profile doesn't even matter. A nasty profile keeps auction bids low (a couple hundred dollars versus up to $10k from domain resellers).
  - Let the revived domain sit on its old content with SSL for one to two months before building. This shows where the traffic trend steadies and gives a benchmark. He credits another SEO for the idea.
- Site buying:
  - He buys mostly in home niches and avoids tech and gadgets because they aren't evergreen. He prefers evergreen topics like curtain rails.
  - Due diligence is a gut feeling: a quick look in Ahrefs. Under about $20k he barely checks backlinks, since a larger portfolio spreads the risk.
- Algo-hit sites:
  - He loves them. Sellers who are demoralized will take cash up front with no due diligence at a low multiple.
  - He says about 20% of these don't work, and he takes those losses.
  - Example: a site bought for about $3k that peaked at $15k a month and was down badly. His team got it back to about $3k a month.
- Revival playbook, done every time:
  - Fix speed.
  - Redesign (he says the design is horrifying 99 times out of 100).
  - About 20 homepage naked or branded anchor links, niche edits, about 500 citations to the homepage, and a foundational link package.
  - Wait a month, then check whether it is reversing before looking at other fixes.
- Design and speed:
  - Fixing a site's look can take about 10 minutes: new logo from Canva, a better font, or a Fiverr designer to move from Figma to WordPress.
  - Speed tools: WP Rocket with Cloudflare, or NitroPack.
  - Page builders: Gutenberg is the fastest. Elementor sites score in the 80s, especially on the Hello Elementor base theme. Thrive sites land in the 70s. Keep each builder on its native theme.
  - Most sites he sees score in the 20s. They are working to get indexy.io from the 60s to 70.
  - Warning: a redesign made one site beautiful but extremely slow, and traffic tanked.

## Lead gen: the towing site

- He bought a national "Yelp for towing" site making about $400 a month. It already ranked for "towing company near me" terms and the previous owner didn't know how to scale it.
- It now makes low five figures a month (about $10k). He says the site was worth about $250k at the time of one conversation.
- Acquisition of tow company customers:
  - Rank for B2B keywords such as towing leads and how to market towing companies.
  - Run Facebook ads targeting people with "towing" in their job description, offering a free month of calls.
  - Follow-up is automated: Zapier, then a five-email sequence, then a signup link for the free trial, then a subscription.
- Billing: moving billing from WordPress to SPP.co (a client dashboard) lifted conversion by about 50%. He recommends it for national lead gen.
- Hiring a call operator:
  - Tow owners dislike hearing an accent, so he hired locally in Canada or the US.
  - First a US person on commission only. Then a friend in Vancouver, convinced with base pay, large commission and equity.
  - The pitch: if the site goes from $250k to $700k, she gets a percentage of the sale.
- Staff incentives: his director of ops came by acquisition. Jacky bought 75% of his site and then hired him. He says buying a site from someone with a system in place is a good way to find staff, and he rates affiliate marketers above in-house and agency people.
