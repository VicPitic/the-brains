## Cloning indie hacker SaaS apps and outranking them

- Jacky says the biggest SEO opportunity right now is SaaS, a "beautiful industry" with very high margins, because "indie hackers are terrible SEOs." They follow LLM advice and are some of the worst SEOs.
- The SOP: go on TrustMRR, find an app that shows its revenue, out-market it and outrank it.
- Indie hackers publish their MRR on TrustMRR, so they make themselves targets. Jacky notes they usually rank for nothing, especially for what they do.
- Example: for an AI interior design app (a wrapper), he would one-shot the build overnight, then rank for "best AI interior design app" with guest posts, parasites or other tactics.
- Do it times 10. To be "extra spicy," launch multiple versions of the same app. Disclose per FTC rules; he says it is legal and still converts.
- Indie hackers all launch the same thing (social media schedulers, meta description checkers, robots.txt/noindex checkers) by ripping whatever Peter Levels, Marc Lou or Jack Friks launched. They blast a site with 1,000 articles, get hit, then say "SEO didn't work for me."
- Anyone who actually does AI SEO and builds a tool will make something "infinitely better" than what indie hackers ship.
- He credits indie hackers with great paid-ad funnels: $300-400K per month does not come organically. To launch an SEO software company, study their Meta ad library and roll it out on YouTube and Meta.
- He finds TikTok buyers weak and the targeting worse than Meta.

## How Jacky picks and validates software ideas

- He builds software from his own tool stack and personal need: "all my software that I launch, I launch because I have a personal need," assuming other agency owners have the same problem.
- Example: he paid keyword.com $600 a month for rank tracking, then vibe coded his own tracker. He launched it as a bring-your-own-key lifetime deal and sold about 100 licenses.
- To validate, he reuses one Meta pixel across all his sites. For each new launch he creates a retargeting campaign that spends about $30. If there is demand, it converts.
- For the lifetime deal, Meta ads pulled in about 15 sales for a little over $100 spent.
- His audience overlap is high because most of his software is in SEO and sold to SEO agencies.
- If a competitor survives on a $600 a month subscription, the market must have a wide base (a fat margin). The rest is a CAC-to-LTV ratio.
- Ideas for SEO agency owners: all-in-one client reporting; proposal software (he saw one on TrustMRR doing about $10K a month).
- He launches roughly once a week and runs about four AI coding threads in parallel. He argues anyone can build now, and marketers have the edge because engineers have "zero creativity."
- His goal is to plug AI into each app so it generates ads, runs prospecting ads and iterates once traction shows. He says he is nearly there.
- If he started over awkward and without sales skills: consumer software, driven by ASO and SEO. He took an iOS app from 0 to $1K per month in one month with only SEO, solo. He prefers iOS over Android because Apple users convert better.

## Diversifying income streams

- Jacky says his income from 2-3 years ago is gone. Display ads went away, and Amazon affiliate fell from roughly $300K to about $30K, "a 90% haircut."
- If he had gone all in on one venture then, he says he'd be at about $10K per month today. Hence he keeps launching and diversifying.
- He calls himself the "Thanos of moderate success" (his podcast co-hosts' joke). He launches non-stop and focuses on whatever hits.
- Winners cover losers, like a portfolio. His Reddit marketing tool started at $20-30K per month and is now under $4K. Local Rank went to $50-60K per month and is now about $30K. Index Checks grew 70% month over month last month (absolute revenue not shared).
- His advice: don't buy one domain and give it one shot. Expect to fail multiple times.
- On launching: don't over-engineer. Find out whether others share the problem and whether you can market to them.
- Marketing is now "a skill issue" and mostly about creatives. He says AI closing the marketing feedback loop (generate ads, analyze, iterate) could be "game over" for marketers. He estimates one to two months of leeway, and says now is the time to sprint.

## Affiliate, display ads and mom-and-pop sites

- Display ads are not dead, but only large publishers and influencers remain. A friend at a Raptive meetup says only big influencers there still make $50-100K per month in display ads, driven by their own social traffic.
- Informational keywords are gone to AI Overviews. His example: "my dog ate chocolate" is answered in the overview, so nobody clicks the blog.
- Affiliate is still alive and a good portion of his income. "Best XYZ" money is there even in industries with AI Overviews and Google Ads, because people still click organic results.
- Display ads and affiliate are not mutually exclusive. Keywords like "berberine supplement side effects" can still carry ads.
- Small blogs at DR 20-30 can't rank for listicle keywords, where most affiliate money sits.
- He checks only daily revenue. If he sees a down day he goes to rank trackers, then Search Console, and GA last.

## Parasite SEO, indexers and ranking tactics

- He is very bullish on parasites right now. They rank almost instantly, especially with instant indexers that get a URL indexed within about 5 minutes. Then you validate with likes and CTR signals.
- Parasite pages sit deep in a host site with weak internal linking, so without an indexer they never get indexed.
- To keep them ranking, send user signals (CTR, Chrome signals) continuously.
- A trick: 301 dead, penalized domains to the root username URL on a social platform (e.g. linkedin.com/jackie), letting it flow down into the post, which keeps the post stuck longer than pointing at the post directly.
- He does not use social media parasites himself (too weak for his competitive B2B niches). He has private, exclusive deals with high-DR publishers, 50/50 performance splits with nothing paid upfront, and says publishers still contact him.
- Economics: the best parasite placements cost $500 to $5K per month, paid upfront with no ranking guarantee. His targets are KD 80+ keywords, not zero-KD ones. Some placements fail, and winners cover losers.
- Listicles remain the core tactic, for Google and for ChatGPT. Find domains already ranking or cited, and pay them. "Everyone is open to it." Many are glorified content farms making little money.
- For ChatGPT: vary titles, don't repeat "best X 2026" 100 times, and don't always rank yourself #1. Place yourself in the top 1-3 of a top-10 list.
- LLMs no longer rely on Google's index as much (Anthropic always had its own, ChatGPT and Google have diverged). He says what ChatGPT cites is "pure trash" and easy to abuse because its spam filters are new.
- He is also bullish on buyer's guides over listicles, as a one-to-one relationship that adds fewer competing entities.
- His mistakes list: do not publish 1,000+ pages in a day, or a million programmatic URLs. Google hates it.

## Starting SEO: Jacky's framework

- "Learn by doing." Stop reading, iterate, and feel how the algorithm responds. He dislikes "SEO philosophers" who parrot Google's recommendations and don't practice. LLM-fed SEO advice comes from white hat in-house people at DR90 sites.
- If a site is penalized, don't dwell. It is hard to recover from an algo penalty, so move to a new domain. He jokes about 301ing the old one to a competitor.
- He prefers partial-match domains over exact match. His example: "Local Rank" tracker for "local rank tracker," since the two names differ by one word and confuse Google's entity matching.
- He would not focus on the main site for the first three months. Go offsite instead: paid (Meta), parasites, and guest posts on sites ranking for keywords tangential to your industry. Example: pitch a "best local rank tracker" article on a site that ranks for best SEO software.
- He is "so against" on-site SEO in 2026 because offsite gives more swings, and he wouldn't run aggressive tactics on his own site since Google is "waiting for an excuse."
- Google's response to AI slop: it can only favor high-authority sites (Forbes, etc.), which are harder to abuse but still buyable (a Forbes article for about $2K). So the winner is whoever has the largest wallet. He says Forbes is still safe from penalties partly because of its Google Ads spend.
- Sites hit by site reputation abuse (e.g. Outlook India) moved to a subdomain and left their guest posts behind.
- He regrets going so public about spamming Reddit, which may have cost him revenue.

## Reddit strategy and buying subreddits

- Reddit marketing is much harder now. Accounts need dedicated mobile IPs, and bans are quick.
- His preferred play: buy aged, active subreddits instead of farming accounts. Rank irrelevant subreddits for any keyword as long as they have members and activity (e.g. buy a fashion subreddit and rank it for "best SEO company").
- A black market exists. Prices he has seen run from about $5K for a small subreddit up to $50K. He would pay up to $50K for high-risk software niches and $50-100K for supplements. Gaming subs are far pricier ($500K-$1M).
- Pricing is "whatever you're willing to pay." Assess the mod team: New York mods won't sell for $20K, but mods in South Africa or in financial trouble might.
- He is not much into Reddit now and focuses on Google and LLMs.

## Choosing a niche and consumer vs. SEO products

- He says SEO in consumer niches is "way easier," "a walk in the park," compared to ranking digital marketing keywords.
- He has two consumer brands outside marketing.
- Rank and rent is tough for introverts because ranking is easy and selling is the hard part. He is "terrible" on sales calls.
- Fit the business to your strengths: charismatic people can do agency; awkward or engineering-minded people should do SaaS.
- Within SEO, pick narrow tool niches like indexing and rank tracking rather than competing with Semrush or Ahrefs. Many "SEO influencer" indexers are just API calls to other providers.
- Index Checks sees heavy fraud: stolen cards used to buy credits and burn through them, one person tried about 40 cards a day.
- He suggests the "Nord tactic," named after NordVPN owning many VPNs that top every listicle. Publish a listicle and build every tool on it, each with its own brand and backend, with FTC disclosures. People still buy.
