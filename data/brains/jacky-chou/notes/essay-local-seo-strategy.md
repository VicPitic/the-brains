## What local SEO is and how Jacky defines the strategy

- Local SEO is SEO aimed at ranking in local search results pages so more customers find and visit a business.
- Jacky frames the strategy as the overall process a local business owner uses to optimize site content and online presence for higher local rankings.
- Traditional SEO builds visibility on a wide scale. Local SEO concentrates on attracting customers near the physical location and getting them through the door.
- He says it is critical for businesses that depend on foot traffic or serve a specific geographic area.
- He says it takes "a lot of time, planning, and perseverance", and the strategy must be monitored and improved as the search landscape changes.

## How Google ranks local results: relevance, prominence, distance

- Jacky says Google mainly uses three signals for local rankings:
  - **Relevance:** does the business listing match the searcher's intent?
  - **Prominence:** is the business popular both online and offline?
  - **Distance:** how close the business is to the searcher at the moment of search.
- Distance can't be changed, so the strategy is to match the other two criteria well.
- If a searcher uses a location or "near me" and the listing matches the query well, the business can appear in Google's local 3-pack, which shows the top three matching local businesses.

## The five local ranking factors to target

1. **Accurate NAP (Name, Address, Phone):** keep it identical across the website, all social profiles and local directories. Inconsistent NAP can confuse search engines into thinking the business isn't legitimate or that there are multiple businesses.
2. **Titles, headers and meta descriptions:** include the city and locally relevant keywords for the service or product, on the homepage, blog posts and other pages.
3. **Localized content:** write about neighboring attractions, upcoming town events, or how-to articles about the services offered to signal relevance and authority for local searches.
4. **Local backlinks:** get high-quality links from trustworthy sites in the industry. They strengthen trust and relevance signals and can lift rankings significantly over time.
5. **Local schema markup:** give crawlers factual business data such as address, phone number, hours, ratings and reviews. It also shows snippets of information directly in the SERPs and adds credibility.

## Google My Business optimization

- Jacky puts the GMB page at the top of every business's local SEO to-do list as the simplest way to build brand awareness. A free listing improves visibility on Google Search and Google Maps.
- Stats he cites: GMB signals make up 33% of all local pack signals, and Google is the first place 63% of consumers go for reviews before visiting a business. Having no presence means losing customers to nearby competitors.
- Steps:
  1. Claim the listing. For multiple locations, use Google's Bulk Location Management Tool with a custom spreadsheet or Google's template.
  2. Fill in as much as possible: a short description, photos of products or services, and an eye-catching profile photo.
- He calls the profile photo one of the most important features for consumers in local results.
- Use a local search rank checker to compare review count and overall rating against local competitors and to find insights that improve reputation and rankings.

## Citations: finding and fixing them

- Citations are any place on the internet where the business's basic information appears. He says they make up about 7% of local search signals.
- Two types:
  - **Structured:** listings you create by filling out forms or building a profile page.
  - **Unstructured:** mentions you don't create, such as news articles, blog posts and "Best of" guides.
- Every citation must match. Incorrect info across citations costs visibility and can lower SERP rank.
- Audit process:
  - Search the business name, address and phone number to see where it's listed.
  - Also try a misspelled brand name, a slightly different address, and look for duplicate listings to surface inconsistent citations.
  - Note and correct the wrong ones. If a guide or blog post has it wrong, contact the author with the right details.
  - Control as many listings as possible for consistency and to monitor customer feedback.
- Beyond GMB, claim Yelp Business Page, TripAdvisor Business Profile and a Facebook Page.
- Citation software and local listing management services can handle the manual work.

## Website optimization and content

- Jacky argues ranking the company website on page one beats a third-party listing, because a click on your site (rather than Google's directory or Yelp) signals curiosity about your brand. Give visitors as much information as possible to make an informed decision.
- Website tactics:
  - Place valuable local keywords in content titles, site headings and image elements.
  - Add schema markup in the HTML to highlight relevant information.
  - For multi-location businesses, build a dedicated landing page per location and keep each URL basic so search engines and customers can find it.
  - Make the site mobile-friendly. He cites that 88% of local searches are on smartphones or tablets.
- Content:
  - He calls content "the backbone of any local SEO strategy" and says it can generate more leads than paid promotion and advertising.
  - It must be meaningful and useful, not just published in hope of leads. Keep tone and quality consistent across the site.
  - Pick broad topics that represent the business as a whole, not just ways to sell products. This shows industry understanding and positions the business as a thought leader on the local scene.
  - Search engines are the most common source of blog traffic, so include keywords related to the brand. Even a few high-quality pieces can set a business apart from competitors.

## Customer reviews

- Jacky says reviews are one of the top three ranking variables for getting into the local pack, and most shoppers rely on them for purchase decisions. Review quality and quantity on local listings can pull customers in or push them to competitors.
- Respond to both positive and negative reviews. Good responses can turn a one-time customer into a loyal one or bring a critic back.
- Actively ask for reviews using a range of tactics:
  - Feedback kiosk at checkout
  - Post-visit emails
  - Website review landing pages
  - SMS notifications
  - Asking directly
- Offering several routes makes the request convenient, and a steady flow of reviews strengthens the business image.
