## Can an Amazon FBA business be sold: business type decides saleability

Jacky says saleability depends on three things: business type, age, and annual net profit (SDE/EBITDA). The rule for the whole process is to always consider the buyer's perspective. Without that, a listing "could sit on the listings for months."

Business types, from least to most sellable:
- **Reseller (buy box):** Buys wholesale and sells at retail on Amazon. Can be hard to impossible to sell even if profitable. The reasons are volatility (unlimited competition), unreliable supply (wholesale items may not stay available), and nothing proprietary (the buyer is only buying access to a Seller Central account).
- **Private label:** Own brand, full control of the product. More attractive because supply is consistent and the products have a sales history, so they are likelier to keep selling.
- **Proprietary products:** Only your company sells the product, so buyers pay a premium. He calls this the "gold medal" category.

Buyers rank unique brand and product highest. His example: supplements with a custom formula are worth more than generic supplements. Private label comes next, then reseller.

## Valuing an Amazon FBA business

- Jacky says it is hard to know the true value until the business sells, but there is a rule of thumb: multiply annual net profit by two. In practice the SDE is usually multiplied by two.
- The offer is directly proportionate to net profit.
- SDE = Revenue – Expenses + Add-backs.
  - Expenses include cost of goods, employee fees, Amazon fees, software fees, fulfillment services, shipping services, and company taxes.
  - Add back any salary you pay yourself.
  - Personal expenses unrelated to the business do not count as expenses.
- SDE is the valuation basis for revenue below roughly $5 million to $10 million. EBITDA is used above $10 million.
- The buyer purchases inventory at cost, including the cost of getting it into FBA warehouses.

## Preparing your books so buyers don't walk away

- Jacky says to know your numbers inside out and keep them organized immaculately before looking for a buyer.
- "Buyers are often looking for that one reason not to buy." Clean books remove at least one reason.
- Start keeping current, accurate records now if you don't have them. Savvy buyers study trends in the P&L: are sales trending up, down, or steady?
- Some buyers also check the Amazon Best Sellers Rank to see where the business is trending.
- Use accrual accounting to track profitability each month. A proper P&L shows exactly how much profit the business makes monthly.
- The business is only considered if it has turned a profit, and higher profit makes it more attractive.

## How to increase the sale price

Jacky frames each lever through the buyer's lens.

**Age.** Buyers think in this progression: year one is discovery, years two and three are growth, and year four is optimizing net profit for sale.
- Under 1 year: not great, expect low-ball offers.
- 1 to 2 years: good.
- Over 3 years: great.

**Diversification.** The biggest risk of an FBA business is account suspension or losing Amazon rankings. Proving the business can weather a storm raises its value.
1. List multiple products in multiple niches.
2. Build profitable paid traffic inside Amazon (pay-per-click or sponsored ads).
3. Add traffic sources outside Amazon: a website, social media, email marketing.
4. Keep any single product or niche under 70% of the business's income.

**Defensibility.** How hard is it for a competitor to take your share? If the product is easy to source, anyone can launch a copycat listing. A patent raises value. Ways to increase defensibility:
- Proprietary products.
- Strong customer loyalty to the brand.
- Higher quality than copycat products.

**Competition.** Rankings can vanish overnight, and so can income, so buyers avoid businesses facing sophisticated FBA competitors. Keeping only one or two products in highly competitive markets removes that risk in the buyer's eyes.

## Sell or hold?

Jacky offers these questions to ask yourself:
- Is managing the business a chore?
- Am I working on something more exciting?
- Do I simply want more cash in the bank?
- Could I put the capital from the sale into a new venture?

If you answer yes to one or more, it may be time to sell. If you answer no to all, holding a bit longer may be best. Make sure the business's value isn't declining as time passes.

## Who your buyer is, by price tier

- **Under $500,000:** Mostly private investors seeking better returns than a bank.
  - They have low risk appetite because they deploy their own capital.
  - This tier holds the bulk of listings, so it is a buyer's market.
  - Many are new to Amazon and hunt for the best deal in range. They favor proprietary or own-brand businesses, which have a higher chance of finding a buyer.
- **$500,000 to $1 million:** A "black hole." Few private buyers will put down this much cash and it is a bit low for private equity, so the sale may take longer.
- **$1 million to $5 million:** "This is where the party is at." There are many cash buyers, and many want SBA financing, which goes up to $5 million.
- **$5 million and above:** Private equity buyers, strategic buyers, and family offices.

## Finding a buyer, timelines, transfer and payment

**Finding a buyer**
- List on marketplaces such as businessforsale.com, flippa.com, or bizbuysell.com.
- Ask other FBA sellers if they know an interested buyer.
- Many recommend a trusted broker for a smoother process.
- Jacky doesn't offer brokering but recommends Thrasio. He has heard only good things from sellers and says it moves quickly, with deals closed in under 5 days.

**Average time to sell, by price**
- Under $100,000: 66 days
- $100,000 to $500,000: 118 days
- $500,000 to $1 million: 151 days
- $1 million to $2 million: 128 days
- $2 million to $5 million: 146 days
- Over $5 million: 170 days

**Transfer:** It depends on your country. You must notify Amazon of the change and get its approval of the new owner.

**Getting paid:** Jacky calls this arguably the most important part for the seller. Use an escrow service that holds the money until both parties are happy, then releases it to your bank account by wire transfer.
