## Parasite SEO in late 2024: what still works and what doesn't

- Jacky was an early public tester of parasite SEO. Last year, buying posts on big sites (e.g. Outlook India) ranked for almost anything, from "best protein powder" to "best CRM software". He ran the tests in public so he wouldn't risk his partnership sites.
- After Google's manual site reputation abuse action, he says well-known public parasite sites are much harder to rank on for competitive terms. He calls it "not really an update" because Google had to manually go in and hit people.
- Private or lesser-known parasite sites he tested still "work like a charm". As long as Google doesn't know about a site yet, it is still easy to abuse.
- He says Reddit is currently the easiest public parasite site to abuse and works best of the public ones.
- Free parasites have not worked for him except Reddit.
- Reddit risk: he 301-redirected about 10 domains to a Reddit subdirectory in public. It ranked well, then Reddit removed the whole subreddit.
- Parasites are a tester and a funnel for him. He posts tests in public to attract partnership leads.
- His total parasite earnings are under about $10k. He did not sell the "best Vancouver realtor" page he ranked.
- Over 99% of his revenue comes from partnerships.

## Partnership model: how Jacky sources publishers

- Most deals are inbound. Publishers range from large bloggers at DR60 to private equity firms holding six or seven DR80+ sites. Once one signs, he gets access to their whole portfolio.
- He is now scaling outreach with a filter of DR60+ and contacts "anyone and everyone", which he calls a shotgun approach.
- He only works in English, since there is more money there. He sees Brazilian Portuguese and German as promising markets and notes Google hasn't hit parasites in other languages as hard. He believes Japanese and Korean publishers prefer local partners.
- He screens publishers by traffic history. If a site fell from about 1 million sessions a month to about 50k, he declines unless they accept adult content. His reason: he can only humanize a limited number of articles at once, so he won't produce hundreds of articles for a site that won't rank.
- He sets niche boundaries up front. His niches are mostly supplements and pharma (including peptides). He avoids casino and payday loans in partnerships, and asks whether publishers are open to OnlyFans content. In parasite tests he'll try anything.
- OnlyFans keywords are low competition, and he says real volumes can be 50-100% higher than tools show, sometimes up to 10x.

## Partnership deal terms and contracts

- Always use contracts. He says publishers who offer handshake deals will "screw you in a heartbeat" once it takes off.
- The split is always 50/50 with the publisher. The publisher gets a maximum of 50%, and there is no negotiation. If a publisher wants less for himself, he points them elsewhere, since an inexperienced operator "is gonna run your site to the ground".
- Where a third party makes an intro, he gets a 10% kickback and the publisher still gets 50%.
- Terms: Jacky's side owns the content it creates and gets two years of residuals. If the publisher terminates, they must either remove all the content or buy him out for two years of revenue. He calls this aggressive.
- Example: one top partnership hit $500k revenue a month within two months. The publisher was paying an agency $250k a month for content, grew resentful of the cost, and has been trying to get out since. Their own attempts to replicate it in another subdirectory didn't rank.

## Content production and avoiding detection

- For test pages he doesn't optimize. For pages he wants to rank, he humanizes and then re-optimizes.
- Humanizing: content must pass AI detector tests, then is optimized with Surfer or equivalent.
- Evidence: partnership sites he humanized and kept quiet about stayed fine, while sites where he went aggressive without humanizing all got hit. He doesn't publicly report the numbers of the surviving sites.
- On-page effort: heavy formatting, unique edited images, and E-E-A-T signals. Pages look like Forbes-style articles with "reviewed by" and "edited by" lines. He pays real people to sign off, using their names.
- Velocity matters. Sites where he blasted thousands of articles a month got hit quickly, while 50-100 a month stayed under the radar. He tested across more than 15 partnership sites.
- His guess (he says he doesn't know for sure): what matters is the ratio to the site's normal publishing rate. A site like TechCrunch could absorb 50 extra a day, while a newspaper posting one a day could not absorb thousands.
- Content cost is negligible. He uses Koala, with free credits as a large affiliate. The host mentioned a competing tool at 7-8 cents per 1,000 words, which Jacky did not claim as his own cost.

## Linking, maintenance and operations

- Internal linking is always done: "no-brainer, it's free".
- Backlinks only as needed. He found no relationship between link building and success. Some sites he built links to never flew, while several with zero links took off after about six months.
- He builds links retrospectively, only for pages already on positions 5-10 that he wants in the top 3. He doesn't start until a page is around page two.
- Partnerships can take months. Some sites began climbing after six months, so he warns that cutting stagnant sites early may be a mistake.
- Planning: a massive content calendar. He pushes harder on sites that perform and cuts back on stagnant ones.
- Stack: Google Sheets, VAs, Trello, Slack, and a master partnerships sheet. He tracks thousands of pages across about 15 publishers.
- Freshness: he runs a plugin that changes the publish date and the first paragraph of each article. He updates pages when new, higher-paying offers come in.
- Keyword strategy: about 50-60% different keywords across domains to avoid footprints. The core keywords are shared across sites. He targets low-KD (0-10 on Ahrefs) but high-volume supplement keywords.

## Monetization: brand deals and affiliate commissions

- He calls his model "retail rank and rent": brands pay him to be number one for a keyword, and he keeps a list of historical payers to re-approach.
- He asks for at least 40% commission on articles he ranks for.
- He prefers Amazon over custom affiliate programs like FanFuel or Sale Health at the same commission rate, since Amazon converts better and pays more overall. He notes those two companies sponsor him.
- He also promotes direct offers from manufacturers or retailers.
- Best payout: about $40k a month for being ranked number one plus 40% commission. He attributes it to VC-funded brands that don't care about cost.
- OnlyFans-adjacent traffic: he says some networks pay per click, so traffic doesn't need to convert.
