## Why Jacky says SEO suits private equity firms

- Jacky cites a stat that SEO-generated inbound leads have a 14.6% close rate, against 1.7% for outbound leads. The cited reason is that inbound leads come from people who initiated a call or navigated to a website, so they are actively shopping and intend to buy.
- His logic for private equity: potential investors search Google for relevant terms, so ranking for those searches puts the firm in front of them. A strong online presence makes a firm more likely to attract new investors and grow.
- SEO raises both the quantity and the quality of organic traffic, which comes from people searching specific keywords or phrases.
- He treats it as ongoing work: "SEO is an ongoing process," so monitor and adjust the strategy regularly to stay ahead of competitors.
- If a firm wants faster results, he suggests hiring an SEO company to save time.

## On-site SEO: keywords and content

- Keyword research comes first. Investors find a firm through the terms they search, so those terms must appear on the site.
- Tools he names: Google Keyword Planner, SEMrush and Ahrefs.
- He recommends focusing on long-tail and LSI (semantically related) keywords. Long-tail phrases are more specific, less competitive and more likely to convert into leads. LSI keywords help search engines understand the context of a page.
- Content should be high-quality, informative, relevant and kept up to date.
- Include target keywords but avoid keyword stuffing.
- Use headings and subheadings to break content up, and lists and bullets to make it easy to scan.

## On-site SEO: tags, URLs, sitemap, schema and mobile

- **Title tags:** include target keywords and keep them under 60 characters so they are not truncated.
- **Meta descriptions:** include target keywords, keep them under 155 characters, and make them compelling and clickable to lift click-through rate.
- **URLs:** clear and easy to understand, with target keywords, no special characters, and as short as possible.
- **XML sitemap:** list every page on the site, including pages not linked from elsewhere, so search engines can find, crawl and index them.
- **Navigation and schema markup:** clear navigation helps investors find information. Schema markup gives search engines extra detail about the firm and its services.
- **Mobile:** most people browse on mobile, so use a layout that adapts to screen sizes and make sure pages load quickly on phones.

## Off-site SEO: backlinks, social and reputation

- **Backlinks:** inbound links from other sites signal credibility and authority. Quality beats quantity: a few links from reputable, high-authority sites are better than many low-quality ones.
- Ways he lists to build them: creating high-quality content, guest blogging and broken link building.
- **Social media:** keep profiles on LinkedIn, Twitter, Instagram and Facebook, post regularly and engage followers to build relationships with potential investors and drive traffic to the site.
- **Online reputation management:** investors often research a firm online before investing. Search the firm's name on Google and social media regularly, respond to negative comments or reviews, address issues promptly, and create positive content.
- **Reviews:** encourage clients to leave reviews as social proof that builds credibility and trust and can improve visibility and rankings.

## Local SEO, Google My Business and directories

- Local SEO targets investors in a specific geography: use local keywords, create local business listings and build local reviews.
- Put the city and state in title tags, meta descriptions and content.
- **Google My Business:** a free tool for keeping name, address, phone number and hours accurate and consistent across the web. Add pictures and videos and respond to reviews.
- **Directories:** in addition to GMB, keep profiles on platforms like Yelp, Yellow Pages and BBB. They add visibility, help local SEO, build trust and bring in reviews.

## Measurement and technical SEO

- **Google Analytics:** shows visitor numbers, where they come from and how they interact with the site, so you can spot patterns and opportunities.
- **Google Search Console:** shows how the site performs in search, flags issues hurting visibility, and reveals which keywords people use to find it so content can be optimized for them.
- **Technical checklist:**
  - Mobile-friendly, via responsive design or a separate mobile version.
  - Fast loading on desktop and mobile: optimize images, compress files and minify HTML, JavaScript and CSS.
  - HTTPS to encrypt data between server and client.
  - An improved XML sitemap to help crawling and indexing.
  - A robots.txt file to keep search engines from crawling pages not relevant to the business.
