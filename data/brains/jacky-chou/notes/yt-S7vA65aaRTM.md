## Jacky's 90-day plan to reach $10k/month from zero

- Jacky says his best starting business for someone with no skills, network or audience is an agency. Knowing what he knows, he says he could get to $10k/month quickly with a couple of high-ticket sales. His own example is an LLM agency.
- He claims that anyone who sticks to the plan for 90 days will get there. His daily "massive lock in":
  - Cold email every day.
  - Cold call every day.
  - Two TikToks, two Instagram Reels, one long-form video.
  - One post on X.
- Offer: it must be high ticket and a "no-brainer", meaning zero risk. At the start he would use performance-based terms ("if you don't hit these parameters, you don't pay").
- He suggests working a McDonald's shift to pay for tools while starting out. He says one shift a week would cover them.
- His outlook for an average-IQ person doing this by brute force: mid four figures in 90 days, and it could replace a full-time salary. A smarter person who knows what they're doing can reach $10k/month.
- His caveat: cold calling US buyers from a country where an accent makes it harder is rough. He says Canada works.
- He says "no one's going to do this", so it takes someone very hungry.

## From agency to SaaS: the playbook he repeats

- His SaaS pivot came from his daily YouTube videos. He made local SEO videos for about a year and spotted holes in the local SEO software market.
- Cursor's arrival made building easy. With business partner Peter Wang he launched Local Rank, and they now launch SaaS companies repeatedly with the same playbook.
- The playbook:
  - Build deep knowledge of an industry (local SEO, Reddit marketing, getting mentioned in LLMs).
  - Notice the problems you hit yourself. He says about 90% of the time other people have them too.
  - Solve them with software.
  - Talk about those problems publicly and market to your viewers.
- Stay in B2B, and sell software for something you could sell as a service.
- He speaks the language of agency owners because he is one. Agency owners have high LTV and heavy switching costs. His Indexsy customer LTV is about $1,000.
- Agency vs SaaS:
  - An agency is painful to manage but one of the easiest businesses to start. You tell the team to do something and it gets done.
  - SaaS has a never-ending feature list, but margins are far better than an agency's.
- In his November 2025 revenue, much of the software revenue did not exist a year earlier. It all arrived within 12 months of his pivot away from affiliate.

## Ranking in ChatGPT and AI search

- Jacky says he was cited in ChatGPT within 24 hours and ranked number one in 10 days.
- His SaaS tactic for ChatGPT: get about 20 founder friends to each write a "best [category] 2026" listicle on their blogs and put you at number one. Consistently showing up first is a strong positive signal to LLMs. His line: "2026 is the year of the listicle."
- AI Mode on Google probably needs real Google rankings, since it uses Google's ranking. ChatGPT is the opposite. He cites a study of about 35k cited URLs in which roughly 50% of cited domains had zero traffic.
- He calls these the "golden years" of LLM citations because they are easy to game.
- ChatGPT cites Reddit heavily, so getting onto Reddit also helps with LLM visibility.
- He believes a YouTube "best X" video would likely work for Gemini and AI Mode. He is unsure about ChatGPT.
- Where SEO still pays: industries where ChatGPT and AI Mode won't give answers, such as casino, CBD, drugs and adult. He says the money there is still about 90% of what it was.
- Consumer queries like "best protein powder" are already hit hard by AI answers. He says SEO is now shifting toward LLMs.

## Reddit playbook for promoting a SaaS

- Jacky calls Reddit the easiest channel to game right now, for both Google rankings and LLM visibility.
- Step by step:
  1. Search your target keyword on Google (for example "best local rank tracker"). He says Reddit shows up about 90% of the time.
  2. Comment your brand in that Reddit thread.
  3. Wait two weeks, then buy upvotes. Traffic starts almost immediately.
  4. If the thread is locked, post the same question in the same subreddit ("what's the best local rank tracker in 2026?") with your parameters, without shilling yet.
  5. Buy around 500 upvotes to make it viral and push it to the front page. It can replace the old ranking thread.
  6. Edit the post to say you found your own tool as the best option.
- Upvotes change the narrative. A post with early hate can turn positive once it has upvotes, because Reddit users follow the crowd.
- Prices: about 10 upvotes for 10 cents, sold at 1 cent each. His own company sells them through a business called Trackings, and other providers exist.
- He suggests using one account to promote your own services.

## Google ranking and local SEO signals

- Google uses Chrome data. If a page ranks between positions 5 and 20, sending a large amount of real human traffic can lift it. His example: about 50k visits from Facebook.
- If you stop paying for traffic, the page stays up only if people still click it naturally. If users bounce back to search results ("pogo stick"), the ranking falls.
- Google Maps ranking is mostly user signals now. In his order of importance:
  1. User signals such as Google reviews and driving directions (also click-through rate).
  2. Citations, meaning directory listings, which are like link building.
  3. Everything else.
- With two otherwise identical listings and the same review count, he says the one with 150 daily visitors outranks the one with 50.
- Competitors get real people to visit locations by partnering with big mobile games and apps. The user walks to the business and gets in-game currency. He says he lacks the technical skill to build this but thinks it's an eight-figure business if someone cracks it.

## Diversification, exits and surviving volatility

- Jacky worked in high-risk, high-volatility industries (SEO, affiliate), so he is a strong believer in taking money off the table.
- His revenue went from about $500k/month to roughly $150-200k in about three months, a hit of about 60%. He says it forced team reorganizing, projections and painful cost cutting, down to going through credit card statements.
- Dropshipping story: it reached $240k/month at 40% margins. Because they stayed on dropshipping instead of moving to private label, Facebook changes plus iOS cut it to about $100k/month at 5-10% margins. He sold to private equity for about $150k, received about $100k, and the buyer went bankrupt.
- He has had three or four low-six-figure exits. The biggest was about $300-350k, a content site sold to Thrasio after buying it for about $150k and holding it for two months.
- He says 99% of content-site peers from the COVID era are "completely destroyed" and down about 90% in enterprise value. He says they didn't sell, and he had personally urged several to do so.
- His residual income lines:
  - Binance affiliate: in 2017 he outranked Binance for its own name for about three months, because its homepage lacked title tags.
  - OnlyFans: traffic sent to a network that works with agencies.
  - Mediavine: residual from partnership SEO, where he partnered with legacy media brands, wrote affiliate articles on their blogs and split revenue 50/50.
  - FanFuel: an affiliate network.
- Partnership SEO took him from about $70-80k/month to $500k in about three months. His lesson: "If you see an opportunity, you should like really really go in hard." He rejects "passive income" as a myth.

## YouTube as a growth channel

- Jacky started daily videos in 2023 with about 20K followers on X. The first video got roughly 100 views.
- Differentiation: other creators in the space did highly edited quick cuts, so he made slow, unedited, long videos. He credits consistency over execution.
- Average views are about 1,000, mostly US-based viewers, many of them agency owners with buying power. He says the same thousand people returning daily is worth more than going viral.
- Prep time dropped from about 2 hours for the first episode to about 20 minutes.
- For the Local Rank launch he did $20k right out of the gate. About 90% of that revenue came from YouTube, tracked via coupon codes. The launch video had about 1K views, and the launch tweet on X had about 50k views but drove only about 10% of revenue.
- His advice to anyone struggling: start posting videos, whatever kind. He calls video "by far the biggest lever".

## Conversion, team and operations

- VSLs: Jacky says he thought they were "scammy" but A/B tested with and without, and having one helps. He thinks they work especially well for North American buyers.
  - His is about 2 minutes of him selling and showing the product.
  - He doesn't center it on the page. He puts a headline with the top of the VSL visible.
  - He says viewers who watch about 90% convert at around 60%, though he'd need to pull the exact numbers.
  - He writes the script with ChatGPT.
- SaaS landing pages: copy someone doing a lot of revenue, since most converge on what works. He wouldn't copy Peter Levels' page, which he says isn't optimized and sells on personal brand.
- Team and delegation:
  - He has about 13 people on his team, up one or two from the year before.
  - He delegates anything he has to do more than once.
  - Delegated work will never meet your standards, so be OK with "good enough".
- Community is the business he'd keep: Jacky is bullish on his Slack-style community (he mentions "advice"). He says it's the easiest, and he talks daily with like-minded marketers.
- Reality check: November 2025 revenue of $374k is gross. He says net is about 40%, and take-home after taxes is less. Net worth is about $10 million after about 10 years, and he lives humbly.
