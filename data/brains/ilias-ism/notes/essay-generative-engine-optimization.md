## What GEO is and how it differs from SEO

Ilias defines Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) as optimizing content to get cited by AI search engines such as ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude and Google AI Overviews.

- Traditional SEO optimizes for keyword-matching algorithms and ranking among 10 blue links, with backlinks as the main authority signal.
- GEO optimizes for AI language understanding and targets citations in AI-generated answers. AI engines cite roughly 3-5 sources per response, so the competition is for a handful of mentions.
- His framing: "Instead of fighting for search rankings, you now present information to be selected as a source with credibility."
- He argues that if content isn't optimized for this, you become invisible, because the AI writes one synthesized answer instead of listing links.
- He stresses that GEO builds on SEO and doesn't replace it.

## Why GEO matters for a business

Ilias says ChatGPT alone drives traffic to over 100,000 websites monthly.

- Evidence he cites: Vercel gets 10% of new signups from ChatGPT citations, and companies report 30-40% visibility improvements with proper optimization.
- AI search sessions run about 6 minutes, versus quick bounces on traditional search.
- Purchases can happen inside the AI interface, for example Perplexity's "Buy with Pro" one-click purchase.
- He argues that being the top citation in a generative answer can beat ranking #1 in Google for credibility and conversion.

## The three types of generative engines and how to approach each

Ilias splits generative engines into three types, each needing a different approach.

**Google AI Overviews**
- They rely on the same crawling and indexing infrastructure as regular search, so master traditional SEO first. If Google can't find and understand the content conventionally, the AI Overview can't access it either.
- Needs: clean title tags, meta descriptions and structured data; a clear H2/H3 hierarchy; 2-3 sentence concise answers to the query; bullets and numbered lists.

**AI search engines (ChatGPT, Perplexity)**
- They run real-time searches on Google or Bing, aggregate top-ranking pages, rewrite them into a conversational answer, and attribute sources.
- Strategy: keep strong traditional rankings in Google and Bing, use headers and bullets the AI can parse, put the direct answer or definition in the first 2-3 sentences, and bold key terms.
- His conclusion: AI systems cite content that ranks well in traditional search.

**Training-data models (Claude, Llama, DeepSeek)**
- These remember brands that appeared most often on trusted sites while being trained.
- Strategy: publish regularly on news sites, industry blogs and trusted platforms; get mentioned in forums, reviews, podcasts and social media; think in years, not months. "This is a marathon, not a sprint."

## What the Princeton study found

Ilias leans on the first systematic GEO study from Princeton researchers: 10,000 queries across nine domains, testing nine optimization strategies. Some tactics delivered up to 40% improvement in visibility in AI responses.

Which strategy works depends on content type:
- Debate/opinion content: an authoritative tone increased visibility.
- Factual content: statistics and citations dominated.
- Historical content: quotations performed best.
- Business content: fluency optimization showed strong results.

His takeaway: one-size-fits-all doesn't work, so tailor the strategy to the content type.

## Seven GEO tactics ranked by impact

Ilias ranks these by measured improvement:

1. **Add compelling statistics (39.5%).** Turn "Many marketers struggle with content creation" into "87% of marketers report struggling with content creation, according to recent industry surveys." Bold the numbers, name the source, refresh regularly. If none exist, create them through surveys or analysis of your own data.
2. **Integrate credible citations (40.4%).** Every major claim gets a source, for example Gartner predicting a 25% drop in traditional search volume by 2026. Mix primary and secondary sources, keep them recent, and use inline "(Source, Year)" style.
3. **Include strategic quotations (38.1%).** Interview experts or pull quotes from conferences and podcasts. Attribute every quote. "Original quotes > recycled quotes every time."
4. **Optimize for fluency (35.2%).** Paragraphs of 2-4 sentences, simple active voice, 3-5 bolded key phrases per section, a subheading every 150-200 words, roughly 8th-grade reading level.
5. **Add technical terms (32.7%).** Define terms on first use, use them consistently, and consider a glossary for complex topics.
6. **Write authoritatively (30.1%).** Use definitive statements, avoid "might," "maybe" and "possibly," take clear positions, and back claims with evidence.
7. **Simplify complex ideas (15-30%).** Use analogies, break processes into steps, create visual hierarchy, and summarize in bullets.

## His daily 10-minute GEO tracking and outreach routine

Ilias says you don't need expensive tools to track GEO. His routine for StoryChief:

- Every morning he spends 10 minutes asking ChatGPT and Perplexity money queries such as "What are the best content marketing tools?"
- He keeps a list of money queries on his phone (for example "best content marketing tools," "content management platforms," "social media scheduling software") and rotates through variations daily.
- When he sees listicles being cited, like "15 Best Content Marketing Platforms in 2025," he visits the article, finds the author's email with LinkDR, and pitches them.
- His pitch: "I saw your article was cited by AI platforms. I'm the [role] of StoryChief. Would love to get included in your next update. Happy to offer $500 for the mention, set up our affiliate program for you, or do a guest post exchange."
- Outreach works because it leads with "your article is being cited by AI" and offers real value. One publisher replied with rates of $300 per post or $200 per link insertion, do-follow.
- When he finds weak listicles getting AI citations, he writes better ones and reaches out to the same sites that linked to the weak ones.
- His framing: "I'm not begging for links. I'm offering to pay for placement in content that AI already trusts."
- He recommends starting manually to understand what works, then using AISEOTRACKER to find opportunities at scale and monitor GEO performance.
