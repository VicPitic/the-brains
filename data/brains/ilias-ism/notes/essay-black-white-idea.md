## The Black & White idea test: catch attention with a binary under 60 characters

- Ilias, writing about a framework from Wono (the creator behind the viral "Mrs Watanabe" YouTube video), treats content as attention management in four steps: catch, hook, retain, convert.
- Catch: grab attention first, because the more you catch, the more you can funnel into later steps.
- Hook: engage the viewer so they want to keep consuming.
- Retain: keep them engaged for as long as possible.
- Convert: turn that attention into an action such as a like, share or subscription.
- The framework's point is that execution matters as much as the idea: even a remarkable idea falls flat without a plan for managing attention.

## Layered ideas vs Black & White ideas

- The framework says to focus on the ingredients of an idea, not the idea itself.
- There are bad ideas and good ideas. Good ideas split into two types.
- Layered ideas are complex and multi-dimensional, and hard to convey in simple words. They are good at retaining attention but weak at catching it.
- Black & White ideas can be broken into a binary format in under 60 characters. Their concise nature makes them strong at catching attention.
- Their simplicity is the strength: they can be understood in a few words without losing substance.
- The binary shape sparks curiosity about the nuance behind it, and that curiosity carries attention into the hook, retain and convert steps.

## How to build a Black & White idea (the Mrs Watanabe example)

- Start by having ChatGPT write descriptions around your topic, then ask it for a black and white title.
- Break the title down into smaller parts, keeping only the essence of the story.
- The resulting title: "How Japanese Housewives Outsmarted Global Finance".
- Under 60 characters (also the ideal SEO title length) is the signal that you have something to work with.
- The core is two counterpart thoughts inside one title:
  - Japanese vs Global
  - Housewives vs Finance
- Wono's example: a recording of a popular streamer reading the title aloud. The streamer's tone on "How Japanese..." shows the "catch attention" moment, then he opens the video with his fans and watches it fully (hook, then retain).

## Why the binary title works: tension in the reader's mind

- Ilias says the framework creates "conflict and tension in the mind" of the reader, which leaves them intrigued and hooked. He links this to the Ovsiankina effect (the urge to finish an unresolved task).
- Catching and hooking attention is only the front half. After the title lands, the content still has to retain and convert that attention.
