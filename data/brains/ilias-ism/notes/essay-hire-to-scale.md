## Why Ilias hires a team instead of doing the work himself

Ilias argues that a business owner who does everything personally is the business, and that this caps growth and freedom. He uses a story from his local neighborhood to make the point:

- A ramen shop owner opened a second location and spends busy Friday nights out with friends, because newly hired staff run the kitchen and serve customers.
- The sushi restaurant owners across the street never hired anyone, to save on expenses. They cook, serve and clean themselves, rarely take holidays, work weekends, and close the restaurant when they are sick.
- Ilias's summary of the sushi owners: "They are the business!"
- He notes the ramen chef's fame comes from press coverage (his digital PR tells a story of the chef having "traveled to Japan" to perfect his recipes), which draws visitors from across the city. Fame alone didn't scale it; hiring a team did.

## Ilias's own situation at MagicSpace SEO

Ilias applies the lesson to his SEO agency in Switzerland, MagicSpace SEO.

- Right now it is just him and a co-founder, both doing client work themselves. His words: "We are the SEO agency!"
- His typical day covers content creation, keyword research, guest posting, link building and finding new clients.
- All of that serves one goal: improving website authority and boosting his clients' search positions.
- He concludes he can't do everything himself forever. As clients grow, he needs to hire more people to serve them, or he risks losing them.

## Scale by documenting processes as SOPs

Ilias's key to scaling without losing quality is building standardized systems that future team members can replicate. He plans to turn his own way of working into standard operating procedures (SOPs), a blueprint of the agency's approach. Areas he lists:

- Using SEO tools correctly.
- Keywords: content gaps, topical maps, structure, content calendar.
- Writing: content briefs, content creation, editing, publishing.
- Link building: guest posting, handling payment, cold email outreach.
- Reporting: creating reports, sending them to clients, explaining them.
- Client management: onboarding clients, handling client requests.

## Why SOPs matter

Ilias lists four benefits of documenting the processes:

- Smooth onboarding of new team members.
- Consistency even as the agency scales.
- Flexibility for him to step away at times.
- Higher business valuation if he ever wants to sell.

He frames putting these in place early as laying "the groundwork for scalable and sustainable growth."

## Teams and processes raise exit value

Ilias says this approach is why his previous B2B SaaS startup (he is an exited founder of Officient) sold for a great price.

- He had built a team and processes that let him step away from the business and work on other things.
- The acquirer was willing to pay a premium for that.

## The risk of not building a team

If he doesn't do this, Ilias says he'll be stuck like the sushi owners, still doing everything himself even as the business grows. His advice to any business owner: think about how to scale operations so you don't have to be there all the time and can focus on the things you enjoy most.
