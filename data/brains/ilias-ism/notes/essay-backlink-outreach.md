## How Ilias gets 15+ backlinks a month with outreach

- Ilias treats backlinks as the core of SEO success. His agency, MagicSpace SEO, consistently lands 15+ new backlinks every month by combining manual outreach with AI tooling.
- He cites three reasons backlinks matter: they are a key ranking factor (Google reads them as a signal of authority and relevance), they drive targeted referral traffic, and they build brand awareness and credibility.
- He considers manual outreach one of the most effective link building methods because it lets you target highly relevant sites, build genuine relationships with site owners, and secure contextual editorial links, "the most valuable kind."

## The five-step outreach process

1. **Keywords and competitors.** Decide which keywords to rank for and which pages to build links to. Ilias uses LinkDR to find keywords, then Ahrefs to find the top 10 competitors per keyword. He exports the keyword and competitor backlink lists to CSV and imports them into LinkDR.
2. **Start an email campaign in LinkDR.** It finds prospects, generates personalized emails and tracks results. It includes an email finder, so no manual contact hunting. It filters prospects by criteria such as excluding competitor sites, low-DA sites, and sites outside the niche.
3. **Craft the outreach email.** See the principles and template below.
4. **Follow up strategically.** See the sequence below.
5. **Track and analyze.** He tracks open rates, reply rates, links acquired and time to conversion, then uses the data to find the best-performing templates and tactics.

## Email principles and template

His four principles:
- **Personalize:** use the recipient's name and reference something specific about their site.
- **Provide value:** explain how the content benefits them and their audience.
- **Keep it concise:** get to the point quickly.
- **Include a clear CTA:** make the next step easy.

His current template: subject "Quick question about [their site]". It opens with who he is (MagicSpace SEO) and a specific compliment on the recipient's article. It then pitches a guide on a related topic with three bullet points of what it covers. It closes by asking if they'd like to check it out and offering to "write a custom intro if you'd like to share it."

## Follow-up sequence

- He sends 2-3 follow-ups spaced 5-7 days apart, each from a slightly different angle: a gentle reminder, an added value proposition, then a final "break up" email.
- Follow-up #1 (5 days later, "Re:" the original subject) restates the topic and highlights three key stats from the guide that fit the recipient's audience, then offers the full article.
- Follow-up #2 (7 days later, subject "One last thing re: [topic] resource") is a last attempt that says no worries if it's not a fit and he won't bother them again. It sweetens the offer with an exclusive quote for readers, a custom graphic to accompany the link, and sharing the post across social channels (10k+ followers).

## Best practices

- Focus on quality over quantity: a few relevant, high-quality links beat dozens of low-quality ones.
- Offer genuine value by thinking about how the other site and its audience benefit.
- Build relationships, not just links; aim for long-term partnerships over one-off swaps.
- Be persistent but respectful: follow up, but accept a no.
- Keep testing and optimizing based on results.

## Case study: 10 links for Xnapper in 30 days

Xnapper is a screenshot tool for Mac. The goal was high-quality backlinks to boost its SEO.

**Strategy:**
- Identified 100 relevant tech bloggers, productivity experts and Mac software reviewers.
- Used a personalized template highlighting Xnapper's unique features.
- Offered a free pro license to sites that reviewed and linked to Xnapper.
- Ran a 2-email follow-up sequence.
- Managed outreach manually to keep quality high.

**Results:**
- 100 emails sent, 25 replies (25% reply rate), 10 links acquired (10% conversion).
- Average Domain Rating of acquired links was 50+.

**Takeaways:** personalization was crucial for busy tech bloggers; the free pro license was an effective incentive for reviews; follow-ups mattered, since 30% of links came after the initial email; and manual outreach allowed better customization per prospect.

## Other link building strategies

Ilias lists these alongside outreach:
- **Industry directories and lists:** niche directories and curated resource lists (he points to a list of directories for submitting a tool).
- **Social media and communities:** joining relevant Facebook groups, subreddits and other communities to find site owners and influencers.
- **Guest posting:** builds contextual links, establishes authority and expands audience; he scales it with AI.
- **Broken link building:** find broken links on other sites and suggest your content as the replacement; Ahrefs helps find opportunities.
- **Resource page link building:** find resource pages in your industry and suggest your content.
- **Digital PR and newsjacking:** create newsworthy content or tie expertise to current events to win links from news sites and publications.
- **Skyscraper technique:** find popular content, make something better, then pitch the sites linking to the original (popularized by Brian Dean).
