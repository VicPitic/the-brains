## Why SEO matters for SaaS startups

Ilias treats SEO as the most cost-effective, compounding channel for early-stage SaaS. He gives five reasons to start early:
- **Cheap acquisition.** Compared to paid ads, SEO gives affordable, consistent traffic and leads over the long term, which matters for startups with small budgets.
- **Compounding growth.** Results build as authority and rankings accumulate. Within 6-12 months, SEO "can become your top channel for new signups."
- **Brand awareness and credibility.** Ranking at the top for relevant keywords makes the brand visible and positions it as a leader in its space.
- **Market insight.** Keyword research shows the problems customers are trying to solve and the solutions they search for, which feeds product development and marketing.
- **Boosts other marketing.** High-ranking SEO content can be repurposed for social, email and sales enablement.

## Start with audience and goals

Ilias says every SEO strategy starts with a deep understanding of the target customer: ideal users, use cases, pain points, jobs to be done, and the solutions they search for.
- Build detailed buyer personas and map the customer journey and funnel, from problem awareness to solution research to vendor evaluation and selection.
- Use that map to decide which keywords to target and which content to create at each stage.
- The ultimate goal is usually revenue growth, but he tracks leading indicators too: organic traffic, keyword rankings, backlinks and signups.
- Pick 3-5 core metrics, set "aggressive but realistic" targets, and get stakeholder buy-in on them.

## Keyword research process

Ilias's keyword research is a repeatable sequence:
1. Brainstorm an initial list around the product's core features and benefits, from the ideal customer's point of view ("What would you search for?").
2. Expand it with Ahrefs, SEMrush and Google Keyword Planner to get search volume and difficulty. Look for relevant keywords with high volume but low to medium competition.
3. Go after long-tail keywords tied to specific use cases, features and pain points, as well as "head" keywords that define the product category. Long-tail terms have lower volume individually, but "they can really add up."
4. Categorize keywords by intent (informational, navigational, commercial investigation, transactional) and match each to a funnel stage.
5. Do competitor research: analyze what top competitors target to find gaps. Ahrefs shows any site's top organic keywords.

## Content strategy and on-page SEO

Ilias maps the keyword list to a content plan with these page types:
- **Product and feature pages** for the core product, key features and major use cases, optimized for the most important commercial keywords.
- **Blog posts and articles** for informational, top-of-funnel keywords, to attract the audience and build authority.
- **Guides and tutorials** for "commercial investigation" keywords, helping buyers understand how to solve their problem.
- **Case studies and proof** for bottom-of-funnel searches, showing customers and data-driven results.
- **Comparisons and reviews** targeting "[your product] vs [competitor]" and "[your product] reviews," written honestly and objectively.

Prioritize by keyword priority and keep a calendar. His cadence target is at least 1-2 new optimized pieces per week.

On-page checklist:
- Target keyword in the title, meta description, URL and H1.
- Short, descriptive URLs.
- Title tag and meta description written for clicks.
- Keyword and variations used naturally in the copy, with keyword-rich headings and subheadings.
- Descriptive image alt text.
- Internal links to relevant pages.
- Better page speed and mobile friendliness.
- Yoast SEO can help optimize individual pages, and existing content should get an SEO audit and updates.

## Link building for SaaS

Ilias calls backlinks from authoritative sites a key ranking factor, "votes of confidence." Quality beats quantity: "A few high-value links will do more for your SEO than dozens of low-quality ones." He avoids spammy tactics that risk penalties.

Tactics he recommends:
- **Guest blogging** on respected publications in the space, with a natural link in the author bio or body.
- **Digital PR:** pitch newsworthy data and stories to journalists, who often link back to the source.
- **Broken link building:** find broken links on relevant sites and ask them to swap in a link to your content.
- **Partnerships and co-marketing:** co-branded content, research, tools or events, with both sides promoting and linking.
- **Original research and data:** studies, surveys and data reports "tend to earn a lot of natural backlinks."

## Technical SEO checklist

Ilias lists these technical priorities for SaaS sites:
- **Site architecture:** a logical, flat structure where every important page is reachable within 3 clicks of the homepage.
- **Navigation and internal linking:** clear menus, keyword-rich anchor text, and a sitemap.
- **Duplicate content:** consolidate with 301 redirects, rel=canonical tags or parameter handling.
- **Site speed:** optimize code, use caching, compress images, use a CDN.
- **Mobile:** responsive design, since Google uses mobile-first indexing.
- **Structured data:** Schema markup to help engines understand content and enable rich snippets that improve click-through.
- **HTTPS:** SSL certificate and migration, a small ranking factor and important for security.

Audit with Google Search Console, Screaming Frog and DeepCrawl, and fix anything hindering indexation first.

## Promote content, then measure and iterate

Ilias says optimized content is "only half the battle"; it also needs promotion to earn engagement, social signals and backlinks.
- Share across social channels, both organically and with paid promotion, and engage in communities such as Reddit, Quora and Facebook/LinkedIn groups.
- Build an email list and send new content to subscribers, encouraging shares, comments and links.
- Reach out directly to influencers and publications; a mention from a prominent figure can amplify reach.
- Repurpose content into videos, infographics and slide decks for additional channels.

Measurement loop:
- Track keywords, organic traffic growth, backlink profile and conversions in Google Analytics, Search Console and Ahrefs. On-page metrics to watch: bounce rate, time on page and conversions.
- Update, expand or repurpose top-performing content, and find related terms to rank for.
- Run regular technical audits and monitor speed, mobile friendliness and crawlability.
- Reinvest the extra revenue into more optimized content and more high-quality backlinks.
- He calls SEO "a long-term, iterative process," not an overnight result.

## SaaS SEO case studies

Ilias uses three companies as proof that a comprehensive SEO strategy drives SaaS growth:
- **Zapier:** comprehensive guides and tutorials on automation and productivity, long-tail keywords for connecting different apps, a free tool that recommends Zaps based on your tech stack, and backlinks from Forbes and Entrepreneur. He cites an estimated 1.8M organic visits per month and rankings for over 400,000 keywords.
- **Ahrefs:** in-depth data-driven posts and guides, free tools (backlink checker, website authority checker), influencer roundups and interviews, and promotion to 200k+ email subscribers and social followers. He cites over 500k monthly organic visits and top-100 rankings for terms like "SEO" and "backlinks."
- **Intercom:** content for every funnel stage, with educational blog content for informational keywords, product pages, guides and case studies for commercial keywords, a large library of video and webinars, and guest posts and partnerships with SaaS blogs. He says organic traffic has grown steadily year over year and drives a significant share of new signups.
