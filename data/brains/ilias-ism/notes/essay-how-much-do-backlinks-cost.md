## Backlink pricing by Domain Rating (2026)

- Managed backlinks from a link building service cost about $150 to $500 each.
- Ilias's LinkDR per-link prices:
  - DR 30+ (100-10k visits/mo): $160
  - DR 40+ (500-20k visits/mo): $280
  - DR 50+ (1k-40k visits/mo): $400
  - DR 60+ (1k-80k visits/mo): $500
- Each price includes finding the publisher, outreach, a standard article, and the publisher's fee.
- Large vendors' public price lists sit in the same range: roughly $130 to $160 for DR 30+ and about $500 for DR 60+.
- Publishers themselves ask a median of $100 for a guest post on a DR 30+ site, based on 10,030 sites in LinkDR's link building statistics.
- The managed premium over that $100 covers finding and vetting the site, outreach, writing the article, paying up front, and replacing links that disappear.
- Ilias's rule: price rises with Domain Rating and real traffic. A site with real readers is worth more than a high-DR site with none.
- Data point: 43% of DR 30+ sites get under 1,000 visits a month.

## What drives backlink prices

Ilias lists seven drivers:

1. **Domain Rating.** Higher DR sites have more links of their own and charge more.
2. **Organic traffic.** Real readers matter more than a high DR number.
3. **Niche.** Casino, crypto, CBD, and finance links cost much more because fewer sites accept them.
4. **Content type.** A listicle or product review usually costs more than a standard article. At LinkDR it adds 20%.
5. **Link type.** A link added to an existing article (a niche edit) is sometimes cheaper. In LinkDR's data, 38% of publishers charge less for it and 49% charge the same.
6. **Outreach method.** Custom outreach to relevant sites costs more than picking from a list of sites that sell to everyone.
7. **Approval.** Reviewing each site before placement takes extra work. At LinkDR it adds 20%.

## Pricing models for link building

Ilias describes four models:

- **Per link:** pay for each placed link. Easy to compare and budget. This is LinkDR's model: pick a DR tier and a quantity, and see the total before paying.
- **Packages:** a fixed bundle, such as ten links at mixed DR, for one price.
- **Monthly retainers:** a set budget each month for ongoing link building.
- **Pay per result:** pay only for live links. He advises checking what "live" means and for how long.

## True cost of doing link building yourself

- Buying the link is only part of the cost. You also find the site, pitch it, negotiate, write an article, and check the link.
- His example: if one link takes five hours and your time is worth $50 an hour, that is $250 before the publisher's fee.
- This is why he says many teams outsource link building even when they could do it themselves.

## Red flags in cheap backlinks

- **Hundreds of links for under $100.** "Real publishers do not work for $1 a link."
- **"Guaranteed DR 70+ links for $20."** DR can be inflated with spam links.
- **No site list or sample URLs.** You cannot judge what you cannot see.
- **The same sites for every niche.** These sites sell to everyone.
- **Private blog networks (PBNs).** Built only to pass links, and Google targets them.
- His bottom line: a cheap link that Google ignores is not cheap, and a link on a spam network can hurt you.

## How to budget for backlinks

1. **Find your gap.** Count the referring domains of the top three pages for your keyword, and use a backlink checker to see who links to competitors. Budget from the link gap between you and competitors.
2. **Pick a mix.** Most campaigns use mostly DR 30+ and DR 40+ links, plus a few stronger ones for key pages.
3. **Spread it out.** A steady pace each month looks natural and is easier to measure.
