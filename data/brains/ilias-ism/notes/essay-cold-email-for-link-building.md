## Cold-email domain setup: separate domains, warmup, and mailbox structure

- Ilias says not to use the primary business domain for cold email outreach. Buy a lookalike domain instead, e.g. "try(name).com".
- Any fresh outreach domain needs warmup so emails land in the inbox, not spam/junk. Warmup means using the mailbox like a normal person: sending and receiving emails, subscribing to industry-relevant newsletters, signing up for some websites.
- Because manual warmup takes time, he recommends an email warmup tool. Before that, set up DNS records properly: SPF, DKIM, DMARC and MX.
- He recommends Google Workspace or Office365 for the best deliverability chances.
- Standard is 3 mailboxes per domain, with US-sounding names (e.g. "James Williams" as `james.w@(domain).com`).
- Give each mailbox a basic signature (name, title, company name) and forward everything it receives to your normal email address (Gmail: Settings > Forwarding and POP/IMAP > Add a forwarding address; Outlook 365: Settings > Mail > Forwarding > Enable forwarding, optionally keep a copy).

## Link-building cold email: what makes the email work

- Link to your assets and theirs. Nobody wants to search for your site, so include a plain link to it (homepage, landing page, tool or blog post).
- One campaign opens with a short line like "you missed {our name} in your {post}". Both parts are hyperlinked: your name to your relevant content, their post to their page.
- A strong incentive is "key for any campaign". Examples: access to your affiliate program, free access to your platform or service, and offering assets they may need (images, screenshots, testimonials).
- Simplicity boosts engagement and open rates, makes replying easy, and clearly shows your value proposition.
- Subject lines: short and catchy, 5-7 words max. He says short subject lines get "2x higher open rates than long, detailed ones".
- Body: concise and focused. Use hyperlinks to reference pages, and include a link to schedule a call if needed.
- Use plain text, prepare follow-up emails in advance, and keep emails simple and personalized.

## Showing value to the prospect

- The success of outreach hinges on showing how you benefit the prospect or their readers. Ilias's checklist:
  - Is your content useful for their audience?
  - Do you have a relevant, linkable asset?
  - Is your guest blog idea unique and engaging?
- Use tools like Ahrefs or SEMrush to find broken backlinks or outdated references on their site. Offering to fix these adds immediate value.

## Mistakes to avoid in outreach emails

- Unoptimized subject lines: test emails to catch input codes or reference numbers leaking in.
- Broken variables: check personalization fields like `{{firstName}}` actually render.
- Grammar errors: proofread thoroughly, especially the subject line.
- Generic greetings: avoid "Dear Sir/Madam" or "To Whom It May Concern".
- Bad introductions: skip the "My name is... I'm the content manager at..." opener.
- Instead, grab attention with humor, a genuine compliment, or a simple question.
