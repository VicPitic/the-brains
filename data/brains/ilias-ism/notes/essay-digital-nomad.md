## Early life and the case for freelancing

- Ilias started at 18, fresh out of a Belgian school, with graphic design and web dev skills. He found a typical 9-5 back home unappealing and went remote instead.
- He freelanced in graphic design and web development, which let him work from anywhere (cafes in Chiang Mai, coworking in Bali, a balcony in Barcelona).
- He frames the goal as "collecting experiences, not business cards", and says the lifestyle is great for meeting people, learning cultures and juggling time zones.

## From freelancing to founding Officient

- Freelancing was fun, but he wanted to "run my own online show", so he moved into startups.
- His first company was Officient, run from a workspace in Ghent. Three years in it had a strong team and a lively office.
- Along the way he built open-source tools with Vue.js, React and Next.js. Those ventures taught him social media and how brutal debugging can be.
- His view: there is always more to learn however hard you hustle. He decided the nomad lifestyle was the real prize, more than the startup game.
- He is now an exited founder of Officient, building MagicSpace SEO, LinkDR, AI SEO Tracker and GenPPT.

## Four things to sort out before becoming a nomad

1. **Steady cash flow.** Online jobs, freelancing or your own business all work, but income must be reliable. Keep an emergency fund for unexpected hiccups.
2. **In-demand online skills.** Graphic design, web development, content creation, digital marketing, virtual assisting. Thriving depends on skills that are hot online.
3. **Travel and health insurance.** Solid coverage saves a lot of hassle abroad.
4. **Adaptability.** He compares the lifestyle to improv comedy: time zone shifts and income swings require flexibility.

## Practical problems he learned to handle

- Bad Wi-Fi is the classic failure. His fixes: find a better cafe, get a faster VPN, or befriend a local with the password.
- He had to explain complex designs to clients over laggy calls.
- He also mastered tourist visas, health insurance, foreign bank accounts and public transit in new cities.

## Covid and the shift to remote work

- When Covid hit, Officient went fully remote: the office became Zoom squares and coffee breaks became Slack emojis.
- Ilias used the new freedom to travel: Barcelona, Lisbon, the Douro Valley in Porto, and a few months in the Canary Islands where he learned to kitesurf.

## Where he lived afterward

- **Paris:** about a year, working remotely from a small flat with an Eiffel Tower view while polishing his French. It taught him foreign banking, insurance and transit.
- **Canary Islands:** moved on a digital nomad visa and lived in a coliving space, where he met remote workers from many countries.
- **Switzerland:** worked amid the mountains and wrote blog posts about running a remote business. His takeaway: nomad life "isn't a sprint; it's a marathon". It means creating a home base in each place and adapting, not just finding the best cafes.

## Community and the nomad mindset

- Location independence "doesn't mean being a lone wolf". Facebook groups, coworking spaces and cafe meetups give a worldwide network of remote workers who swap tips on workspaces and insurance problems.
- His four mindset lessons:
  1. **Balance is everything.** Alternate all-nighters with exploring.
  2. **Never stop learning.** Every job, project and city teaches something.
  3. **Your network is your lifeline.** Connect with nomads, join groups, build a social presence.
  4. **Embrace change.** Expect internet issues, late payments, even a pandemic, and adapt.
- His conclusion: being a nomad is "more than a lifestyle; it's a mindset, a celebration of freedom, and a relentless pursuit of living life on your own terms."
