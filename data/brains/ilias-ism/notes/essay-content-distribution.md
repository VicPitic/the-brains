## Why one article should be distributed to seven channels

Ilias argues that most creators publish and move on, and that distribution matters more than publishing:
- The typical workflow he criticizes: write, publish, tweet once (maybe), move on. He likens it to "building a store in the middle of nowhere and expecting customers to magically find it."
- "One well-distributed article will outperform ten undistributed ones."
- Competitors with "worse" content can get 10x the reach because they treat publishing as the beginning, not the end.
- Mindset shift: stop thinking "I need to write more content"; start thinking "I need to distribute better." "Content distribution isn't extra work. It's THE work."
- Quick audit: pull the top 10 posts by organic traffic and check whether each has been distributed to all 7 channels. He says a site with 20-50 posts likely has them getting about 10% of their potential traffic.

## The 7X framework: one article, seven formats

When an article is published, Ilias wants it to become, at the same time:
- Twitter thread (immediate engagement)
- LinkedIn article (professional network)
- Pinterest pins (visual search traffic)
- Reddit post (community engagement)
- Medium syndication (backlinks and reach)
- Newsletter (owned audience)
- Quora answer (intent-based traffic)

Suggested timing: Twitter day 1, LinkedIn day 2, Reddit days 3-5, Medium day 7, newsletter day 8; Pinterest and Quora are ongoing.

## Twitter thread, cross-posted to other platforms

- Don't just tweet a link: "Nobody clicks those anymore."
- Paste the article into Claude or ChatGPT with a prompt like: "Turn this blog post into a 10-tweet thread with a strong hook, key insights as individual tweets, and the link in tweet 1."
- Structure: tweet 1 is a controversial hook plus the link; tweets 2-9 are key takeaways with specific examples; tweet 10 is a CTA back to the full article.
- Use Typefully to schedule and auto-cross-post to LinkedIn, Threads, Bluesky and Mastodon. One thread reaches five platforms.

## LinkedIn native articles

- Why: higher engagement than regular posts, stays on the profile permanently, and LinkedIn promotes native content over external links.
- How: use "Write article", paste the blog content with a more professional rewritten intro, add images, and say "Originally published on [YourSite]" in the first paragraph. End with a CTA back to the site.
- Bonus: tag relevant people and companies in the article to increase visibility.

## Pinterest: one pin per example

- Pinterest is a goldmine for any content with visual elements, because users are actively searching.
- Create one pin for each example or tip in an article, each linking back to the post. Example: an article of "52 Cover Letter Examples" becomes 52 pins, each showing one example.
- Use Canva templates for consistency, or BlogToPin.
- The funnel: someone searches "cover letter example", the pin appears, they click, land on the site and convert.

## Reddit and Quora: answer questions, then link

**Reddit** (users hate self-promotion):
- Find relevant subreddits, look for questions the article answers, write a genuinely helpful reply, then mention "I actually wrote a detailed guide on this: [link]."
- Sort by "new" and be the first valuable commenter, since early engagement brings more upvotes and visibility.
- Where text posts are allowed, format the article's insights as a post with "Full details in my blog post for anyone interested." Reddit posts can rank on Google, so this is SEO plus distribution.

**Quora** (he calls it the most underrated channel):
- Search for questions on the article's topic and write comprehensive 500-800 word answers using rewritten sections of the post. Use the blog outline as the answer structure to save time.
- End with "I wrote a complete guide on this: [link]."
- Why: answers rank on Google, Quora shows answers to people following topics, and traffic is high-intent.

## Medium syndication and newsletter

**Medium** (100M+ monthly readers, ranks well in search), two approaches:
- Backlink play: rewrite the article slightly (different intro, condensed middle), publish with "Originally published on [YourSite]" at the top, and get a backlink from a high-authority domain.
- Canonical play: paste the exact article, then set Settings → Advanced → canonical URL to the original, so Google treats it as syndicated and SEO isn't hurt.
- Medium also surfaces posts to people who would never find the blog.

**Newsletter** (owned audience):
- Subject: "I just updated [Article Topic] with new data."
- Body: why it was updated, the 3 biggest takeaways, a CTA to read the full article, and a PS: "If this was helpful, forward to a friend."
- Pro move: segment the list and send different angles of the same article to different segments.

## Measuring ROI per channel

- Track per channel: traffic, engagement time, conversions, and backlinks from syndication. He recommends Datafast for this.
- Common pattern he reports: Twitter drives the most traffic, LinkedIn drives the highest-quality leads, Pinterest drives steady long-term traffic, and Reddit drives either zero or huge spikes. Knowing this lets you prioritize.
- He claims an undistributed top-10 list of posts represents "$50k-500k in potential traffic value (based on search volume × cost-per-click)."
