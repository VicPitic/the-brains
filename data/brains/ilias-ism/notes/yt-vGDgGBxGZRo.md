## Ilias's background and how he learned SEO

- Ilias started as a developer and front-end designer, building sites with Vue.js, React and JavaScript, and SaaS software with a team.
- He moved into SEO because side projects had no distribution: "nobody finds your apps", so he had to figure out traffic.
- He learned by doing: search a keyword, implement strategies, then watch Google Search Console every few days to see whether rankings improved or dropped.
- He argues SEO suits developers because it can be engineered: build a site, understand the algorithm, make it rank higher. He contrasts it with TikToks, brand marketing or social media where you need to learn Photoshop.
- He notes SEO advice conflicts constantly (backlinks matter or don't, descriptions matter or don't, entities, topical clusters), so the only way to find the truth is your own experiments.
- Today he runs an SEO agency plus side products. The agency is Magic Space SEO in Switzerland: strategy, consulting, blog posts and backlinks, fully done-for-you with Slack access. The tools are AI SEO Tracker, Link Dr and GenPPT.

## How he tests SEO

- He does not run formal A/B tests with five near-identical pages. He thinks single-variable tests are time-consuming and only give marginal gains: a table instead of an image won't matter against a competitor with a higher domain rating.
- He tests ideas on his own live sites or directly with clients, for example backlinks plus entity stacking.
- If something works, he repeats it later to confirm. He also keeps doubting it, "because everything changes all the time" as the algorithm shifts.

## Links over content in 2026

- Ilias says both on-page and links matter, but more mentions and links is the best strategy.
- Anyone can now publish 100 blog posts instantly. Tools make about 30 articles a month with images and links for around $100 per month. Content alone is no moat.
- The advantage is links nobody else can buy. His main version is a private network of his own legitimate sites that get their own traffic and don't link out to anyone else.
- Commodity links (parasite pages, profile links, directories, Link Tree style pages) raise the baseline for everyone. His analogy: in Switzerland everyone wears luxury, so basic gets you nothing. "The only way to stand out is get the links that nobody else has."
- Beyond blog links he wants broad awareness: listicles, guest posts, YouTube videos, Reddit, Medium, TikTok, Facebook, Twitter. Google's AI overviews and "people also ask" pull from all of these, so appearing everywhere builds one overall awareness graph.
- He notes AI overviews attach videos to paragraphs, and he thinks YouTube videos that mention you get extracted for content.

## AI SEO Tracker: how it works

- The product tracks whether a site is mentioned in ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot, Perplexity, AI Mode and AI Overviews. It costs $79 per month, and he expects the industry to settle around $799 per month at the high end.
- Setup: it scrapes the site, converts it to markdown, and runs a set of prompts to infer what the site is about.
- It builds a customer journey map of prompts from awareness through solution-aware to bottom-of-funnel. Example for a cloud-links tool: "how do I scale an SEO agency", then "automated backlink building software", then "what is cloud stacking".
- It runs each prompt in an incognito browser, copies the AI answer, and checks whether the brand is mentioned. It extracts every brand and entity mentioned, shows competitors and their order, and re-scans weekly.
- It lists the source pages feeding each answer and groups source domains by domain rating bucket.
- Roadmap goal: click a button to generate the missing blog post and backlinks. He expects customers want the least work possible: put in a credit card and be mentioned everywhere next month. He thinks people would pay about $500 per month for five posts plus five backlinks.

## Getting cited in AI answers: the GenPPT case

- GenPPT is bootstrapped (zero funding) and competes with tools that raised around $50M. Ilias says it is mentioned in several AI answers, though usually around position 9 to 18 and about number 10 in ChatGPT listicles.
- Method: look at the sources feeding AI answers for a prompt (for example "best AI presentation maker"), then get into those sources.
  - He emailed the author of a "best AI presentation makers" listicle (Cripesh) and got GenPPT added.
  - He wrote a guest post himself on a Story Chief style guest-post site, and commented on a Reddit thread.
  - He emailed GCS Network and got a link there too. Roughly four sources now feed ChatGPT results.
- Result: after a few mentions the brand started appearing in AI results. He says AI results differ from Google because people scan the whole list, so even position 12 is useful.
- To rank higher in those lists, the product itself must improve: better conversion and features.
- Source patterns from his tracker data:
  - YouTube had about 460 mentions across 31 keywords, the most common source, so a decent YouTube presence helps a lot.
  - Medium, G2, Reddit, Wikipedia and blog or guest-post sites also appear.
  - Many cited domains have domain rating 0 to 20 or under 40, unlike Google money keywords where high domain rating is needed. Example: a security-site network ranked high at DR 40.

## The email outreach process for hard links

- His AI email writer scrapes the target article and the sender's site, then checks whether the sender is already mentioned and whether a backlink exists. It works out the author's angle and what the publisher cares about (keeping the article current, giving readers actionable tools, partnerships and traffic).
- The email references specifics from the article and a gap in it. Example: the listicle ranks a tool at number six but doesn't link GenPPT in that section, so "readers are asking where to actually buy".
- He offers real value instead of asking for a free link: a link to their article from his sites, a guest post, co-created content, social promotion to his audience, and original data or a case study.
- For a hard target like Shopify, he says a plain link ask won't work. Offer data they would love, for example scraping the top 1,000 Shopify stores' schema/structured data and charting which schema (such as product offer with date and coupon) correlates with ranking in the top 100.
- He calls it "hardcore enterprise B2B sales", and says this is how he got the links that cheap spam never would.

## Link Dr: SERP-based backlink prospecting

- Core idea: backlinks from pages ranking in the top 100 for your keyword are worth far more than random links.
- Given a keyword (for example "best link building tools"), the tool pulls the top 100 results, which costs about 10 scrapes. For each site it:
  - screenshots the homepage to check it is legit,
  - records domain rating and visits,
  - saves the pages it ranks for and related similar sites,
  - scrapes contact details from homepage and contact page, and uses the Hunter API for emails,
  - flags available parasite opportunities.
- Output: one exportable list of contacts you can paste into a Google Sheet and start outreach. He uses the same method for clients and for his own GenPPT link.
- Fast domain rating growth for Link Dr (reaching about DR 55 within roughly a month): he linked the new brand from his own sites (the agency, AI SEO Tracker, GenPPT, clients), plus profile links (calendar page, rank tracker), Indie Hackers guest posts and directories. His own sites don't link out to anyone else, which helps.
- Link Dr itself gets little organic traffic because SEO tools are extremely competitive. He relies on:
  - a free "top directories to submit to" list on LinkedIn, offered in exchange for a comment,
  - Reddit: a post about building an AI-powered link building tool (with feedback requested, no link) that still ranks in the top 3 on parasite SEO a year later,
  - press mentions, Twitter and LinkedIn.

## Social and free-offer tactics

- Giveaways and freebies work well on social. His examples: "put your domain in the comments and I will give you one SEO tip", or "one free entity stack link" for followers.
- People love free stuff, and SEO people respond especially well to domain-review style offers.
- Facebook (discussed by the host) can be used with ads that drive people to a page profile; Ilias adds that this is a cheap way to gain visibility there.
