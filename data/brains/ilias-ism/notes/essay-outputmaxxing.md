## Outputs, not detection, are what people pay for

- Ilias frames the rule as outputmaxxing: AI tools should ship outputs (artifacts), not reports or dashboards.
- His test, one question: "After someone uses your tool, what exists that didn't exist before?"
- If the answer is "a decision" or "awareness", he says you built a feature, and "an agent will eat it".
- If the answer is an artifact (an email sent, a post published, a link placed, a deck exported), people will pay, because the tool "collapsed the distance between knowing and done".
- His line: "People do not pay to know. They pay for the thing to exist."

## The portfolio comparison: same founder, 100x revenue gap

Ilias treats his product portfolio as an accidental controlled experiment. Same founder, same distribution playbook, same market:

| Tool | What it does | Revenue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AI SEO Tracker | Detects: tracks AI search visibility | ~$200/mo |
| LinkDR | Detects and places links | Low five figures/mo |
| GenPPT | Produces: prompt to finished deck | ~$10,000/mo |

- AI SEO Tracker gives a list of where ChatGPT mentions you and where it doesn't, then the user has to go do the work elsewhere.
- LinkDR also finds link opportunities, but it gets the link placed. He says that is the entire difference.
- The one detection tool in his portfolio that makes real money is the one that ends with something that did not exist before.

## Why detection is becoming a commodity

- Five years ago a good audit was worth $5,000 because finding problems was hard.
- Today any decent agent can crawl a site, pull rankings, diff against competitors and produce a better audit than most agencies for about $0.50 of tokens.
- Every insights dashboard, tracker and audit tool now competes with a ChatGPT prompt.
- The bottleneck moved "from knowing to shipping", and he says most tools in his industry still sell knowing.
- What is still expensive and manual in his growth playbooks:
  - The insertion email actually sent to the author ranking #7 for your money keyword.
  - The comment actually posted in the Reddit thread sitting at #3 on Google.
  - The listicle actually published on your own domain.
  - The creator outreach actually delivered to the YouTuber whose comparison video got 39k views.
  - The LinkedIn post, video script, image or deck, actually made.

## The $18,000 listicle: a blind spot in detection tooling

- GenPPT's revenue dashboard showed a 16% drop. Ilias pulled 90 days of attribution by channel, page and plan tier, as he would for a MagicSpace SEO client.
- His best revenue month ever was mostly someone else's blog post: StoryChief's December listicle "Best AI Presentation Makers".
- It ranked on Google through January and February and sent about 18,000 visitors to GenPPT.
- Conversion rates: that referral traffic converted at 11.9%, versus 4.7% for Google organic and 1.9% for direct. Hundreds of paying customers came from one article on a domain he doesn't own.
- When the post slipped off page one, referrals decayed 95%, which explained most of the "revenue drop".
- Lesson he draws: he never saw it happen because the page wasn't his. His analytics, rank tracker and AI visibility tracker were all blind to the highest-converting asset in his funnel.
- He had even rewritten that StoryChief article earlier, yet had no output system to defend it, expand it, or turn the finding into the next 20 placements.
- Takeaway: third-party placements can be the best-converting asset in a funnel, so the answer is a system that produces more of them, not another dashboard.

## What he is rebuilding: every result gets an action

Using LinkDR research on "best ai presentation maker" (the keyword the StoryChief listicle ranked for), he got 99 results, 58 contact emails auto-extracted, Reddit threads at position #3, YouTube reviews from three weeks earlier, and an AI Overview citing competitors. The old report-style page leaves the work unstarted. The new version attaches an action to each result:

- **Listicle:** "Write insertion email", with the contact attached and the pitch drafted, personalized to what their list already covers, including dead tools they still recommend.
- **Reddit thread:** "Write comment", in the voice of the subreddit, with honest trade-offs and the tool mentioned once, like a human would.
- **YouTube review:** creator outreach.
- **Rollout:** V1 generates the prompt and the user fires it with one click. V2 executes through MCP connectors: "agents with hands, not dashboards".

## Closing the loop across products

Ilias plans a portfolio loop where detection feeds production and production feeds verification:

1. AI SEO Tracker detects you're missing from the eight listicles ChatGPT cites for your category.
2. LinkDR drafts and sends eight pitches with contacts already found.
3. The tracker verifies when the citation appears.

He says outputmaxxing is now the bar for everything he builds. His closing point: "The fix wasn't another dashboard. It was an output."
