## Search intent first, then a manual outline

- Ilias says Google doesn't penalize "AI content", it penalizes bad content. A post that shows experience, satisfies intent and is useful can rank whatever tool drafted it.
- Topic selection: pull queries from Google Search Console (positions 3-20), Ahrefs or LinkDR's keyword analyzer. Check which formats already rank, which questions are answered and what gaps exist, then decide the unique experience or data he can add.
- The outline is written by hand ("non-negotiable"), before AI drafts anything: Problem → Promise → Proof → Process → FAQs → Next steps.
- In the outline he adds the entities and phrases readers expect to see, and marks where screenshots, charts or personal anecdotes will go.
- Prompt for finding gaps: "You are an editor. Given this query: 'write blog posts with AI', list subtopics, entities, and FAQs the top 10 pages cover." Then ask for 5 gaps he can fill from hands-on experience.
- If starting from zero, he suggests seeding supporting content so new posts have internal-link fuel. Outrank is the tool he names for this: 30 SEO-ready articles in month one (code ILLY for 10% off; affiliate link).

## Drafting with AI section by section

- Work one section at a time, with prompts that are specific, short and scoped to the outline.
- Example prompt: "Write a 120-180 word section that explains why outlines matter for AI writing. Constraints: no fluff, simple language, include one actionable sentence. Target reader: solo founder. Tone: practical."
- Cap paragraph length and word count, ask for bullets, examples or mini checklists, regenerate until crisp, and delete clichés.

## Inject E-E-A-T, the part AI can't fake

He says this "is where rankings are won". Add:
- Your own screenshots, results and failures.
- Primary sources, with outbound links where helpful.
- Author bio, byline, and clear contact/about pages.
- Internal links from older, relevant pages (seeded with Outrank posts if needed).
- Visual content such as memes from MagicMeme, for engagement and shareability.

## On-page SEO checklist

- Unique title tag containing the target phrase naturally (here "write blog posts with AI").
- H1 aligned with the title; the first 100 words answer the query.
- Descriptive subheadings, short paragraphs, scannable bullets.
- One primary CTA plus relevant internal links.
- Compressed images with descriptive alt text.

## Fact-check and de-template before publishing

- Verify every stat and claim and replace them with primary sources.
- Remove generic filler such as "leverage" and "unlock".
- Read the post aloud and cut 10-20% for clarity.
- Run a plagiarism check if needed.
- He calls AI drafts "starting points".

## Publish, then iterate; the 90-minute workflow

- Publish, request indexing in Google Search Console, and iterate on impressions and queries over the next 2-4 weeks. Add one improvement per day: a stat, a screenshot, an FAQ.
- 90-minute workflow for one post:
  1. Pick keyword and outline (15 min)
  2. Draft sections with AI (25 min)
  3. Add proof such as screenshots and examples (20 min)
  4. Optimize on-page and links (15 min)
  5. Publish and request indexing (5 min)
- Length: as long as needed to satisfy intent. Many queries are solved in 800-1,500 words when the post is specific and visual.
- Disclosure: if AI meaningfully assisted, he says disclosing editorially is good practice and can build trust with technical audiences.
- His summary: "Writing blog posts with AI doesn't hurt SEO, publishing low-quality, unoriginal, unhelpful content does."
