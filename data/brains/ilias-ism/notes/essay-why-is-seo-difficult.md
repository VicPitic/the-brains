## Why SEO is hard: five forces Ilias names

Ilias frames SEO as "one of the most dynamic and complex disciplines" in digital marketing. It requires balancing technical expertise, creative content strategy and adaptation to algorithm shifts. His 2025 view is that AI, tougher competition and changing user expectations have made it harder. He lists five core reasons.

## Reason 1: Core updates keep rewriting the rules

- Google ran four core updates and three spam-focused updates in 2024.
- He says the March 2024 core update took 45 days to roll out and cut low-quality content in results by 45%, beating Google's 40% target.
- He says the December 2024 update leaned on E-E-A-T (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness), hitting healthcare and finance hardest.
- Updates often demote previously working strategies "overnight". Recovering from a drop is a "Sisyphean" task, because drops rarely come from one issue. They come from a content ecosystem that fails evolving quality benchmarks.
- His adaptation playbook:
  - Monitor continuously with Google Search Console plus Ahrefs or Semrush.
  - Run regular content audits against E-E-A-T, especially for YMYL (Your Money or Your Life) topics.
  - Diversify traffic sources so organic search is not the only channel.
- He notes programmatic SEO (pSEO) sites can recover from updates.

## Reason 2: Technical SEO is the invisible foundation

- Mobile accounts for 63% of global search traffic (2024 figure he cites), and Google "always" crawls sites on mobile. Responsive design has to be flawless.
- He says an August 2024 Core Web Vitals update tied rankings to LCP, FID and CLS.
- Enterprise blockers he lists:
  - Legacy CMSs that cannot support modern performance work.
  - Balancing rich media with page speed.
  - Coordinating dev, design and content teams.
- He says 58% of sites misuse structured data. Wrong schema risks being misread by search AI models.
- He says the 2024 BERT update favored contextual understanding over keyword density. His response:
  - Use NLP in content creation.
  - Do entity-based optimization aligned with Google's Knowledge Graph.
  - Keep refining semantic relationships inside content clusters.

## Reason 3: Quality content is hard to scale

- He says Google's September 2024 Helpful Content Update formalized penalties for "content mill" tactics. 23% of domains relying on unsupervised AI articles lost SERP visibility.
- Scaling good content is still expensive. His 2025 enterprise survey figures:
  - Original research: 68% cite budget constraints.
  - Updating legacy content: 72% struggle to track ROI.
  - Multilingual localization: 54% report quality degradation.
- What works: a hybrid model. Use AI for ideation and drafting, and keep human editors for E-E-A-T elements like expert interviews and data validation.

## Reason 4: Link building and local SEO got harder

- He says the March 2024 spam update hit private blog networks (PBNs). He cites Ahrefs data showing a 62% drop in PBN-powered rankings.
- He says nofollow and UGC links (Reddit, Quora) now appear in 37% of top-ranking pages, which shifts how links are earned.
- Modern link acquisition, in his view:
  - Digital PR tied to newsworthy data studies.
  - Content partnerships with academic institutions.
  - Outreach at scale with LinkDR (his link-building tool).
  - Tracking brand mentions (e.g. with Mention) to recover unlinked citations.
- For local SEO, he describes a 2024 "Near Me" update. Google Business Profiles increasingly need:
  - Real-time inventory integration for product businesses.
  - Hyperlocal, neighborhood-specific content.
  - Video testimonials.

## Reason 5: AI Overviews and AI search

- He says Google's 2025 AI Overviews (formerly Search Generative Experience) answer 28% of informational queries directly, collapsing click-through on featured snippets.
- Early adopters benefit from:
  - Structured data markup for AI answer eligibility.
  - Conversational content built around question-and-answer pairs.
  - Authority positioning via Google's "Perspectives" filter.
- To adapt, he says to:
  1. Build voice search strategies for Alexa and Siri.
  2. Optimize for semantic search via knowledge panel eligibility.
  3. Create interactive tools (calculators, quizzes) that resist AI summarization.
