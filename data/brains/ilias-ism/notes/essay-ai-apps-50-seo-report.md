## The a16z AI Apps 50 SEO study: what the data shows

Ilias and the MagicSpace team pulled Ahrefs data (October 3, 2025) for all 50 companies on a16z's AI Apps list to see who wins organic search and what separates winners from the rest. Findings below are the team's analysis, published under Ilias's byline.

## Market concentration and the organic vs paid gap

- Combined, the 50 tools rank for 10.6M keywords, get 595M monthly visits, are worth $73.5M in traffic value, and have 1.6M backlinks from 476K referring domains.
- Ilias's read: the market is heavily concentrated. Canva (about 24% of traffic), OpenAI (about 19%) and Grammarly (about 5%) take roughly 65%; the other 47 fight over about 35%. The top 10 tools get 85% of traffic, the middle 20 get 13%, the bottom 20 get 2%.
- His lesson: "You're either top 10 in your category, or not! SEO is more brutal than ever." Being on the list doesn't mean winning.
- Paid search across all 50 tools is only about $268K/month against $73.5M in organic traffic value (a 274x ratio). His conclusion: "Organic is the only sustainable acquisition channel at scale."
- Heuristic: spending over $10K/month on ads while having massive organic traffic suggests organic isn't converting, "a product problem, not a marketing problem." Example: Grammarly has about $93.6K paid spend alongside 30M organic visits; Freepik has $69 paid spend with 52M organic visits.

## Focus on a few keywords, not keyword bloat

- Most of the traffic comes from fewer than 1,000 high-volume terms. Grammarly ranks for 665K keywords, but its top 100 keywords drive about 80% of its traffic.
- Ilias argues most tools waste resources chasing long-tail keywords that bring 1-2 visits a month, and should "focus on the 1-5 keywords that matter."
- His method for this is the Blitz SEO framework (il.ly/blog/blitz-seo), which targets even a single keyword.
- Vertical depth beats breadth: OpusClip (video clipping) and Otter.ai (meeting notes) outperform generalists. "Own one category completely > weak presence in multiple categories."

## What separates winners: content volume, programmatic pages, category education

- Content volume beats link building. Canva (4.3M keywords) and Grammarly (649K) show big content libraries beating selective high-authority linking. Pattern: "Every new template/tool/asset = new indexable page = new ranking opportunity." He calls this programmatic SEO and sells a course on it.
- Inventory-as-SEO works: Freepik's asset pages, Gamma's and CapCut's template libraries, Midjourney's community galleries, and Notion's template gallery each create thousands of landing pages.
- Free tools as lead magnets plus educational content at scale drive Grammarly's coverage.
- Category leaders own educational content: Perplexity (11M visits), Midjourney (2.5M), ElevenLabs (3.8M). Pattern: "How to use [category]" content beats "Why use [our tool]" content. MagicSpace builds this through "pillar posts."
- Developer tools win through problem-specific documentation (Cursor, Replit): "Problem-specific documentation > generic marketing content." Comparison pages (e.g. "Cursor vs VS Code") also work.
- Newer fast movers: Cursor (589K monthly visits, DR 84) and Lovable (317K visits, DR 89) show near-vertical traffic climbs.

## Domain Rating is not traffic

- Authority doesn't equal visits. Canva (DR 93) and Grammarly (DR 90) convert authority into traffic efficiently, while some tools with similar DR get about 10x less.
- Examples of unused authority: Ada (DR 75, 9.5K traffic) and Crisp (DR 88 in the profile, 24.6K traffic, "They need more blog posts").
- Lesson: "Links without content strategy = wasted authority." He still says links are needed, and points to LinkDR for link building.

## Case study: Lovable's dead blog

- Lovable has DR 89 and 317K visits, but Ilias says that excluding branded keywords it effectively ranks only for its homepage.
- Its blog posts on money topics get almost nothing: best-wedding-website-builder (0 traffic, 3 keywords), digital-portfolio-website-examples (0 traffic, 7 keywords), do-you-need-a-cto-as-startup (0 traffic, 1 keyword), what-is-vibe-coding (0 traffic, 1 keyword).
- His point: wedding websites and portfolio builders are valuable topics sitting dead; they "really need to improve this by a lot."
- Takeaway: a brand-driven traffic spike can hide a content strategy that doesn't rank.

## Where authority and brand do the work, and the greenfield cases

- OpenAI (DR 92, 114M visits, 380K keywords): the product is the marketing. Product-led growth creates millions of natural backlinks, and its documentation ranks for thousands of technical queries.
- Anthropic (DR 88-89, 3.2M visits, 24.6K keywords): research papers as content marketing and AI-safety deep-dives earn backlinks from academic sources. An authority-first approach with fewer keywords but higher-intent traffic.
- Perplexity (122K keywords): "Be the answer engine, rank in the search engine."
- Near-zero SEO presence despite funding or buzz: Fyxer.ai (DR 36, 1 keyword), Serval (DR 11, 22 traffic), Combinely (DR 18, 102 traffic), Crosby (DR 22, 30 traffic). Ilias jokes that Crosby on the list looks like an a16z portfolio plug. His view: starting from zero means basic content strategy gives quick wins.
- Geography: B2C tools go global for volume (India-heavy); B2B tools stay US-focused for better conversion and monetization.
