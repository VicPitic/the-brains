## How Sam structures an annual review: the four F's

- Sam sets goals and writes his yearly review in four categories: Family, Fitness, Finance, Fun. Each gets highlights and lowlights.
- He writes these because "they'll be fun to review in a decade" and because writing is therapeutic.
- He doesn't use a template; the framework is just those four headings.
- He also runs quarterly fitness goals, and was goalless for the first time in years after his daughter was born.
- His takeaway for the year: the lowlights (pregnancy complications, his dog dying, injuries, real estate) were as instructive as the wins.

## Sam's 2023 income picture: 13 streams that accumulated by accident

- Sam says 2023 was a huge year for cash flow and net worth, and that it "wasn't really my intention."
- He has 13 income streams: Hampton, podcasting, courses (CopyThat, Ideation Bootcamp), speaking, real estate, Airbnbs, and a few others.
- His goal was never to build many streams or a big annual cash flow. He expected to make more money starting and selling startups.
- Instead he did side projects over the years and they accumulated into strong cash flow.
- He doesn't count stock gains (S&P 500, HubSpot) as cash flow.
- He is also frugal and bad at spending: he stresses over purchases he shouldn't, and thinks he could "let loose a little more."

## My First Million: growth and monetization in 2023

- My First Million hit 100 million downloads and views in 2023.
- It has 550 episodes since 2019, 131 of them in 2023.
- Sam says he never expected it to become what it is.
- 2023 was the first year the show focused on YouTube; it reached about 400,000 subscribers.
- Sam says the podcast is "always very profitable."
- It has grown into a community: fans host their own MFM meetups worldwide, and thousands attended.
- Live shows: Sam, Shaan and Andrew Wilkinson sold out a 2,000-person theater in Vancouver. He also did live pods in Austin, and spoke in LA and Boston (HubSpot conference).
- He doesn't love speaking but enjoys seeing people care about what he's doing.

## Hampton: hiring rules, headcount and the Joe Speiser partnership

- 2023 was the first full calendar year of Hampton, which Sam started with Joe Speiser.
- Their starting rules: don't hire lots of people, and avoid big-city folks because they complain too much.
- They broke both. At 18 months in they had about 16 employees, most in NYC, plus 50+ contractors, nearly 70 jobs created.
- Hampton also hosts member events (rally car driving in Austin, lobster diving in LA and Miami, a team retreat in NYC), which Sam attends as an attendee.
- Sam thinks Hampton will be a legacy-defining company for him and Joe.
- He cold emailed Joe in 2016 and they became close. Joe previously started and sold a few 9-figure companies.
- Sam describes the partnership as aligned on values, with great communication and mutual respect.
- Sam links a Hampton post on the template he and Joe used to make sure they were suited as co-founders.

## Courses: reluctant but they beat expectations

- Sam launched a second course in 2023, Ideation Bootcamp. The other is CopyThat.
- He doesn't love making courses and calls the process tedious.
- He made them because people kept asking him about copywriting and researching businesses.
- Results "blew past" his expectations, but he doesn't plan to make many more.
- His approach: build the product people are already asking you for.

## Real estate: why Sam is selling it all

- After selling The Hustle, Sam thought he'd become a real estate guy. He bought a 20-acre ranch, turned it into an Airbnb, and bought more property.
- His verdict: "real estate sucks. I hate it!"
- The properties make decent revenue, but he finds them boring and doesn't want to spend time thinking about them.
- He's selling everything, even at possibly the worst market timing, to get out.
- The lesson he draws is about time and attention: revenue doesn't compensate for assets he finds tedious to manage.

## Fitness and lifestyle notes relevant to how Sam operates

- Fitness goals he hit in 2023: an NFL-combine-style test scoring average for a wide receiver (4.6 forty, 10'7" broad jump, 32" vertical, 225 bench for 16 reps), lower body fat, and lifts of 420 squat, 325 bench, 460 deadlift at 195 lbs.
- He used MyBodyTutor all year for nutrition and calls it a game changer.
- His biggest fitness limiter is injury (hamstrings, lats, shoulders, Achilles).
- After his daughter was born, his goal became "look good naked with the least amount of time in the gym."
- On fatherhood: he says it has given his life energy and purpose, and that it will change everything, including his career.
- He values loyalty highly and wants more adventure in future years, inspired by Jesse Itzler's point about having only about 30 summers left.
