## Sam's 14-step writing process for The Hustle

Sam and John used this process to launch The Hustle. It let them publish high-quality content daily, sometimes 3-6 blog posts a day each, with no formal writing backgrounds. Sam says copying it will help a new writer finish a stalled project.

1. **Headline first.** Writing it first forces focus on the topic.
   - 3-10 words, plain English, as if telling a friend what the article is about.
   - Example: "I went 30 days with no food, living off only Soylent."
2. **Description second.** One or two sentences, the subtitle under the headline and the text shown below it on Facebook. It keeps the core idea in view.
3. **Write a bad draft.** Sam calls this the toughest part, because "most people are deathly afraid of writing and having their thoughts read."
   - "Accept that it will suck."
   - Write anything, even "I don't know how to start, but I'm just starting now."
   - "Motion creates emotion." Dump every thought on paper and ignore quality and word count.
4. **Incubate.** Walk away for 2 minutes or 2 years. Not focusing on a problem helps produce new ideas (fixation forgetting, "showerthoughts"). Sam calls this step "incredibly important."
5. **Edit.** "This is where the magic happens." He quotes David Ogilvy: "I'm a lousy copywriter, but I am a good editor." Editing "turns crap into gold."

## Cutting, openings and the slippery slope

- **Cut first.** For most people that means deleting the first 25% and the last 25% of the draft.
  - Since grade school, people are trained to write to a word count. Sam says that is wrong.
  - "Every single word must be necessary. Nothing more, nothing less." Don't use 1,500 words for what takes 500, because extra words dilute the message.
- **Make the first sentence "punch the reader in the face."** Attention is hard to get, so the opener must be undeniable.
- **Create a slippery slope.** Each sentence exists only to get the reader into the next one: the first sentence sells the second, the second sells the third, and so on.
  - Tactics: lead with a personal story, hint at what's coming later, or pose a question.
  - His Soylent example: "I spent the last 30 days eating nothing but Soylent... Why would I do something so stupid? I'll explain. But first, if you aren't familiar with Soylent, here's the gist."

## Writing simple

- Target a 7th to 8th grade reading level. His rule: "a great post has C+ writing and A+ storytelling, insight, or analysis."
- Check the grade level with the Hemingway app.
- Skip flowery or complicated language, whatever a 5th grade English teacher said.
- Keep paragraphs to 2-4 sentences and sentences to about 25 words or fewer, so the page is easier on the eye.
- Warren Buffett's shareholder letters on the complex insurance industry average 13 words per sentence. Sam's point is that even hard topics can be simplified.
- Avoid adverbs (words ending in "ly"), which weaken the verb. "I yelled at the boy" beats "I angrily yelled at the boy." He cites Stephen King: the road to hell is paved with adverbs.

## Headlines: where most of the work goes

- Sam says 90% of people read the headline and never consume the article, yet people rarely perfect it.
- Return to the headline after editing:
  - Write 25 headline options.
  - Show them to a few friends and ask which one they would click.
  - Pick from those.
- His claim: once the headline is written, "you have spent ninety cents out of your dollar."

## Images and copywork

- **Image:** don't use the logo. Make it "clicky." Google images may violate copyright, so he recommends Unsplash.
- **Copywork for inspiration:** pick a blogger, author or poet you like and write their work out by hand.
  - It shows how they structure writing and what their texture feels like.
  - Sam spent two hours a day for 6 months copying favorite authors and says it "worked wonderfully."
  - He says you don't need to go that far. Copying a few sentences before starting work helps.
