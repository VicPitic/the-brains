## How Sam got into business and what drives him

- Grew up in St. Louis, Missouri. His mom was a teacher and his father owned a produce business selling fruits and vegetables.
- His first business was a hot dog stand in college in Nashville. He then saw that the internet was a huge opportunity.
- He cold emailed the founder of what was then called "air bed and breakfast" (Airbnb) and asked for a job interview. He claimed to live in the Bay Area (he didn't), flew out, and got an offer. They then fired him the day before he started, because a background check caught that he had lied on his resume about a DWI.
- Stuck in San Francisco with nothing, he started a roommate-matching business ("Tinder for roommates") and sold it for about $50K.
- He then ran a conference, HustleCon, which made about $60K in its first 6 weeks and roughly $150K-200K in profit in its first 10 months.
- He used that success to launch The Hustle, a newsletter. People laughed at newsletters at the time. He sold it for tens of millions of dollars.
- Near the end of The Hustle, Shaan Puri recorded the first episode of My First Million and The Hustle became its publisher. After about 8-10 episodes Shaan asked Sam to join as co-host.
- His current company is Hampton, a paid community of founders, typically running about $25M revenue businesses, who are interviewed and vetted before joining. He splits his time between MFM and Hampton.
- He says he made enough money that he didn't have to work again, "but I didn't make enough money to please my ego." He also describes himself as an artist "addicted to launching stuff and the dopamine that comes with creating things."

## Why he builds: the hierarchy of motives

Sam describes his drive as layers, like Maslow's hierarchy:
1. **Stuff:** He wants little beyond a big house where family can visit without annoying him because they have their own space.
2. **Time:** He wants to be a "time billionaire." Life is short and capitalism isn't going away, so "the best way to win is to have enough money that you could tell anyone to go [expletive] themselves and I could spend my time how I want."
3. **Rebellion against control:** His parents controlled the money ("you're in my house, I pay the bills, you're going to do what I say"), so he decided to acquire capital so he could do what he wanted.
4. **Insecurity:** He was insecure growing up (braces, girls didn't date him, felt bullied). Seeing peers like Andrew find a path early, he felt behind. He says he doesn't want to tear anyone's tower down: "I want to have a skyscraper just as big because I want to feel adequate."

He also notes that in school he was liked but never "the guy." Starting companies made him "the guy" in his own clubhouse.

## Demystifying successful people (the hustle con insight)

- At HustleCon he hosted roughly 150-200 speakers from startups of the 2014-2018 era (Grammarly, Casper, Away, Warby-type names, The Athletic, OkCupid/Match).
- Backstage he concluded most were in the same ballpark of intelligence as him. In some cases he thought they were less smart. Only three or four people (he names Max from Grammarly) had clearly more "horsepower."
- They had the same fears he did. He describes the Match.com CEO as so nervous before speaking that Sam had to calm him down. Others agonized over firing someone, or over how to spend $100M just raised in order to live up to the valuation.
- His takeaway: "the people I admire are screw-ups and fearful just like I am." It was like going from bad eyesight to putting on glasses: "the world is clear, I can dominate this just like these guys do because it's okay that I'm a screwed up person."
- On social comparison: podcasts show prepared, polished versions of people. "Behind the scenes everything tastes like chicken." In his forum groups (about 24 people over 15 years), everyone has something driving them insane, most often people problems.
- He says the best people when they're jealous of someone ask "what are the cheat codes, how do I work backwards from what they did," instead of tearing the person down. Capitalism isn't winner-takes-all, since many people can create value.

## Running companies: delegating to a CEO

- At The Hustle he was command-and-control. At Hampton he hired a CEO who runs the company day to day, heavily inspired by Andrew's model.
- Sam acts as the marketing department: the podcast and his personal brand drive growth, and he comes up with ideas that pounce on opportunities.
- He plans to launch more companies and hire more CEOs. He wishes he had done this sooner but lacked the confidence and was "too much of a puss to break the mold."
- He still works a "9 to 5." His workload is about 40 hours a week, sometimes 30, sometimes 90. The change is that he is no longer in charge of HR or budgets, and focuses only on where he adds significant value.
- He quotes the idea "there's no such thing as problems, there's only people problems," so having someone else handle them is a good life. He still has one: keeping the CEO happy and rewarded, but he works with him, not with the 20 employees or 50 contractors.

## Money, family, fame and who to trust

- Parents' reaction over time: for about 5 years they told him to get a real job. After the exit (a headline said he sold The Hustle for a rumored $40M), they asked whether he had health insurance. Later came pride, then a kind of intimidation.
- His advice on money and family: tell relatives that the money isn't important, and "more likely than not you should always say no" to requests for money from family or distant family, even though it creates tension.
- He used to exaggerate prices to look cooler. Now he doesn't disclose what things cost, because it isn't important and he doesn't want people to feel inadequate.
- On fame: the podcast reaches millions per month, around 100 million downloads and YouTube views last year. He feels gratitude when approached but also discomfort for safety reasons, and because he doesn't want to be seen as a guru. "I'm just thinking out loud." He says the real ballers are the quiet people he talks about.
- Trust: he keeps about 5 friends who have keys to his house, access to his bank account, and a role in his kids' trusts, then another 10-15 people he'll share anything with. Everyone else gets a no. The house rule is "No New Friends." He was deliberately rude to one new friend for 6 months because he assumed the man wanted something, then apologized once the friend proved himself.
- He uses excuses (covid, pregnancy, the baby) to decline invitations rather than a flat no.

## Health, routines and habits

- He had a drug and drinking issue after college, was a college athlete, then got fat and "lumpy" for 6-7 years. After selling the company he spent about 3 years on fitness, lost around 30 lbs, and learned nutrition from his wife. He says girls still don't care. Shirtless posts mostly draw men.
- He took an SSRI for 10 years and no longer does. He credits better eating, much more exercise ("like a dog that's worn out at the end of the day") and a stable, supportive wife. Anxiety is about 50% when the home front is good. He still takes lorazepam for flying due to claustrophobia.
- Typical day:
  - Wakes 7:00-7:30, no alarm.
  - Lifts weights 5 days a week. On non-lifting days he does a cold plunge. On lifting days he also takes a 1-hour walk.
  - Plays with the baby until 9:00, then decaf coffee made with a Comandante grinder and an AeroPress.
  - Podcast days (2-3 a week): prep 9-11, record about 1.5 hours.
  - Lunch at noon, usually a meat and a carb such as chicken and sweet potato.
  - Works 1-5 with breaks every 30-50 minutes to see wife and baby.
  - Gym at 5:00 for about 1.5 hours, dinner and baby time, then Legos with his wife from 9-10 with the TV off.
  - Bed at 10:00, reads 10-11.
- He does mostly the same things every day: same food, similar exercise, similar reading. He is a homebody who has honed in on what makes him happy.
- Tools and products he recommends:
  - **MyBodyTutor** (about $300/month) for diet coaching via texts/calls and a fitness coach (about $350/month) who writes his workouts and sets a quarterly athletic goal.
  - **Ninja Creami** for protein ice cream. One pint is about 300 calories and 65g of protein: a cup of milk, a cup of almond milk, two scoops of protein, frozen overnight. He says most Americans eat too little protein.
  - **Fiv** app plus a Garmin heart-rate strap to stay in his target heart-rate zone with audio cues instead of a watch.
  - **Opal** (and Freedom) to block social media. He is only on Instagram before 9 a.m. and after 8 p.m.

## Reading and learning

- Reads about a book a week, for entertainment rather than self-improvement. Mostly history, lately some novels. He reads only on a Kindle Oasis (glass screen, side handle for reading in bed) and not audiobooks.
- Process: highlights on the Kindle, researches what to read on Goodreads, and the next morning looks up topics on Wikipedia for context and reviews his notes. He pulls shocking facts for the podcast and Twitter.
- He reads for about 60 minutes a day. If a book doesn't hook him by about 10%, he drops it. A 350-page book is about 7 hours.
- Books he rereads: *Carnegie* by David Nasaw, *Mastery* by Robert Greene (it changed his view from "generalists are awesome" to "you need a skill set"), and *Titan*, the Rockefeller biography. He admires Rockefeller as honest, loyal and confident.
- With Shaan on the podcast, the chemistry comes from 600 episodes together and from "Bro moves," such as always guessing under when asked how much something cost, to set each other up.
