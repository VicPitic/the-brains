## Sam's ambition: a ~$10m/year business with 2-4 people

- Sam's claim to fame: in 2016 he started a newsletter called The Hustle. By about year 5 it was at roughly $13m in revenue and 1.7m daily readers, plus podcasts and events.
- He estimates the sale year would have hit about $18m (they sold early in the year).
- He says: "Knowing what I know now, I think I can grow to +$10m in revenue with 2-4 people." He puts the timeline at 2-3 years.
- He floats that his own newsletter might become that business: "Hell, maybe that's what this newsletter will become."

## Creator economy: small teams are making serious money

- Sam spoke with 6 content creators in one week.
- 3 were small, with fewer than 100k followers on their platform of choice. Each made over $1m/year.
- 3 were bigger, about the size of My First Million. They did over $10m/year with tiny teams.
- All six had tech audiences.
- His takeaway, in his words: "It's raining money for creators."

## Newsletter test: how Sam is running his own list

- This was week #3 of a newsletter test. Because of mostly good feedback, he decided to send weekly.
- He is still tinkering with the "schtick." Current positioning: help an already successful person have a better career, business and "rich(er)" life.
- The test pool is about 2,000 people. After 2-3 weeks he plans to send to the other ~50k people who signed up for Copythat or his personal email list, which he hadn't used in years.
- He asks readers to help spread the word when he widens the send.
- Subscribe flow tactics: the rest of the letter is gated ("Join 32,000+ people who get it every Monday"), and clicking any link in a past email unlocks every letter.
- He asks one optional profiling question after signup (job title, company, company size) "so I can make cool stuff for people like you."
- Deliverability tactic: he asks new subscribers to reply to the welcome email, even just "hi." It is "good karma" and "shows the Google gods that I belong in your inbox and not in spam."
