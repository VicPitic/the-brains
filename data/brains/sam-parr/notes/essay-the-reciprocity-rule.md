## The gift-first principle (reciprocity rule)

- Sam's rule: "If you want something from someone, do them a favor or give them a gift first… even if they don't ask for it."
- He grounds it in Robert Cialdini's *Influence*. The receiver feels obligated to repay, and this holds in every human society.
- Unexpected, unasked-for gifts still trigger the obligation.
- Small favors can produce outsized repayment. Owing someone feels bad, so people over-repay to relieve the guilt.
- Sam adds a caveat: give because it's the right thing to do, not only as a tactic. Don't become "an evil genius" with it.

## Winning a negotiation with a $1.99 Coke

- Sam bought a Honda motorcycle on Craigslist listed at $2,200. He had just sold another bike for $1,800 and showed up with only $1,800 in cash.
- Before the meeting he stopped at Walgreens for two Cokes (one regular, one diet). He told the seller he'd bought a drink for him because they were on sale.
- He emailed the seller beforehand, and the seller called him right away to set up a viewing.
- Sequence: gift first, then a 30-minute test ride and inspection, then the price talk.
- He opened low: "I have a budget of $1,600." The seller said that was too low and claimed other buyers at $2,200.
- He countered at $1,800 and showed the cash: "I've got cash here now and most people on Craigslist flake anyway."
- The seller replied: "Well, since you brought me something to drink, I guess I can do $1,800." Result: $400 off.
- Sam doesn't claim the Coke alone caused it: "does it really matter?" He'd pay $1.99 even if it only raises the odds by 30%.

## Why a small gift works on skeptical counterparties

- Sellers arrive expecting a fight. They anticipate "lowballers, tire-kickers, and flakes."
- A random passerby isn't expecting to be screwed, but a seller is. So a small kindness catches them off guard.
- Sam's version of the tactic: be nice and disarm them before the price conversation starts.
- He pairs it with credible buying signals: cash in hand, a clear budget, and a quick close (the "most people flake" line).
- Sam says he has bought hundreds of things off Craigslist, and the negotiation is always the hardest and most uncomfortable part.

## Using a gift to land a free speaker

- Sam got Andrew Warner (founder of Mixergy) to speak at his Bootstrap Live conference for free.
- He first sent a bottle of scotch and a handwritten letter, then asked if Warner would speak.
- The ask came after the gift, not with it.

## Evidence Sam cites that reciprocity moves behavior

- **Restaurant tips**: one piece of candy with the bill raised tips 3.3%. Two pieces raised them 14.1%. Delaying the second piece for emphasis raised them 23%.
- **Donations**: the Hare Krishna Society hands out free flowers (about 5 cents each). When the person says thanks, the giver replies "And we accept donations so we can continue good works." That usually brings in a dollar or two.
- Sam's takeaway: a cheap, unrequested gift followed by a clear ask converts far above its cost.

## Playbook Sam implies

1. Give something small and unexpected before the ask (a drink, a handwritten note, a bottle of scotch).
2. Let some time or real interaction pass (a test ride, a conversation).
3. Make your ask with clear, credible terms (a stated budget, cash on hand).
4. Do it because it's good practice, not just manipulation.
