## Sam's definition of "successful" and who he enjoys being around

- Sam's personal definition of success has nothing to do with money or job title: "A poor teacher can be successful to me."
- For the purposes of this essay he narrows it to "rich" business types.
- His observations come from a conference weekend in Austin, Texas, plus a ~30 person friend group there that he "loves like family." Most are entrepreneurs; the rest are very successful at whatever they do.
- Through his job he has met hundreds of people with net worths from $100M to $20B. They "come in all flavors": organized or not, nice or mean, and not even the majority are cut-throat.

## Sam prefers kind people over cut-throat ones

- Cut-throat people care about numbers, spreadsheets and metrics, and put them above everything else.
- Sam describes one successful acquaintance who brags about how brutal he is to work for ("Perform or die") and says Elon Musk falls into that category.
- He does not enjoy spending time with that type. The successful people he spends time with are "incredibly kind."
- Example: Noah Kagan is very successful, and when Sam talks about business too much, Noah interrupts with "So how's Sam?" Sam teases him for sounding like "a cringy therapist."
- Takeaway for who to surround yourself with: kindness and genuine interest in the person beat intensity about metrics.

## Sam likes successful people who talk about life more than business

- At the conference he had a 2.5 hour dinner with George Mack (about 30, "incredibly smart") and Paddy Galloway (29, "a YouTube savant"), both wildly successful for their age.
- Sam is a client of Paddy's and was supposed to ask him a bunch of business questions. He forgot, and the conversation was mostly ideas: George's "strange, cool theories" on life, Sam's views on the importance of family, and Paddy's Irish Catholic background (which Sam shares).
- His reaction: "I LOVED it."
- Pattern: the best conversations with high performers are about ideas and life, not tactics.

## Sam gets along best with people who grew up poor or middle class

- Sam grew up in a working class family in Missouri.
- The successful people he connects with tend to come from poor or middle class backgrounds.
- Contrast: at a recent dinner with venture capitalists, they were "smart, kind, and interesting," but also "vest wearing, fancy education types," which he says is not the type he gets on best with.

## Sam values humility and self-deprecation

- He says "the best ones are humble and self-deprecating."
- Codie Sanchez and Chris Petkas are his examples: they are "hot shit" but still make fun of themselves (and him).
- Summary of his filter for people: kind, more interested in life and ideas than metrics, modest origins, and able to laugh at themselves.
