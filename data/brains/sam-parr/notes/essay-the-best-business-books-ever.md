## Sam's pick for the best business book ever: "Titan" by Ron Chernow

- Sam calls Titan, Ron Chernow's biography of John D. Rockefeller Sr., "seriously, without a doubt, the best business book I've ever read."
- He likes Rockefeller as "dynamic, inspiring, and rich (both figuratively and literally)": the richest man ever, thanks to Standard Oil.
- The traits he admires: loyal, disciplined, "confident yet humble, and ruthless." Rockefeller would crush other people's companies without a second thought, then be home for dinner, and he never cheated on his wife.
- Favorite quote, on perseverance: "I do not think that there is any other quality so essential to success of any kind as the quality of perseverance. It overcomes almost everything, even nature."
- Sam also wrote a 3,500-word history of Rockefeller and Standard Oil for The Hustle.

## Strategy and power: "The 48 Laws of Power" by Robert Greene

- Sam recommends it as a book that is "unapologetic about giving you the tools and tactics to succeed in the ruthless world of business."
- It runs over 400 pages. Greene spends hundreds of pages on the 48 laws, giving a historical example of each and how to use it today.
- Sam wrote a cheat sheet and summary of the book on his blog, and says it is still one of his most popular posts.
- Greene's framing, which Sam quotes: you're better off following the teachings of Moses, Jesus or Buddha for long-term happiness, but many people live by very different rules. Most of them eventually self-destruct, yet they can do real damage to your personal and professional life on the way.
- Sam's takeaway is that you need to understand how power plays work, mainly to defend against people who use them.

## Networking: "How to Win Friends and Influence People" by Dale Carnegie

- Sam first read it in 7th grade and revisits it every year or so. He says it "has changed my life more than any other book of all time."
- Written in 1936, it is one of the most popular non-fiction books ever and arguably the best-selling business book.
- Favorite quote: "You can make more friends in two months by becoming interested in other people than you can in two years by trying to get other people interested in you."
- Practical reading: to build relationships, lead with genuine interest in the other person rather than pitching yourself.

## Copywriting: "The Adweek Copywriting Handbook" by Joseph Sugarman

- Sam's claim: learning to communicate with the written word is "hands down, without a doubt, no questions asked THE most important skill any business person can have." The skill is copywriting.
- He picks Sugarman's handbook as the best on the subject because it is simple, easy and fast, yet comprehensive enough to cover the basics. His line: it "covers everything you NEED."
- The standard he takes from the book: copy should be so compelling that the reader cannot stop until they've read all of it, "as if sliding down a slippery slope."

## Sales and persuasion: "Influence: The Psychology of Persuasion" by Robert Cialdini

- Sam's logic: copywriting is the most important business skill, so learning how to influence others is essential.
- He says nearly every book, paper or study on persuasion after 1984 cites Influence. It is well researched, thorough and easy to grasp, and "the standard" on salesmanship.
- Favorite quote: "Embarrassment is a villain to be crushed." Sam highlights it as a reminder not to let fear of looking foolish stop you from selling or asking.

## How Sam picks business books

- The blog he wrote this on (The Anti-MBA) started as a book club where he blogged about his favorite books, mostly business or "business-y" ones. He pushed this reading list as a substitute for getting an MBA, an idea he also wrote about at The Hustle.
- His list maps one book to one core business skill: biography and perseverance (Titan), strategy and power (48 Laws), networking (Carnegie), copywriting (Sugarman), sales and persuasion (Cialdini).
- He says copywriting and persuasion are the most important skills. He also picks short, practical books for the basics rather than exhaustive ones.
- He wrote the post in 2017 in his early 20s and keeps it up for nostalgic reasons.
