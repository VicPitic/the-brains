## How Sam writes copy that gets people to act

- The goal of every piece of writing is to move the reader to act on what you want.
- He believes a good piece works like a "spell": the reader keeps reading because they want to, and rarely notices it's working.
- Sam doesn't call himself a great writer: "My grammar sucks. I am notorious for typos. But I am better than most."
- His edge is studying people: "I study humans and what makes them tick."

## The core rule: write like you're writing to a 5th grader

- Use words with only one or two syllables.
- Use short sentences.
- Use paragraphs with only a few sentences.
- Aim for a 5th grade reading level, and use the Hemingway App while writing to check it.
- He frames this as a cheap, easy-to-copy tactic: it takes little work and works on most readers.

## Every sentence has one job: earn the next sentence

- "The point of the first sentence is to get you to read the second sentence. And the point of the second sentence is to get you to read the third sentence. And so on."
- A well written email, blog post, or book creates a slippery slope, and the reader "melts away into the copy."
- Short sentences and short paragraphs keep the slope smooth, because each step is easy to take.
- He applies this to emails, blog posts, books, and sales copy alike.

## Make the writing do something to the reader

- Sam opens the essay by telling the reader their reaction (to the headline, to the sentence about their reaction) is "all on purpose."
- The hook is intrigue: people scroll slowly and keep reading because they are curious, not because they are forced.
- He argues people are mostly blind to persuasion working on them, and they enjoy it anyway.

## Sam's five-step learning list for copywriting

1. Take Neville Medora's Kopywriting Kourse.
2. Read *On Writing* by Stephen King.
3. Read *The Adweek Copywriting Handbook*, even if you don't write books or sales copy.
4. Read *Influence* by Robert Cialdini. He calls it "the greatest and most useful book I've ever read."
5. Use the Hemingway App while writing, aiming for a 5th grade level.
