## Goal setting with A, B, Z: three points, not a full map

Sam sets goals with a three-point system called A, B, Z (he explained it to Shaan Puri, who coined the name). A is the starting point. B is the next destination, usually 3-6 months out. Z is the big 3-5 year plan. He does not plan C, D, E and the steps in between until he gets there, other than vague ideas he expects may change.

His claim: "Most successful people I've met set goals. And the process of getting specific about goals creates breakthroughs." He says he has interviewed or met thousands of successful people, including billionaires and hundreds of millionaires.

## Why A, B, Z works: distraction, direction, constraint

Sam gives three reasons:

- **It limits distractions.** People often skip steps A and B because they worry about later stops. In doing A and B you gain new insight, and if you don't, you're still better off.
- **It points the steering wheel.** Ambitious people have high horsepower. For many potential winners, the issue isn't pushing the gas pedal but which direction the wheel points. Z guides you, and you work backwards from it. Z might change, and that's fine, but an end destination beats none.
- **It creates constraints.** Sam relays that "someone once said it's easier to 10x something than 2x" and says he isn't sure that's true. He values the constraint question anyway: if you HAD to 10x this outcome, how would you do it? "If you want to achieve Z, what must B be?"

## Rules for writing goals

- Goals must be specific. Not "lose weight" but "weigh 198 pounds by May 1st, 2026."
- Goals must contain both process and outcome: "weigh 198 pounds by May 1st, 2026 by eating 2,000 calories a day and lifting weights 3 days a week."
- Z should be ambitious, with about a 50% chance of happening.
- B should have about a 75% chance of happening.
- Everything works backwards from Z.
- Z can change occasionally, but changing it should be a big deal. Sam says it's an art to tell "I'm tired and therefore want to change" (bad) from "this truly isn't what I want anymore."
- Tell a few people (a spouse, business partner, friend) for accountability.
- He splits goals into the 4 F's: family, fitness, finance, fun.

## Worked example: finance and business (made-up numbers)

Sam's illustrative case is a 25-year-old.

**Goal Z:** By 30, have $10M liquid invested in the S&P 500.
- Sell the media company for $20M while owning at least 70%, netting a little north of $10M after taxes.
- The business needs to do $14M in revenue the year before the sale.

**Goal B (in 6 months):** Currently at an $8M run rate, which is 800k subscribers at $10 of annual revenue each. Target is a $10M run rate, so 1M subscribers at the same $10.
- That's 200k net new subscribers.
- The base churns about 30% a year, so roughly 120k are lost over six months.
- Real target: 320k gross adds.
- At $2.50 blended CAC, that's $800k of spend, about $133k a month.

The result: his main focus becomes spending money to acquire subscribers. He expects life to happen and new variables (CAC may change) to show up, but he knows what he's doing and why, and "most everything else doesn't matter."

## Worked example: fitness

**Goal Z:** At 36, aiming for age 41, "be an athlete who lasts."
- Run a sub-25 second 200m.
- Hold a 45+ VO2 max.
- Deadlift 2x bodyweight.
- Touch his toes with straight legs.
- Go five years without a soft-tissue injury that cost more than two weeks.
- Pick up his kids "without making a dad noise."

**Goal B:** Complete a full combine on December 1 without injuries.
- Do every workout the coach lists.
- Any pain above 3/10 means an automatic 2 days of rest for that body part.
- Targets at 202 lbs: 40-yard dash 4.7; vertical jump 32"; broad jump 10'1"; deadlift 405 (2x bodyweight); 225 bench for 12 reps; mile in 6:30.

## Goal template

Sam's fill-in-the-blank format:

- **Z:** By [date, 3-5 yrs], I will [outcome], measured by [number].
- **B:** By [date, 3-6 mo], I will [outcome], by [process].
- **Because:** B gets me to Z because [the link].

His instruction: "Try it. Now."
