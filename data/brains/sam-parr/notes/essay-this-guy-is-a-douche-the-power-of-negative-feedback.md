## How Sam treats negative feedback

- Sam argues that "negative feedback is far more valuable and actionable than positive feedback."
- He backs this with a passage from the book *Smartcuts*, which says negative feedback helps people learn and grow faster than positive feedback.
- Sam wrote this in 2014, in his early 20s, on his old blog The Anti-MBA. He later labeled it an "old post".
- He encourages critical feedback and says he can now laugh off harsh comments that used to upset him.

## Mindset required for negative feedback to work

Sam quotes *Smartcuts* on when negative feedback helps and when it hurts:
- It only works in the right mindset, otherwise it can be "catastrophic".
- Make it about the thing you did, not about you.
- You have to be "really secure" to feel good about yourself when all you get is critique.
- The fix is to build your self-esteem muscles by showing yourself you are okay when things go wrong. Feedback is about what you do, not who you are.
- "De-couple the performance from the self-esteem."

## Separating useful criticism from noise

- A popular California blog, The Bold Italic, ran an article on Sam's motorcycle trip, panic attacks and blog.
- Its Facebook comments included insults, such as calling him a douche.
- Sam says that feedback was not helpful: "those guys are just a bunch of jerks." He found the comments funny.
- He notes they were the only negative comments he got. He had 100+ positive comments on reddit.
- The takeaway is that random internet abuse is not the feedback he means. He wants structured, honest critique from people who know him.

## Sam's method for getting actionable negative feedback

1. Create a Google Form with two questions: what are my 3 biggest strengths, and what are my 3 biggest weaknesses.
   - He added the strengths question as a "red herring". It gets people talking and speaking their minds.
2. Send the form to 10 of your closest co-workers and friends.
   - Tell them you are working on yourself and want feedback because you value their opinion.
   - Tell them the form is anonymous.
3. Take the three most common weaknesses mentioned, print them out, and tape them to your bedroom door.

## Why the process works

- Anonymity lets people be honest.
- Asking for 3 weaknesses forces specifics instead of vague praise.
- Looking only at the three most common answers filters out one-off opinions and surfaces patterns.
- Taping them to the bedroom door keeps the weaknesses in front of you daily.
- Sam says he got "phenomenal results", and calls it a trick that "sounds crazy" but "works wonders".
- He says that even if people do not call you a douche, the results will still be helpful.
