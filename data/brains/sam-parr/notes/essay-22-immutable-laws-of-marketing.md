## Law of Leadership: be first in the customer's mind

- Sam summarizes *The 22 Immutable Laws of Marketing* and rates it "a 9/10 book".
- He holds that being first beats being better. The first brand in a customer's mind "almost always wins, regardless of product quality".
- Example: IBM dominated early computers because it was first, not because it was better.
- Being first to market is not enough. What counts is being first in the mind. If customers don't think of you when the need arises, "you've already lost".
- Example: Hertz was top of mind in rental cars, so Avis had to position itself around "we love being number 2".

## Law of Category: invent a category when you can't be first

- If you can't be first in a category, invent a new one. Customers remember the category leader, not the runner-up of the same type.
- Example: Charles Schwab wasn't the first brokerage, but it was the first discount brokerage and dominated that category.
- Law of Division: over time, broad categories split into subcategories, and each becomes its own game with its own winner. The computer category split into desktops, laptops and tablets, each with distinct players.
- Law of Duality: most markets eventually become a two-player race, and everyone else fights for scraps (Coke and Pepsi).

## Law of Perception: marketing is perceptions, not products

- Sam's framing: "Marketing is not about products…it's about perceptions." The best-perceived product wins, not the best product.
- Example: people consistently prefer Pepsi in blind taste tests, yet Coke still dominates.
- Law of Focus: owning one simple word or idea in the customer's mind is power. Brands that try to mean too many things mean nothing. FedEx owns "overnight" and no one else comes close.
- Law of Exclusivity: two brands can't own the same word in the prospect's mind. If someone already owns it, pick a different hill to die on. Volvo owns safety.

## Positioning when you're not the leader: ladder, opposite, attributes, candor

- **Ladder:** your rung in the mental hierarchy determines your strategy. Don't act like #1 if you're #3. Lead into your spot. Avis embraced being #2 with "we try harder".
- **Opposite:** if you're not the leader, don't copy them. Be the opposite. 7UP became "the uncola" against Coke.
- **Attributes:** you can't beat a leader at their own game, so pick a meaningful opposite attribute. If Coke is classic, Pepsi positioned itself as youthful.
- **Candor:** admitting a negative makes the message more believable. It disarms skepticism and builds trust. Avis said "we're number 2" and then followed with "we try harder."

## Brand discipline: sacrifice, line extension, and perspective

- **Sacrifice:** winning requires saying no to customers, features and markets. "You have to give something up to stand for something." Domino's gave up menu variety to win on one promise: delivery in 30 minutes or less.
- **Line extension:** trying to be everything to everyone ruins the brand, and stretching a name across many products weakens its identity. Launch a new brand instead. Heinz failed at U.S. baby food because no one trusted a ketchup brand with infants.
- **Perspective:** marketing wins and losses compound over time. A short-term win like a price cut often causes long-term brand damage. Discounting boosts sales briefly but trains customers never to pay full price.

## Strategy and execution: singularity, unpredictability, resources

- **Singularity:** one bold move usually wins, not a dozen scattered attacks. Success comes from strategic precision. His analogy: the Allies didn't win WW2 with 10 small attacks, they won because of Normandy.
- **Unpredictability:** you can't forecast the future, so build agility into plans and assume change is coming. Five-year plans in fast-moving industries are "delusional".
- **Resources:** even brilliant ideas need money to survive. Without dollars behind it, even the best marketing will fail.

## Ego, failure, hype, and trends

- **Success:** success breeds ego, and ego leads to failure. The moment you believe your brand is invincible, it starts dying. Coca-Cola launched "New Coke" out of arrogance and nearly killed itself.
- **Failure:** failure is part of marketing, and "the only sin is pretending it's not". IBM exited its copier business instead of trying to save it.
- **Hope:** the louder the hype, the less substance there usually is. Real success grows quietly. Ford's Edsel got massive pre-launch buzz and then flopped.
- **Acceleration:** trends build slowly and last, while fads explode and die. Microwaves were a trend, pet rocks a fad.
