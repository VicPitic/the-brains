## Time cost vs. purchase price

- Sam says the real cost of owning something is "maintenance hours, not purchase price."
- He has owned "cars, houses, other stuff." He now owns little: he rents and has no car.
- He says he is happier with fewer possessions.

## What owning stuff really costs

- Each owned thing adds a recurring to-do list. Sam names the chores: maintain, fix, register, renew, insure, update, subscribe.
- His test for any purchase is therefore the ongoing hours it demands, not the sticker price.

## Renting instead of buying a home

- Sam gets criticized often for not owning a home ("You're throwing so much money away!").
- He is unsure the criticism is right. He says he thinks he "makes more money now" by not owning stuff, though he doesn't give figures.
- What he knows for certain is that his mind is lighter and his life more convenient.

## Takeaways

- Count a purchase's ongoing maintenance hours alongside its price.
- Fewer possessions means fewer obligations and less mental load.
- Sam weighs convenience and mental lightness above the "rent is wasted money" argument, and says owning less may also have helped his income.
