## The "overnight success" myth and the 5-10 year apprenticeship

- In 2013, Sam wrote that "overnight success" is "complete nonsense" for most people.
- The internet has produced a few lottery winners who went from $0 to $1B quickly, but he argues most people who reach monetary success needed decades.
- His claim: "Not everyone goes from idea to IPO in months. Behind (nearly) every success is years, and years of hard work."
- Sam presents this as an early-20s view from his old blog, The Anti-MBA, and keeps the post up for nostalgic reasons.

## Mastery and apprenticeships

- Sam became obsessed with apprenticeships after reading *Mastery* by Robert Greene.
- His summary of the book: successful people and "geniuses" are people who mastered their craft, and mastery requires intense study for 5 to 10 years, which is essentially an apprenticeship.
- Greene illustrates this with the stories of Darwin, Einstein and Ford.
- Takeaway Sam draws: treat the first 5 to 10 years of a career as a deliberate apprenticeship, not as a delay before success.

## The CEO Doc: evidence of how long success takes

- Sam built a Google spreadsheet he calls "The CEO Doc" that shows how long it took well-known founders and CEOs to become "overnight successes".
- Its purpose is to prove the point with data rather than assertion.
- Observations he draws from it:
  - Andrew Mason and Jeff Bezos "skyrocketed to success" (the fastest cases in the doc).
  - Jack Dorsey has been programming for a long, long time before his success.
  - "The magic age to start something amazing looks to be 27."
- Takeaway Sam draws: even the apparent fast risers had a long runway of prior work, and the typical starting age for the breakout venture sits in the late 20s, not the early 20s.
