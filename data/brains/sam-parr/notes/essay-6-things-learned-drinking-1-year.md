## Quitting alcohol: Sam's one-year results (2014 essay, reaffirmed 2017)

- Sam wrote this in July 2014 on his old blog, The Anti-MBA, after one year sober. In a 2017 update he says everything in it "is still true but 10x" and that he still doesn't drink.
- He frames it as personal, not a campaign: he's fine with others drinking, still goes to bars, and says very little of it is scientific.
- He had stopped for personal reasons and planned a longer post on the effects on his relationships, finances, body and mind.

## Drinking made him gain weight; exercise reversed it

- He gained about 35 pounds after starting to drink. He attributes it to more than the calories from thousands of calories of beer a week.
- He was a college runner and always fit, but stopped working out once the drinking started because he was too hungover or drunk.
- Drunk eating compounded it: White Castle, greasy pizza at 3 AM, "late night burritos". A pounding headache plus regret killed motivation.
- After a few months sober he started working out again and the weight came off. He was running again at the time of writing.

## Exercise changed his mood, confidence and work ethic

- Beyond losing "Drunk Sam weight", the mental effects were the best part: mood up "by a 1000%", happier, more alert, more confident.
- He noticed a better work ethic: working hard physically and mentally started to feel good.
- By his account, if he goes a day without working out he feels like crap.

## Confidence from not drinking made him more attractive to people

- His biggest fear about sobriety was dating: that women would think he was a freak or a religious nut.
- He relied on booze for "false confidence". After a few months working on his mindset, he was confident enough to tell dates he doesn't drink.
- Women liked him more because he "stuck to my guns and believed in something". That raised his confidence further, a reinforcing loop.
- It wasn't only women: male friends wanted to hang out more, and strangers seemed drawn to him.

## Sober fun is harder but better, with no hangover cost

- He couldn't remember the last fun night without a hangover. For years he avoided committing to Saturday or Sunday morning plans because he'd be drunk or hungover.
- Having fun sober took work, but done right it felt like "putting on glasses for the first time after years of poor eyesight".
- Going to bed happy and waking up still happy, and not having made drunk decisions, felt great.

## Facing hard moments sober, and controlling emotions

- He used to drink before big emotional events (mourning, weddings, meeting or breaking up with someone) to avoid facing reality. Facing them sober felt like an adrenaline rush.
- Example: leaving his job to run Hustle Con. He felt every emotion, including the fear and stress, and was excited to get out of bed.
- He now embraces obstacles because he likes feeling the stress, pain and achievement of overcoming them: "Feeling stuff is a total rush."
- His claim: "When you control your emotions you control your reality." Drunk Sam had no control and blurred perception.
- Sober, he says he hadn't been angry once in about 8 months. When something bad happens he steps back, analyzes what's going on and how he feels, then decides, which makes him less impulsive.
