## Syed Balkhi's path from blogger to capital allocator

- Syed Balkhi started a blog called WP Beginner about WordPress, writing about plugins and how-tos, and it started getting traffic.
- After a few years he began buying WordPress plugins, and "it starts working."
- After roughly 10-13 years he owned something like 30-40 WordPress plugins.
- Sam's account of the outcome: the company does over $100m in revenue and is worth north of $1 billion. It is fully bootstrapped, with no funding or loans, and Syed owns all of it.
- Syed is only 32 and is an immigrant who wasn't wealthy.
- Sam spoke with him on My First Million and called him "one of the most impressive businessmen I've ever chatted with."

## Why discipline and a capital allocator mindset impressed Sam

- What stood out to Sam was how disciplined Syed was.
- Syed described going from internet founder to capital allocator, and Sam found that "inspiring."
- Sam says Syed's example pushed him to "level up" and focus more on a capital allocator mindset.
- Sam doesn't claim to have made that shift yet. He says he is past the initial scrappy stage: he has "some money, success, and an audience," but admits he hasn't always been great at leveling up.

## Creative versus disciplined: where each fits

- Sam describes himself as more of a creative in business and "not super disciplined." He thinks of ideas, does them, and "sometimes they work." He follows his gut.
- He argues there is a place for that approach: "I don't think you can Excel sheet your way to initial traction. At least I can't."
- His reasoning is that early traction requires inventing "a better mousetrap," and that takes creativity.
- His implied sequence: creativity and gut find the initial traction, then a later stage calls for discipline and capital allocation.
- Sam treats being past the initial-traction stage as the reason to level up toward the more disciplined mindset.
