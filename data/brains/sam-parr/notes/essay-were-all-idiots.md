## The "opposite advice also works" principle

- Sam's one takeaway from 10 years and 800+ conversations with successful founders, plus hundreds of books on how to be successful: "We're all idiots."
- For any single piece of advice from a successful person, assume you can still win by doing the opposite.
- He applies this beyond business, to health, relationships and any other area with a guru.
- Famous Silicon Valley advice ("Do things that don't scale," "Build what the customer wants," "Culture eats strategy for breakfast") can raise the odds of success, but you can do the opposite and still be successful.
- His evidence is pairs of conflicting quotes from successful people he interviewed on My First Million.

## Focus vs. doing many things

Sam's examples of successful people who disagree on focus:

- Ryan Smith (Qualtrics, sold to SAP for $8 billion cash in 2018): "I didn't do anything else... I was literally like Qualtrics for 20 years. Heads down."
- Tom Freston (founded MTV, CEO of Viacom): be a niche network, "do one thing all the time, do it really well."
- Chris Koerner (about $3 million a year from six or seven side businesses): "focus is overrated because compounding doesn't care."
- John Morgan (Morgan & Morgan, says it brings in $2.4 billion a year): people who tell you to focus are also hunting, fishing and golfing.
- Joe Lonsdale (Palantir, 8VC with $13.4 billion): his partners can work on up to six things at once, but an operator should do "one or two at most."

## Funding, hours and goals

**Raise VC vs. never raise**
- DHH (37signals, profitable every year for 25 years, "tens of millions" in annual profit): "We never raised VC... We always had to earn the money we wanted to spend first."
- Brian Halligan (HubSpot, about $11.7 billion): "you should raise a lot so you can kind of make it through."
- Wade Foster (Zapier, $5 billion): "seed strapped," about a million raised through YC.

**Grind vs. a 40-hour week**
- Dharmesh Shah (HubSpot): you can outthink him, but "you will not out grind me, you will not outwork me." He ties outcome quality to the number of iterations.
- Mark Pincus (Zynga): "I'm not stressed enough. I miss the level of intensity."
- MrBeast: goes "as hard as I can, every ounce of my entire body until I just crash."
- Rob Dyrdek (says $350 million net worth): "I work less now than I've ever worked... about a 40 hour week."
- Jack Smith (Vungle, reportedly sold to Blackstone for $750 million): "balance is important and not having tunnel vision."

**Big goal vs. no goals**
- Sam himself: "I had a goal to make $20 million liquid by the age of 30."
- Graham Weaver (Alpine Investors, says $18.7 billion managed) wrote his goals down daily, multiple times a day, in high school.
- Jason Fried (37signals): "I've never believed in goals."
- Dharmesh Shah: "I tend not to have explicit goals."

## Money, risk and trading

**Never risk ruin vs. not afraid to lose it all**
- Jason Fried: "I'll take risks, but I don't take risks that put me at risk."
- Syed Balkhi (Awesome Motive, bootstrapped, $100 million+): be very conservative because "I came from nothing. I don't want to go back to that."
- Guy Spier: set things up so that when he misses badly he is still okay.
- Rohan Oza (Vitaminwater marketer, Glacéau sold to Coca-Cola for $4.1 billion): "you have to be willing to lose if you're going to win."
- Craig Fuller (Firecrown, says a $60 million revenue run rate this year): "I'm not afraid of losing it all. And I know that I can get it back."

**Money buys happiness vs. it doesn't**
- Scott Galloway (founded L2, sold to Gartner for $155 million): "all this bullshit that money doesn't buy you happiness... that is a myth."
- Andrew Huberman: "money can't buy happiness, but it absolutely can buffer stress."

**Pick stocks vs. stop trading**
- Barry Ritholtz (Ritholtz Wealth, about $9.4 billion): index funds are best. "Put the phone down. Stop trading."
- Lloyd Blankfein (12 years as Goldman Sachs CEO): holds "10% in ETFs and 90% in single stocks because that's what I like to do."

## Team, speed and growth tactics

**Remote vs. co-located**
- Wade Foster (Zapier): never had an office, "way before anyone thought that was normal." A 2021 secondary valued Zapier at $5 billion.
- Alex Hormozi (Acquisition.com, says portfolio companies do $250 million+ a year): serious people in "the business Olympics" will be together and co-located.

**Move faster vs. outlast**
- Grant LaFontaine (Whatnot, $11.5 billion valuation in 2025): "If you have time to even think about it, you're moving too slow."
- Laird Hamilton (Laird Superfood, about $62 million raised in its 2020 IPO): "victory through attrition."

**Passion vs. talent**
- Tony Hawk: never considered quitting skating because he never started it for money.
- Scott Galloway: "Don't find your passion, find your talent and then commit to developing mastery."

**Spend on ads vs. never spend $1**
- Alex Lieberman (Morning Brew, sold for hundreds of millions): "my only regret is we couldn't figure out a way... to put more money into Facebook ads."
- Kevin Ryan (Business Insider, sold at a $442 million valuation): at 300 million uniques, "never spent $1 in advertising."

## Admiration and building for your family

- In his 20s Sam became obsessed with putting in work today that makes his future self proud, and says it drove his success.
- With a family, he now asks what he can do today that makes his future family proud, even something small like skateboarding home with balloons for his kids' birthday.
- He treats admiration as a quality to look for in the people you do life with. He admires his wife (he knew he wanted to marry her on a third date when she handled his kidney stone) and his co-founder Joe, whom he trusts so much that on certain topics he feels bad for whoever Joe is up against.
- His aim: on certain topics, he wants people to think "this guy's a f\*cking animal!"

- Hampton (his company) is building Core groups in 18 cities, and its point is access to good advice. Miami and Boston Core groups need an additional member.
- At a Hampton event he met a founder doing $20m a year with an app and 5 employees. He invites readers to send "I met a guy who..." business stories.

- Sam cites a chart on smoking (about 4,300 cigarettes per adult in 1963, less than half that by the early 2000s) and asks what will cause the same drop for cell phones and what year we're in.
- Sam flags S&P 500 annualized returns (3 years +22.9%, 10 years +15.3%, 30 years +10.3%, 50 years +11.9%) as running hot versus the long-run norm of about 10-12%, and notes the last time was the late 1990s before the dotcom crash.
- Robert Greene's point, which Sam shares: advances of $20,000-$50,000 with a year to finish force writers to rush, so the work suffers.
