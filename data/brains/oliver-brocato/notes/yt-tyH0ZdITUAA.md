## Oliver's path into e-commerce

- Oliver started at 14 selling fidget spinners online. He was one of the first to list them on eBay and Etsy.
- He moved into drop shipping, including booty-lifting leggings that blew up on TikTok.
- He then started Tabs Chocolate, an aphrodisiac chocolate that splits in half so each partner gets a half.
- He scaled Tabs from 0 to 11 million a year, dropped out of college, and sold the business.
- Counterfeits, copycats and unauthorized resellers "almost bankrupted the company multiple times." That pain point led him to build Bustem after the exit.

## The universal truth about scaling an e-commerce brand

- Oliver's rule: "if you're in e-commerce and you start making money, people start trying to rip your [stuff]."
- The threats he saw:
  - TikTok Shop sellers.
  - Chinese sellers on Temu, Alibaba and Shein.
  - Resellers on eBay, Walmart, Etsy and Amazon stealing the buy box.
  - Drop shippers and black hat operators who clone a site one-to-one and bid on the brand keyword, hijacking branded search traffic.
- He found his first dupe when his mom texted him an ad for one. He describes it as butterflies, fight or flight, and a knot in the stomach.
- Visible copycats are "the tip of the iceberg." If a founder sees a couple, Oliver says he can almost guarantee there are many more and that revenue is being lost.

## Why small copycats still hurt: death by a thousand paper cuts

- One junk listing alone won't dent a business. Spread across Amazon, TikTok Shop, Temu, Walmart, eBay, Etsy and standalone sites, together they become "a parasite" that drains energy, dollars and resources.
- Some Bustem clients are hit by thousands of bad actors a month. It is "whack-a-mole": take five down and three pop back up.
- Revenue loss is only one cost:
  - Brand reputation is ruined when fakes get shipped or never shipped at all, which brings bad reviews.
  - Fake supplements can be dangerous.
- Example he cites: AG1 pulled its product off Amazon because so many dupes appeared. It then spent a year or two working with Amazon lawyers and authorities before relisting recently. Oliver's point is the opportunity cost of letting the problem get out of hand: "you don't want to be the next AG1."
- Some competitors began as rippers. They copy your images, ads and website, make money, then invest in their own photos, site and ads. Taking no action gives them runway and money to become full competitors, "and by the time that happens, you have no recourse."
- Analogy he uses: "Would you let people walk into your warehouse, take products out and just walk right out?" Copycats do the same thing virtually.
- Bad actors behave like bullies. If you create enough friction and stand up to them, they move on to an easier target.

## Why lawyers and cease-and-desist letters are the wrong first tool

- Positioning: Bustem is not a law firm and not licensed lawyers. Oliver calls it "the cheaper sister or cousin of the law firm" and "the first line of defense" before paying someone $500 or $1,000 an hour.
- A cease and desist "will get thrown out" by bad actors.
- Litigation takes years and can cost hundreds of thousands of dollars, and the defendant can declare bankruptcy, leaving you "holding the bag."
- Using a bazooka on one small seller is wasteful. It makes sense only if someone is stealing millions. For 300 small infringers you can't file 300 lawsuits.
- Many in-house general counsels and law firms work with Bustem. They hand off the "bottom of the barrel" volume work and focus on the high-leverage cases.
- Oliver is clear it is not a silver bullet. The target is a 70 to 80% removal rate, and he says the rest can go to a lawyer.

## The enforcement approach: attack the infrastructure, not the bad guy

- Sending a letter to the bad actor himself gets thrown out.
- Bustem goes after the infrastructure that supports the bad actor: the domain, the registrar, the hosting provider, the payment processor and the platform.
- Reasoning: those parties are white hat players who don't want to be liable or "holding the bag." Bustem shows them proof that the customer is a bad actor and says that continuing to support them creates liability for them too.
- About 80% of enforcement is automated. AI agents fill out abuse forms and report inboxes at registrars, hosts, payment processors and platforms.
- Bustem also gets sites delisted from Google and flagged so visitors see a warning that the site isn't authorized.
- Reported success rate: 70 to 80% of targets crippled or removed from the internet.

## How Bustem's detection works, end to end

1. Onboarding: the brand fills out questions, uploads documents, and signs a power of attorney so Bustem can enforce on its behalf. It connects Shopify, social accounts and Amazon storefront.
2. Asset bank: Bustem scrapes the brand's images, keywords, product titles and brand search terms.
3. Daily searches: thousands of searches a day across platforms and social channels. These include reverse image search, frame-by-frame breakdown of videos, product titles, brand names and keyword combinations.
4. AI triage: each search returns a huge number of results. AI analysis and cross-image comparison find the one-to-one matches among them.
5. Human review: a team of 30+ identification analysts manually reviews what the AI and automation surface.
6. Client approval: the brand clicks "bust them" to approve or denies each one. Denials ask for a reason, such as not interested or an authorized reseller that goes on the whitelist. Approvals and denials feed machine learning and train the analysts to surface more relevant results.
7. Enforcement: after approval, Bustem works the takedown via the infrastructure route above.
8. The scan reruns every day, so it keeps surfacing net-new and repeat infringers who pop back up.

## The hybrid software plus human service model

- Oliver describes the product as hybrid: AI agents and automation plus a human team of identification analysts, enforcement agents, a team lead, a client success manager and a head of brand protection.
- Every client gets its own Slack channel. Oliver's insight: clients don't want to log into a platform or have homework. They "just want these bad guys taken down."
- Weekly automations post into the Slack channel: what was found, actions taken, removals, and the economic value regained. Bustem calculates how much revenue the removed bad actors were doing.
- This delivers "white glove agency" service on a productized, efficient backend, which is how Oliver says it works at scale and cost-effectively.
- Pricing: about 170 brands as clients, including Comfort Clothing, Oats Overnight, Primal Queen and Neurogum. The average retainer is about $3,000 a month. Oliver says a hit team, expert software and everything else for that price is very affordable, and that he may eventually take a percentage of the economic value recovered.

## Who should buy, and how to sell it

- Fit: Oliver says a brand should be doing more than $100,000 a month. Below that, "you got bigger problems to worry about." He says to get back "in the ad account" and scale rather than invest in trademarks early. Elsewhere in the interview he says Bustem works with brands doing more than a million a year that see some sort of problem.
- Sales motion: lead with value, not a sales call. A prospect books a 20 to 30 minute call. Before the call, Bustem runs a full 360-degree threat scan across social platforms, marketplaces and the wider internet.
- On the call it walks through the findings, the removal plan and the process. The audit is free and the prospect keeps it whether or not they hire Bustem. If the problem isn't bad enough, Oliver says Bustem will tell them to come back when it is.
- Customers' own counter-ads act as proof of demand. Brands like Santa Cruz Medicinals and Underbrush run ads telling customers not to be scammed by fakes. Oliver says seeing those is "fuel and reassurance" that he is solving a real problem.
- Counterfeit ads often run aggressively at the bottom of the funnel, so a hypers-scaling copycat can capture a brand's hot traffic and force a brand to descale. Oliver's view: counterfeiters are parasites that suck dollars out of an advertiser's account.
