## Building a $10M-run-rate Shopify chocolate brand (TABS): the origin and product

- Oliver went from zero to about $3M in revenue in the first year selling one product. By the time of the video he had about $2M year to date and an $8-10M run rate for the coming year.
- He says he is "no different" from the audience: not smarter, no higher IQ, he "just put in the work."
- The idea came from his partner, who found a TikTok about a sex chocolate with about 8 million views and 2 million likes. The brand had no social media and no website. Oliver saw large potential because marketing was his skill set.
- His view: "it really wasn't too much about the product but more so the potential to market it."
- Development took about a year to build the supply chain and formulation. They did not reinvent anything: they took popular, effective natural supplements that usually come as pills or powders and turned them into a luxury chocolate experience that tastes good.
- Contrast with gas-station pills, which people are afraid to talk about or admit to using. TABS flips that by being openly branded and giftable.
- Branding and packaging are what acquire customers; taste is what brings them back ("if the chocolate isn't good... they're never going to come back").
- Product details: a chocolatier and food scientist balanced efficacy with taste, and the chocolate uses a custom polycarbonate virgin mold for its shape.
- He stresses an operator has to own every detail: photography, website design and development, marketing, customer support.

## From influencer collabs to a retainer-based creator model

- Launch was December 20, 2021. They sold out of inventory in the first three weeks.
- The influencer-driven strategy was not sustainable: each influencer post spiked sales, then sales fell back to near zero.
- Problems with influencer sourcing:
  - Hard to scale: of every 1,000 outreach emails, only 10-20 responded.
  - About 90% of responders asked exorbitant rates that were not profitable.
  - Of those who accepted, about half took the product and ghosted.
  - So a huge outreach net was needed to land a single working influencer.
- The switch: hire content creators on retainer instead of doing influencer collabs.
  - His first creator was paid a flat fee to make one TikTok a day for 30 days. That creator became one of his best friends.
  - That first 30 days generated roughly $60-80K in sales.
- Scaling it: rather than putting many creators on one account, he created new accounts and gave each creator their own independent account, hiring talented creators to run them.
- Core insight: "TikTok democratized the ability to go viral." It is about whether you can make content that hits the For You page, not follower count. You no longer pay someone a large amount to access their audience.
- Result: nearly all marketing for the first year to year and a half was 100% organic, with almost no paid ads (early ad accounts got banned within minutes).

## Viral spikes, the stockout mistake, and losing momentum

- On January 15, 2022, a creator (Mossy Wolf) posted a TikTok about TABS that got over 6.6M views and over 2M likes in one day. That video drove over $50K in revenue.
- Month two revenue was about $280K, much of it pre-orders.
- The fatal mistake: the chocolate was made in the US but the packaging was made in China. To save money they shipped a full container by boat instead of air-freighting the packaging.
- The container was badly delayed and they were out of product for about three months. They lost all momentum and sales slowly declined.
- They restocked in April. Oliver assumed he could resume at the prior level, and says that was a mistake. His lesson: "this is a game of momentum."
- Takeaways he draws: do not trade supply-chain reliability for freight savings on a one-product brand, and momentum from virality is hard to regain once inventory runs out.

## The 40% revenue drop and the rebuild

- After the stockout he moved to Miami and went all-in on the business, then in August revenue dropped 40% when TikTok started banning all their accounts and videos. He thought it was "game over."
- At the same time his co-founder disappeared and went back to school despite their pledge to take a gap year together. Oliver moved back home, which he calls one of the hardest moments of his life.
- What turned it around:
  - He rebuilt his health habits: eating clean, going to the gym, running.
  - He decided he had to build a real team instead of operating alone.
  - He hired help for email and SMS, conversion rate optimization (CRO) and creatives.
  - That setup went into Q4 holiday season, Black Friday and Cyber Monday, and sales "started to really take form and started to explode."
- His view on mindset: people say go into monk mode and hustle, but "if you're not happy with life... what is the point?" He says his well-being is tightly tied to business performance. He booked a one-way ticket to Europe and traveled, and says things improved across the board afterward.

## Team and channels that drove the $2M year

- Oliver credits a "team of Assassins" as the reason TABS is successful.
- After the rebuild they started spending hundreds of thousands of dollars on:
  - Instagram memes and Twitter memes
  - Bigger influencers
  - Revamping the creator and influencer operation
  - Photo shoots
  - A full site redesign
  - A conversion rate optimization team
- He describes the result as taking off "like a rocket ship": about $2M year to date and an $8-10M run rate.

## Growth plan: international, new channels, paid ads, product extensions

Three goals Oliver lays out for the coming months:

1. **Go international.** More than 40% of site traffic comes from outside the US, but TABS only sells domestically.
2. **Diversify beyond the DTC site.**
   - Amazon and Etsy.
   - Wholesale and retail: smoke shops, sex stores, convenience stores, gas stations.
   - Break into paid advertising, since nearly all marketing so far was organic.
3. **Build new product lines and extensions.** TABS is a one-product brand (a dark chocolate sex chocolate), so he wants new flavors, new "moods" and new products. His stated ambition is for it to be "the one-stop shop" in the category.
