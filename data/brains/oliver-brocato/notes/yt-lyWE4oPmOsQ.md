## Oliver's organic growth model: three distribution pillars

- Oliver says he is 21, expects about $10 million in revenue this year, and did over 500 million views in 2022. He expects to exceed 1 billion this year, with no paid ads.
- He credits an organic strategy for most of his success: "three pillars". These are content creators, memes and influencers.
- The goal is reach, website visitors and sales without paying Facebook or running pay-per-click.
- He cites peers using TikTok organically: a slime brand doing more than $20 million in sales, and a brand called Mini Katanas that sells swords through TikTok.

## TikTok: build a content studio, not one account

- Oliver calls TikTok a "blue ocean": for the first time you can reach millions of people for little to no money. He uses it as top-of-funnel brand awareness.
- He works with user-generated-content (UGC) creators, not big-follower influencers. Each creator gets a brand-owned account on which they are the "Mastermind" with free rein.
- Once one creator works, he repeats it with more creators from different backgrounds and angles. The aim is "a content studio with 400 different creators on your retainer", not one TikTok page.
- His reasoning: "no one can tell you what video is going to go viral", so the only way to win is scale.
- His examples include accounts run by a grandma, a college kid and a prankster. He compares himself to a news channel: he doesn't try to pick the needle, he grabs "the whole entire haystack".
- Formula: quantity × quality = success. He says most people over-focus on quality. If you've made fewer than 500 videos, you haven't given organic TikTok a fair shot.
- Quality should come from the creators, not the founder. Don't micromanage with scripts. Hire talent, cut low performers and double down on people who produce views.
- Account names don't matter. He runs several differently named brand accounts and says viewers don't remember them, and TikTok's base is over a billion users. If an account gets banned or shadow-banned, he makes a new one, and says a fresh account can even perform better early on.

## Creator pay structure: small retainer plus 20% affiliate

- He hires as many creators as he can on a recurring model, not one-post deals.
- Pay is a hybrid: a very small base retainer plus a large affiliate share, 20% of all sales the creator generates.
- Reason: it aligns incentives. If pay isn't tied to performance, a creator can drive 2 million views and only $100 in sales with no reason to care.
- Principle: "pay out as little money as possible and as big of a rep share as you can afford. When they win you win."

## The one-two punch: viral video, then reply videos that sell

- Punch one: make a controversial or attention-grabbing video that isn't about selling or converting. The goal is eyeballs and top-of-funnel awareness.
- Punch two: once a video is viral, make videos replying to comments on it. You can make as many reply videos as you want.
- Reply videos are where you "sell hard". List features, benefits and value propositions, say what separates you from competitors, and address friction points and pain points.
- If there aren't enough valid comments, he says to create comments about the points you want to address.

## Sourcing creators and recycling content through affiliates

- Finding creators: search Twitter and LinkedIn for "UGC", "user generated content", "UGC creator" and "TikTok content creator". Also use your own network, and DM people whose content you like while scrolling.
- Creators with under 5,000 followers tend to be more excited to work with you.
- Reposting: Oliver has more than 2,000 affiliates in a Discord, each with their own Instagram and YouTube accounts. They repost his most viral videos as Reels and Shorts across hundreds or thousands of accounts. Some posts hit about 1-5 million views, and reposters are paid on affiliate commission too.

## Meme pages: rent distribution by the day

- Pillar two is meme and theme pages on Instagram.
- He pays page owners $100, $300 or $500 for a 24-hour post. Owners change the link in their bio to his website for 24 hours.
- He turns his viral content into memes for this. The meme account's owned audience replaces paying Facebook or pay-per-click for traffic.

## Micro influencers: pay for distribution, work at scale

- Pillar three is influencers, and he prefers micro influencers with roughly 20,000 to 200,000 followers. He wants their distribution network and content that might hit the For You page, not their brand name or status.
- Difficulties he lists: many ignore cold emails and DMs, many demand unaffordable deal structures, and some take the product and disappear. So you have to cast a wide net.
- Rules:
  - Scale is everything. He says to work with 300 influencers, not three: "you worked with three influencers... work with 300".
  - Don't go through an agency or manager, because you'll get ripped off and it won't be profitable.
  - Don't script it. Creators know how to speak to their audience. Only after they show a finished video should you tweak it so it converts as well as gets views.
