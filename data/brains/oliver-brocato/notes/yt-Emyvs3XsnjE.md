## Why Oliver builds in public and how he thinks about a personal brand

- Oliver separates himself from the "get rich" content crowd. He says people pumping videos like "how to make your first $10,000" are running a content business: ad revenue, sponsorships, affiliate deals, or selling a course.
- He states he is "not a content creator" or an influencer, has "no stake in this game," and is not trying to make money off his audience. He frames his content as chronicling what he's doing and showing what's possible.
- He admits it is partly selfish. He started building in public 3 years ago with his e-commerce brand Tops, and says it changed his life through the people, advisers, investors, friends and network it opened up.
- Example of the payoff: he can tweet "I need a Google ads guy," "my Facebook ad account got banned," or "if anybody knows a good designer hit me up" and within seconds has scores of messages from people offering to help.
- His principle: "by establishing a personal brand you increase your surface area for luck and opportunity like nothing else."

## His 2024 situation and the test he set himself

- He describes a chaotic year behind him: he left school, moved around (Airbnbs, country to country), with many ups and downs.
- He now feels stabilized: money in the bank, a home, a routine, a strong team, resources and the skill set. He calls it "the lottery ticket."
- The pressure that comes with it: with every piece in place, "there are no excuses" if he doesn't perform. He sees it as a test of whether he can execute, and says it is his duty to go all in.
- He is working on two things: his personal brand and a product called Study Buddy.

## Scaling the personal brand into a media company

- The personal brand (YouTube, Twitter, Instagram) has become a real business, cash flowing over $40,000 a month.
- Plan: scale it and expand to new platforms and audiences, including:
  - Long-form YouTube (this video was his first long-form video, shot raw while walking down the street).
  - More short-form video.
  - A newsletter.
  - Possibly in-person events.
- Stated goal: turn "Oliver B", described as "a dude with a phone" on Twitter, into "a more legit media company."

## Study Buddy: what it is and where it stands

- Study Buddy is an AI Chrome extension for students. A button is injected into an online assignment page (open-notes quizzes, homework). The student clicks it, the extension scrapes the question, runs it through AI, and returns the answer plus an explanation of why it's correct.
- Launched only a couple of months before the video. It scaled from zero to over 2,500 paid users.
- Current revenue: about $25,000 MRR. His goal is $250,000 MRR by end of year, which he calls "really, really possible."
- Ad spend to date: about $112,000 total, producing over $160,000 in revenue.

## The plateau: ad bans, no paid channels, and organic growth

- Paid acquisition is blocked: Facebook is rejecting the ads, TikTok accounts are suspended, and Snapchat won't let them publish creatives. They have been unable to spend on marketing.
- MRR has sat at about $25k for 2-3 months. He says he's growth-oriented and gets frustrated and "exasperated" without visible upward movement.
- Despite that, the product grows 3-7% month over month organically, even with churn. His read: the product works, people love it, and they tell friends, so it has "huge legs."
- Work done during the plateau: many improvements to the landing page, the product and the user experience, with no change in the numbers.

## Testing acquisition channels and his mindset on the hard part

- Current approach is to find channels that work, then pump spend behind them. Channels he's testing:
  - Meme pages.
  - Influencers at every size: micro, macro, and people with millions of followers.
  - TikTokers, Instagrammers and YouTubers.
  - Different paid ad platforms.
- His view on stage: "right now is literally the hardest part." Once something is working, it's easy to get to a 2, 5, 10, even 100 exit. At the moment nothing is working and there is no momentum.
- Metaphor he uses: the woodpecker hitting the tree. They've hit it about a hundred times and it only takes the 101st, 102nd or 103rd for it to crack. He says he can feel they are near the boiling point and that the key is patience.
