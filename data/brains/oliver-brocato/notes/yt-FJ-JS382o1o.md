## Oliver's repost-everywhere growth system for a viral-by-design product

- Before launching Tabs, Oliver and his cofounder Jake had a leggings drop-shipping brand that blew up on TikTok. The market saturated quickly as copycats came in.
- Their reset criteria were a product that can go viral and sell itself, and a product with high barriers to entry and a proprietary nature. Oliver says they agonized over branding, design, unboxing and the website before launch.
- Pay-per-click is "pretty linear": spend a set amount per purchase with little chance of exponential return. A cheap influencer post can pull a lot of views and sales.
- Oliver says the product is about 50% of the viral equation, and the creator the other 50%. Their influencers usually get more views on the paid Tabs post than on their normal organic content, which he uses as a pitch to creators.
- Tabs launched around December 20, 2021, with a few influencer posts and no big traction at first. About two weeks later Jake built the repost strategy.
- Result: Tabs grew without paid ads, with over 60 million views at the time of recording. Oliver says they do not run pay-per-click on Facebook, Google or Snapchat. Retargeting for abandoned carts and checkouts is the first paid move planned.

## Repost playbook: one TikTok, many platforms

- Posts started as 2 posts per micro-influencer deal. They downloaded each video as an HD file without the watermark and reposted it on Instagram Reels, Facebook Reels, Spotlight and YouTube Shorts.
- The logo is removed because Oliver says platforms may restrict content that carries the TikTok logo. Natively uploaded reposts took off on brand-new accounts with no following.
- Virtual assistants posted hundreds of times a day. Oliver's logic: spam the platforms so one or two get picked up by the algorithm, then analyze what works and double down.
- This produced tens of millions of views on secondary repost accounts and meaningful revenue.
- Facebook Reels was an early win. One native Facebook Reel hit about 9 million views and drove a large number of sales in a day. Oliver says almost no brands post natively there.
- YouTube Shorts is the best short-form channel after TikTok. They run roughly 15 to 20 accounts, some pulling 10,000 to 20,000 views per video per day.
- Philosophy: "I can't tell you what video is going to go viral... but if I have 30 to 50 videos going live every single day and the quality is at least at a certain baseline, I know for sure that one will pop off." Oliver sums it up: "it's not about picking the needle in the haystack, it's about buying the whole haystack."

## Creator franchises: retainers plus subsidiary TikTok pages

- Pain point: securing an influencer collab takes lots of effort and time. Oliver says nine out of ten influencers ghost you and keep the product even when you offer to pay on posting. It was very hard to scale at the velocity he wanted with VAs and interns managing it.
- Fix: put good creators on retainer to post on a recurring basis on their own pages. Then run subsidiary pages on TikTok, each managed by an independent creator.
- Searching "tabs chocolate" on TikTok shows about 25 brand pages. Operators include TikTokers, drop shippers, content creators, agencies, OnlyFans creators and college kids. Oliver says it does not matter who they are.
- Target: about 50 posts a day live on TikTok and about 50 a day across reposting platforms, "basically becoming a media conglomerate."
- Each creator gets a special link in their bio so Tabs can track sales impact. Top performers can become partners on a rev-share deal.
- Oliver frames them as "100% franchisees". Many are Shopify e-commerce operators who previously ran TikTok pages for their own brands and now take a share of Tabs as a content side hustle.
- Why it works (a point he agrees with): giving creators ownership of a Tabs page locks in the distribution channel better than asking for one-off posts. Oliver notes you cannot run multiple brand pages like this on Facebook.

## Double down when a video pops

- When a video goes viral, they see it on the Shopify dashboard as a spike in visitors and sales, then text the team: where's the video, can we capitalize?
- Oliver says a follow-up video responding to a comment on the viral video, with a call to action, often outperforms the original despite getting about 1/10 to 1/20 of the views.
- When a creator or account pops, they start pumping more content on that account or have that influencer post more, to capture all the potential.
- Best single videos: a content creator's video with over 11 million views, and a micro-influencer in month one with 6.6 million views.

## Product and packaging engineered to make sense on camera

- Oliver says Jake's idea was a clean diagonal break on each chocolate square. Each partner breaks it and takes half, so the concept is explained in about three seconds of video without paragraphs of copy.
- Positioning: sexual-enhancement products traditionally target men or women and stress discretion and no shame. Tabs flipped that into a fun, romantic product for you and your partner, any gender combination. Oliver says it differentiates them from competitors like Sex Crush on camera.
- The idea came from a competitor, Sextz, whose video had about 11 million TikTok views but whose packaging and execution were poor. Jake and Oliver copied the concept and executed it better.
- Details were deliberate: the shade of gold, the back of the box, and emojis revealed when you remove the chocolate. Oliver says there were many arguments over every detail.
- Build: contract manufacturer in Naples, Florida; boxes made in China; virgin polycarbonate molds and plastic wrappers; multiple supplement partners. It took about 11 months and many formulation iterations to pack in natural supplements without hurting taste. Oliver tested samples on friends around his fraternity house.
- Unboxing and packaging are essential to building for virality, since they put people in the right frame of mind.

## Site, conversion and AOV

- Launch site was mobile only as an MVP, because traffic comes from TikTok, Snapchat and YouTube Shorts. Oliver says over 98% of traffic is mobile. A desktop site was in development at recording.
- They moved to pre-orders and stated transparently that delivery takes 4 to 12 weeks. Conversion rate dropped to about a third of what it had been. After catching up on pre-orders and removing the 4 to 12 week notice, conversion did not bounce back as he expected. They were reverse engineering why and A/B testing updates with the help of conversion rate optimization specialists.
- Pricing and AOV: a box (3 chocolates) sells for $30, with average order value in the mid $40s. They started with free shipping at 2 boxes to lift AOV.
- Test in progress: they removed the quantity selector because buyers mostly bought 1 or 2. It became a 1-box or 2-box option, defaulted to 2 boxes, with free shipping and a discount on the 2-box option to cut friction.

## Gifting lounge and where Tabs could go next

- Oliver paid about $7,500 to sponsor a gifting suite lounge tied to a big event (Super Bowl, Met Gala or Oscars). They gave product to celebrities and could use photos at their discretion. He says most attendees were B and C listers and an A-lister would not come for free product.
- Wholesale: they were setting up a wholesale system after a few inbound requests, with TikTok still the main channel. The next product moves are flavors and box sizes (a 1-unit box for wholesale or retail, a 9-unit box).
- Oliver's category ideas: be "the sex shop for Gen Z" by destigmatizing a taboo category; functional foods that pair moods, flavors and supplements with common foods; and, when legal, THC, CBD and psilocybin edibles. He says the infrastructure would let them corner that market.
- He says they are not focused on the long-term vision yet. The priority is handling demand, production schedules and back-end logistics first.
