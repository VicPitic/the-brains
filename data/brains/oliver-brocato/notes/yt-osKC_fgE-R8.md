## Oliver's path to Bustm and what the e-commerce years taught him

- He started in e-commerce at 14 selling fidget spinners. Along the way he did Instagram meme-page ad deals, a social media management business that became a marketing agency, influencer management for an app that raised $10 million in VC funding, and a drop-shipping store selling booty-lifting leggings.
- At 19 he started Tabs, a "viral sex chocolate": the most popular natural supplements, normally pills or powders, in a new format. He made a stigmatized, taboo category feel "very sexy and romantic."
- Tabs got billions of views on TikTok, Instagram and other channels, scaled to eight figures a year, and exited for "a nice cash out" around age 21-22.
- After Tabs he ran a ChatGPT-wrapper Chrome extension. It scaled nicely, but he wasn't passionate about it and couldn't see doing it long term.
- He started Bustm.com in January of the year before this interview (2024 or 2025, depending on timing; the source doesn't say which) and had 100+ brands by November 2025.
- He picked it because it solves a pain he lived with at Tabs. He calls it "productled growth" and "something that I wish that I had when I was running my business."

## How Bustm works

- Bustm is software plus a team of IP specialists and enforcement agents. It finds copycats and counterfeits that rip off e-commerce brands, then gets them taken down.
- **Detection:** the software scrapes a brand's images and keywords, runs thousands of searches, crawls the results and surfaces one-to-one matches, scanning every day.
- **Enforcement:** the team reports bad actors to the platform, the domain registrar and the payment processor, without spending "six figures and 3 years in court."
- **Why this works:** the bad guy won't stop if asked. The enablers are legitimate US companies (Stripe, Shopify, Amazon) that must act or risk being negligent and exposed to lawsuits or class actions. So Bustm goes after the enablers and goes "back and forth until we get them removed."
- **Approval step:** brands must approve each flagged target before enforcement, so Bustm doesn't accidentally report a competitor, supplier, whitelisted creator or authorized reseller.
- It's currently "half software half agency." The goal is to automate everything with AI agents.
- Brand-side harms he cites: duped websites, copied Meta ads, clones bidding on the brand's keywords, fake marketplace listings, stolen Amazon buy box, counterfeit or never-shipped products, and customers who get scammed and never buy from that brand again.
- One client's CPM dropped about 50% and Meta spent more on their creative after Bustm took down a bad actor bidding on the same keywords.
- Some takedowns have turned into clients: former drop shippers came back saying they now build real brands and get trademarks.

## Pricing: from retainer to performance plus monitoring fee

- **Original model:** unlimited monitoring and takedowns for $2,000-$5,000 a month, by brand size and infringer volume.
- **Problem:** at $3-5K a month, brands were very sensitive to results. Bustm only finds and reports; platforms decide whether to act, and they can be slow. Without strong wins, brands wanted to churn. This was worst early, before strong team, processes and SOPs. He says effectiveness is now "three or 4x" higher.
- **Per-kill idea:** he modeled it from one month of actual data (about $50-60K revenue from 20-25 retainers) to price per-takedown at $50-$200 so revenue would match. He first pitched it as a downsell when prospects balked at the retainer.
- **Result:** everyone said yes. He went from closing a couple of brands a month to one or more a day.
- **Problem:** with $0 to start, brands signed but never approved targets, so no takedowns happened. Roughly twice as many brands were on performance deals as retainers, and Bustm earned nothing from them.
- **Fix:** a call with every brand to add a monthly monitoring fee "just to cover our cost and bases." Most were receptive. Some left, which he welcomed as it weeded out tire kickers.
- **Current model:** $300 a month monitoring plus $50-$200 per takedown, alongside the $2-5K unlimited tier.
- **Lesson:** "If you're willing to pay something then you'll be willing to take stuff down."
- **Retention signal:** September had only about $3,000 in churn, which he calls "basically like zero churn."

## B2B distribution: founder brand, referrals and product quality

- Tabs ran on scaled creator and paid distribution: hundreds to thousands of creators posting daily, an army of 7,000 clippers, and sometimes hundreds of thousands of dollars a month on meme pages on Instagram and Twitter.
- For Bustm, he says clipping and creators don't work, and ads are tough to crack.
- Bustm grows from his personal brand, about 50,000 e-commerce entrepreneurs following him on X, plus word of mouth and referrals. "I am my marketing." His content covers his journey, case studies and success stories, such as saving brands hundreds of thousands or even millions of dollars by removing copycats.
- Inbound is now 3-5 booked calls a day from referrals, "found you through Oliver," or word of mouth, with no marketing spend.
- On B2B vs consumer: in e-commerce it's about ad creative and distribution; in B2B "how good is your [ __ ]" is the biggest growth hack, because e-com founders are friends and talk. A weak product means "a churn and burn operation."
- He prefers a business that can survive and grow on word of mouth over one where you "dump a dollar in to get a $1.20 out" and fight CPM increases and shifting market conditions. He says that kind of business compounds.
- Growth plans:
  - Hire a full-time ghostwriter who shadows him, joins meetings and produces content (not an agency or one tweet a day).
  - Cites Adam Robinson (retention.com, RB2B), who he says went from zero to $6 million ARR bootstrapped through LinkedIn and founder content.
  - Starting to test paid ads and other channels, and building a sales team (his first sales hire was brand new at the time).
- His principle: double down on what works.

## Selling B2B: long cycles and early-stage suffering

- Consumer Tabs sold at $29.99 to Gen Z impulse buyers off a TikTok video. B2B means higher price points, multiple stakeholders, finance and legal sign-off, and sales cycles of three weeks to multiple months.
- It is "hand-to-hand combat on every sale": emails, texts, calls, no-shows and reschedules.
- He took 600-700 sales calls himself in about eight months, and says it was humbling to go from running a multi-million-dollar brand to getting no-showed.
- He says every business has to "be bad first." Nobody starts with packaging, pricing and fulfillment dialed in, so B2B has an infancy stage you have to fight through.
- He rates value delivered as separate from product features: improving the customer experience can mean giving clients more perceived value, not only a better product. The same product made clients unhappy at $3K retainers and happy on performance pricing.
- Priorities: more wins, more bad guys found, more successful takedowns, and a better customer experience.

## Surviving failures at Tabs and in a new category

- He compares the journey to the S&P chart: daily or weekly views look like a yo-yo, but the long-term view is a line of best fit up and to the right. "It's never as deep as the way that it feels."
- **Tabs stockout in COVID:** inventory sold out fast, but containers were stuck off the coast and the packaging was made in China, so restocking took about six months. Spending on marketing or ads made no sense, and pre-orders were running while momentum died.
- Restarting took three or four months to get back to a similar level. He expected to turn marketing back on and "be rippy again," and it didn't work that way. He had left college as a sophomore to run Tabs.
- Other Tabs problems:
  - Chocolates melt in summer.
  - Disputes with his business partner.
  - The sale nearly fell through multiple times, with about $50,000 in legal fees and four months running a process.
  - Affiliate fraud: affiliates bought with stolen credit cards, got paid commission, then chargebacks hit, costing $5-10K a day at one point.
  - His best creators and his right-hand person quit.
- At Bustm, selling a business and starting over means rebuilding the foundation of team and momentum, which "takes years to build."
- Operating mindset: he has tried to be less anxious. He says CEOs should create urgency, but constant fight-or-flight is unhealthy for him and the team. He wants seasons of pushing and seasons of coasting.
- He says he's having far more fun running Bustm with compounding growth and happy clients than he did with Tabs' blitz scaling, where "every month we reset from zero."
