## Why most ebooks and info products sell poorly (and what top sellers do differently)

- Simon says the typical model is: pick a topic, spend hours researching, writing and proofreading, upload to Coursera, Udemy or Gumroad, share the link on social media, and make almost no money.
- He argues the problem is that creators pour effort into writing and leave sales "up to random chance."
- People like West Boss, Adam Marthan and Kent C. Dodds make millions on ebooks and courses. Simon says that isn't just because they have big followings. They have followings and sell well for the same reason: they are good at marketing, distribution and selling.
- Top sellers prepare every launch for a long time:
  - They spend years building community and owning their sales channels.
  - They build email lists, attend conferences and spread awareness.
  - They run paid ads and create affiliate programs.
  - Most importantly, they gave away knowledge for free for a long time before asking for money.
- He notes some influencers with 20,000+ followers make under $500 on an ebook launch, while others make thousands in pre-sales.
- His point: you don't need millions. A smaller, realistic result (like his) is still very good, and anyone can do it with work.

## Simon's results: $7,500 in week one, about $2,500 per month ongoing

- Product: "JavaScript in the Industry," a collection of best practices and tips for writing industry-level JavaScript, based on real-world experience. It is 71 pages with many illustrations.
- Simon is a software developer and startup CEO who previously worked in sales and as a freelance engineer.
- First week: he sold 480 copies. Launch price was $19, with a $9 offer for a group of selected people.
- Revenue and costs:
  - Gross revenue: just under $8,000 (the transcript's figure is garbled, so treat the exact gross as unclear).
  - Expenses: $531 total.
  - Profit: roughly $7,450 after expenses and a few fees. He frames it as "more than $7,500 in the first week of sales."
- The book still earns around $2,500 per month.
- It was his first ebook, and he wasn't a full-time ebook author.

## Budget and time breakdown

Expenses ($531 total):
- $47 for an ebook cover (Fiverr).
- $52 for proofreading for grammar and typos (Fiverr). English isn't his native language.
- $182 to have the ebook promoted on a popular Instagram account with almost a million followers.
- About $250 on paid ads on Facebook and Twitter.

Time (about 40 hours total):
- About 16 hours writing the ebook.
- About 10 hours building a website with a Stripe checkout.
- About 4 hours on launch emails and drip sequences.
- About 4 hours on extra marketing (arranging and creating the Instagram promoted post, a few ad hoc tasks).

Takeaway: he spent more time on marketing and distribution than on writing the book. He says you can launch with no spend, but a modest budget gets you further.

## Pick a topic you could write in your sleep

- Simon's first tip: don't use the ebook as a chance to learn something new. He calls it "probably the worst occasion you can pick."
- You're writing to sell. You're doing business, so don't wear yourself down mentally learning a new subject.
- Choose a topic so familiar you could "almost write an ebook about it in your sleep." The book should "write itself."
- His example: the JavaScript book took only 16 hours because he'd worked with the topic for about 10 years and didn't have to look anything up. It still got incredible feedback.
- Save your energy for marketing. Most people (especially in tech) haven't done much marketing, so that's where to challenge yourself.

## Pick your acquisition channels before you write

Before writing, ask: what are your acquisition channels, and how will you reach people? He says one post on your own profile won't cut it, even with thousands of followers. Options he covers:

- **Influencer marketing:**
  - Set up agreements with influencers in your niche, ideally before you write.
  - Reach out to ones you know, or cold-pitch ones you don't for a sponsor deal.
  - Pay a fixed price per promoted post, or offer a cut of each sale they produce.
  - Line up 10 or more professional influencers in the niche in advance and keep them in the loop.
- **Pre-launch:**
  - Charge for the product at a discount before it's done.
  - It tests demand: if almost nobody buys at pre-launch, it may not be worth building.
  - It lets you build hype. Spend one to two months sharing process details, screenshots and small bits before launch.
  - Even with an existing audience, one "I launched an ebook" post isn't enough.
- **Retargeting existing leads (his personal favorite):**
  - See the free lead-magnet section below.
- **Paid ads:**
  - Easy to set up and reliable. Facebook (Facebook, Instagram, Messenger, WhatsApp) and Google (YouTube and many web pages) cover almost the whole internet and are good at targeting.
  - He cites surveys showing people are less negative toward ads and increasingly buy from them.
  - It's relatively cheap. You don't need a million-dollar budget, just a small initial amount for the platform to tune in on an audience.
- Settle on one or two channels you believe will convert. More channels don't necessarily raise conversion, and they make the data obscure and the whole thing unmanageable.

## Give a free ebook first to build trust and an email list

- Three months before launching the paid book, Simon made a quick free ebook. He compiled the code snippets he'd shared on Twitter and LinkedIn over a year. It took less than two hours.
- More than 10,000 people picked it up over the next three months.
- Why it works:
  - **Authority:** many people see your writing quality without paying.
  - **Emails:** you collect leads. He imagines "a list of 10,000 qualified leads laying around the day you want to launch."
- He calls email marketing "super underrated in 2021" and says it has one of the highest click-through rates of any medium.
- Per the teaser at the end, a four-email drip sequence generated more than two-thirds of all sales (details were promised in part two, not covered here).

## His launch playbook in summary

1. Gave a free ebook away to build trust and authority and collect emails.
2. Created the paid ebook and a website with a Stripe integration.
3. Made agreements with a handful of big influencers in the tech Twitter space to retweet the launch announcement.
4. Paid one large Instagram influencer to promote the ebook.
5. Sent a discounted offer to his email list.
6. Set up ad campaigns on Twitter, YouTube and Facebook.

Result: about $7,500 in sales in the first 7 days, and about $2,500 per month afterward. The detailed email drip, conversion campaigns and launch post were deferred to a part two.
