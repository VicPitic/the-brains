## AI implementation partner (the "AI automation agency")

Simon says SaaS is no longer the opportunity it once was. Among the founders he knows who use AI to build, he is "one of the few who's still doing SaaS". The ones doing well with AI are doing something less glamorous: solving problems companies already spend money on.

- **The gap:** Almost every company is under pressure to "do something with AI". Employees have tried ChatGPT and management has seen Claude Code and agent tools, but nobody knows how to connect it to the business. Simon cites a study that only about 5% of businesses have gotten real financial value from AI.
- **The business:** Be the person who makes AI work inside a company. He thinks "AI automation agency" sounds more sophisticated than the work is.
- **Example, 50-person e-commerce company:** The support team answers the same questions all day (where is my order, check the store back end, read the history, write a reply). You map that process with the team, then build an internal agent that reads the conversation, checks the order in Shopify, looks up company policies and drafts the reply.
- **Human in the loop:** Simple questions are handled automatically. Refunds and unusual cases go to an employee for approval.
- **Scope:** The job covers everything around the agent: tools and permissions, testing against real support cases, training the team, and monitoring after launch.
- **Pricing model:** Charge for the implementation, then keep a monthly agreement for testing and maintenance.
- **How to start:** Pick an industry, find one expensive workflow people repeat every day, and build the agent for it.

## Build an audience around AI (brand deals and angel investing)

AI startups are raising enormous amounts of money, and once the product is built they all need people to know it exists. With thousands of AI tools competing for attention, much of that investment money ends up spent on distribution.

- **The business:** Make videos, posts or a newsletter about what is happening in AI. Test new tools, show what they can actually do, and build an audience that wants to keep up with the space. Tool companies pay creators for brand deals to reach those people.
- **His own case:** This is already a meaningful part of his business. He partners with some of the bigger AI tools and occasionally does brand deals with them. Simon admits it is "probably the least surprising idea" on the list, but it belongs because it is one of the most direct ways to make money from the AI boom.
- **The less-discussed angle:** Many creators he knows are becoming angel investors. He has five promising AI startups in his angel portfolio, one of them Lovable. These are early-stage private companies most people can't buy into.
- **How he got access:** Some companies came to him for a brand deal and asked his rate for a 90-second slot in a video. He flipped it: "why don't I give you my money instead?" He invests and has a reason to keep supporting the product as it grows.
- **Why it works for both sides:** The company gets an angel investor who can help with distribution. He gets in before the company reaches the wider market.
- **Takeaway:** The obvious business is brand deals. The more interesting opportunity can come from the relationships with the founders behind those deals.

## AI agent security auditing

Companies are giving AI agents access to customer data, emails, payments and internal tools. Once an agent can take action, one bad instruction or permission mistake can cause real damage. Simon says most companies are moving much faster on giving agents access than on testing them safely.

- **Example, a finance agent** that reads invoices, looks up vendors and prepares payments. Before production, the company hires you to attack it.
- **Step 1:** Map every tool and every piece of data the agent can access.
- **Step 2:** Check whether it can see more than it needs, or act without the right approval.
- **Step 3:** Try to manipulate it:
  - Hide instructions inside an invoice telling the agent to ignore its rules.
  - Change a vendor's bank details.
  - Ask it to reveal employee payroll information.
  - Ask it to prepare a payment above its approval limit.
- **Turn failures into tests:** Every failure becomes a test the company can re-run before the agent is updated.
- **Ongoing work:** Model changes, prompt edits and newly connected tools can bring old problems back, so testing continues after launch.
- **Revenue structure:** The company pays you to audit before launch, build the evaluation suite, and keep testing as the system changes.
- **Barrier to entry:** It needs real security expertise and a lot of trust, which gives it a much higher barrier than a normal AI agency. It is a natural fit if you already work in cybersecurity, penetration testing or software QA.

## AI-powered service company

Companies already pay for research, recruitment, bookkeeping, design and legal support. They know what they are buying and a budget exists.

- **Example, an interior design studio:** A client renovating an office gives the use, style and budget. Normally you spend days finding furniture, checking measurements, comparing materials and building a mood board and floor plan, then rebuild when the client wants a different table, warmer floor or new layout.
- **With AI:** Feed it the client brief, room dimensions and supplier catalogs. It finds options that fit the room and budget, builds a first mood board and prepares the shopping list. You still decide what looks good.
- **3D rendering:** AI can now work inside 3D rendering software, so you can ask it to swap the table, change the floor or move the lighting instead of rebuilding by hand.
- **Client view:** The client doesn't need to know about AI. They hired an interior design studio and that is what they get. Behind the scenes, work that used to take several people is done by a much smaller team or solo.
- **Key insight:** "Some of the smartest people I know and some of the people making the most money with AI right now aren't selling AI. They're using it to amplify work their clients still expect humans to do."
- **Recipe:** Pick a valuable service you already understand, use AI to amplify your work, and charge for the result.

## Practical AI workshops for companies

Many companies have already bought ChatGPT or a copilot for employees, but usage is all over the place: one person uses it daily, another pastes sensitive customer data into a random tool, and half the team barely uses AI because nobody showed them how.

- **The business:** Sell a day that turns employees into AI superusers.
- **Prompting:** Teach the whole team to write prompts with proper context, examples and constraints, then turn prompts that work into a reusable company library.
- **Technical staff:** Help set up Claude Code properly and give it rules and context for their codebases.
- **Workflow builders:** Teach people who build repeated workflows to take real examples from the business, define what a good result looks like, and test whether the AI gets it right before the rest of the company uses it.
- **AI hygiene:** Cover which tools are approved, what data must stay private, and where a person still needs to check results.
- **Packaging:** The exact workshop changes per company, but you can package a few modules, tailor the examples to their work, and sell it as a half-day or full-day session.
- **Recurring revenue:** Come back for recurring training as tools change and employees become ready for more advanced workflows.

## Don't feel late: start boring and useful

Simon acknowledges that Twitter and YouTube are full of people building autonomous companies and raising millions, which can make the AI opportunity feel like it has already passed.

- **His view:** Some of the best current opportunities are "much more boring and straightforward": helping companies answer support tickets, test agents, train employees, and do the same expensive service work they paid for before AI.
- **Customer mindset:** "Your customer doesn't care whether the business looks impressive on Twitter. They care that you solve a problem they already have."
- **What to do:** Pick a market you understand, talk to the people in it, and find one thing they already spend money on. Then use AI to deliver it faster, cheaper or with a much smaller team.
- **Sequencing:** "You can always build something more sophisticated later. First, build something useful."
