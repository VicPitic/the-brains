## The three-stage user acquisition framework

Simon frames every user acquisition as three parts: a method, a channel and a cost. Combining them yields many possible ways to get a user, from free and manual to paid and automatic. He argues you need a road map matched to your SaaS stage, because some methods work only early and others suit growth or automation mode.

He sorts all online marketing into four methods:
- **Incoming:** the user finds you and signs up or requests contact.
- **Outgoing:** you reach out first and convert them.
- **Referrals:** current users bring in new users, who bring in more.
- **Advertising:** like incoming, but you pay to get in front of them.

His road map, which he says he used to build and scale three products and would use again:
- **Stage 1:** first 5 users, then up to 100.
- **Stage 2:** 500 to 1,000 users.
- **Stage 3:** 1,000+ users, continuous growth.

## Stage 1a: Get your first 5 users with manual outreach

- **Method:** outgoing. **Channel:** email or DMs on whatever message app you use. **Cost:** $0, done manually one by one.
- Reach out to your close network: family, friends, colleagues, partners, anyone you think would benefit.
- Say you're launching, that you have an MVP ready, and that you want the first few users.
- Offer a completely free account for life. The only ask is their feedback.
- Not everyone will fit, but if the product solves a real problem, five is achievable.
- Put these users into a small community (Discord, Facebook group, WhatsApp, whatever works).
- Spend time with them: listen to feedback, fix bugs, ship the features they ask for, smooth rough edges.
- Let them have a big say in direction. He calls these "the most important" users because the goal is to turn them into advocates, not just users.

## Stage 1b: Go from 5 to 100 users through referrals

- **Method:** referrals. **Channel:** referral links your early users share. **Cost:** usually zero.
- He could offer them a revenue cut per referral, but in most cases thinks it's unnecessary. If you served the first five really well and made them feel part of the project, they'll happily refer people unpaid.
- Instead, give new users a big early-joiner discount. It makes advocates and newcomers feel part of something up and coming, and it gives advocates a strong offer to pitch.

## Stage 2: Reach 500 to 1,000 users with content and building in public

- Manual emails/DMs and referral incentives won't scale to this level. He designs a new model.
- **Method:** incoming. Users find you and sign up themselves, so you need awareness at scale.
- **Channels:** social media and search engines. He admits he sounds like a broken record: "the best way to create an acquisition model using the incoming method is by creating content and building in public."
- Tactics:
  - Long-form video for YouTube search.
  - Blog posts to show up on Google.
  - TikToks, Reels and YouTube Shorts to catch attention.
  - LinkedIn updates on highs, lows, discoveries and milestones.
  - Lessons shared on Hacker News and Indie Hackers.
- Most important: keep doing it. This stage is "long and hard" and results are slow.
- **Cost:** you should expect to invest in good content. What you can pay per acquisition depends on your ARPU (average revenue per user).
- There will be long stretches with little engagement and no conversions, so monitor and optimize closely.
- He guarantees the model works but takes time. Let revenue from existing users fund acquiring new ones until you hit at least 500 users.

## Stage 3a: Scale with paid advertising

- At 1,000+ users, keep the content engine running (stage 2 doesn't end). Add two models.
- **Method:** advertising. Take the content that proved itself in stage 2 and turn it into paid ads. Also craft, test and scale new creatives and strategies.
- Recommended channels:
  - Google Ads for search ads and YouTube video ads.
  - Meta for image and video ads on Facebook and Instagram.
  - TikTok for video ads.
- Cost depends on ARPU and the return on ad spend (ROAS) you want:
  - **Growth mode:** you may not care about return at all and just reinvest all revenue into new acquisitions.
  - **Profit mode:** aim for roughly 3:1 ROAS. Each $1 spent on a new user returns $3; reinvest $1 into the next user and keep $1 as profit.

## Stage 3b: Scale with an affiliate program

- **Method:** referrals again, but now as an open affiliate program where anyone can sign up, create referral links and promote.
- At this scale you need proper compensation. He has seen programs pay 10%, 20% or even 50% of recurring revenue from referred customers.
- Payout depends on your direction:
  - **Growth mode:** share a big chunk of revenue to keep affiliates highly motivated.
  - **Automation mode:** go lower, down to gift cards, unlocking features or raising usage limits for affiliates.
- In stage 3 you choose between maximizing growth (reinvesting every dime) or taking profit and automating acquisition so new users arrive on autopilot.

## Why this matters: what high-growth SaaS have in common

- He points to bootstrapped SaaS products making nearly $1M a year, and one making nearly $1M a month at only two years old.
- His claim: they are great products, but what makes them exceptional is that they figured out how to blow up their user base and sustain growth month after month.
- "This is not just luck, this is science." He says you can break down what they do and reproduce it.
- The framework is not the only valid road map. You can run ads from the very early stages or scale cold outreach later. It is simply the one he has used.
