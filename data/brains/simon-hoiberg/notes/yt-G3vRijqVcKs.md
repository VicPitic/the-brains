## Simon on lifetime deals (LTDs) as a pricing and distribution tactic

- Simon defines a lifetime deal as "pay once instead of paying monthly or yearly and then own it forever."
- He sold LTDs on AppSumo for his SaaS FeedHive and says he has "never had any concern" or felt cheated by the platform.
- His main advice is to plan the finances first. He warns founders not to make a "killer deal" just to be sellable, and to be fair to their own future finances.
- Lifetime users are not a permanent burden. He says LTD users churn for several reasons:
  - They outgrow the tool.
  - They move to something new.
  - They love it and upgrade to a higher monthly or yearly tier. Some FeedHive users did this.
- On FeedHive, he says the churn rate for the average LTD user is higher than for the average subscriber.

## How AppSumo works and why Simon thinks the cut is fair

- Simon's description of the split: AppSumo takes about 50% of all revenue and puts it into advertising (ads, newsletters, events). Of the remaining 50%, the founder gets 30% and AppSumo keeps 20% as profit.
- He rejects the objection that AppSumo is greedy and only leaves founders 30%. His argument:
  - A distribution channel this size is worth far more than the product itself.
  - Product-centric founders think they are doing AppSumo a favor by listing; he says it's "the other way around."
  - If you can reach that audience yourself, "go sell it yourself."
- He cites AppSumo's reach: a newsletter of about 800,000 people, large-scale ads on YouTube, Facebook, Instagram and LinkedIn, and more than 10 years of experience selling these deals.
- He describes the operations as professional: deadlines kept, payments on time, help with legal forms and integration, and someone always available to answer questions.

## FeedHive's AppSumo results

- Simon says his startup made about $400k in its first year, and about $300k of that came in the first two months from lifetime deals.
- FeedHive ranked in AppSumo's top five best-selling products during the roughly two months it ran there. He notes AppSumo products have done over $1M in total, so it was top five for that period, not of all time.
- Of the roughly $300k in sales he took home about $100k (his roughly 30% share).
- He says he was very satisfied with that result and that it beat what he expected.

## Selling an LTD yourself: the LinkDrip pre-sale

- A year after FeedHive, Simon launched LinkDrip, a link shortener that integrates with FeedHive, and sold LTDs himself as a pre-sale instead of using AppSumo.
- The pre-sale doubled as product validation. He spent about eight hours on a landing page, set up a Stripe account, posted on social media and made a promo video.
- Results: $75,000 in the first month and more than $100,000 over two months.
- Many buyers were existing FeedHive LTD owners who wanted the integration.
- This only worked because he had an audience. At the time he reached hundreds of thousands of people on Twitter, 50,000+ on YouTube, and 200,000+ people in total.
- He warns that self-listing without an audience gets "close to nothing." Founders need to build their own distribution channel first.
- He says LinkDrip later reached about $100,000 ARR (last time he checked).
- He would not do it again. Reasons:
  - Holding $100k of pre-sale cash with no finished product felt stressful.
  - He had to ship against a promised date, which went against how his team normally works (no scrum, no sprints, no deadlines, because he finds deadlines stressful for founders and developers and says quality is "infinitely much better" at a comfortable pace).
  - He says he probably spent significantly more than he otherwise would have because of the rush.

## The risks and friction of LTDs

- Operating cost is the biggest con. He says a tool with heavy ongoing costs, for example one that leans on OpenAI APIs or other computationally expensive services, should reconsider whether an LTD works financially.
- Lifetime value math he suggests:
  - Divide the average monthly subscription by the expected time before a user churns to get average lifetime value.
  - FeedHive's monthly churn is around 8%.
  - In theory you could charge that lifetime value up front and the economics would match a subscription, assuming equal churn. He notes LTD churn is actually higher.
- Relationship friction runs in both directions:
  - Some LTD users show entitlement, expecting premium support for a one-time payment. He compares it to the freelancing meme where the $200 client demands endless revisions and the $10,000 client is easy.
  - Founders also have less incentive to give the same support to someone who cannot churn: "no matter how we want to say this or not the moment the lifetime user has paid you there is less incentive for you to give them the same sublime support experience."
  - Many LTD users have had bad experiences with founders who treated them poorly, so he says the blame is not one-sided.
- He advises founders to minimize friction in this financial and support dependency.

## TinyKiwi: an LTD-only business that works

- Simon bought TinyKiwi, a lightweight browser-based Canva alternative, from a founder he found on Twitter, a bit over a year before this recording. He originally wanted to integrate it into FeedHive and compete with Canva.
- The vision fizzled and the developer lost motivation, so he moved the developer to another product and decided to sell it as a lifetime deal.
- He runs Facebook ads for a $67 lifetime deal. They are still running and generate $5,000 to $10,000 in sales per month.
- It suits an LTD because costs are near zero:
  - The tool runs almost entirely in the browser, with no heavy API or backend.
  - Backend usage makes only a few database calls plus authentication.
  - He calculated only a slight server cost increase for every additional 10,000 users.
- The main running cost is a designer he hired to keep building new templates.
- The lesson he draws: an LTD fits best when new users add almost no marginal cost.

## Other benefits Simon sees in LTD launches

- Word of mouth and getting users on board matter more to him than the up-front cash.
- A launch brings hundreds of users into the product at once, which surfaces bugs and feature suggestions fast.
- He is considering an LTD (own audience and/or AppSumo) for his other product, 8base, and says he is "considering both options."
- Practical downside he flags: after an AppSumo deal ends, the AppSumo page can outrank the product's own site in Google for the brand name for about 60 days. New users see what they missed, which makes the subscription look less attractive. He notes that a founder can ask AppSumo for favors on this.
