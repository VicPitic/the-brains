## Portfolio framing: 80% on what's working, 20% on exploration

Cody defines FOMO marketing as "when we make marketing decisions based on what we could be doing rather than what is working."

- He thinks of marketing as a portfolio: about 80% of resources on what is already working and about 20% on exploring new initiatives.
- The split is short-term bets versus long-term bets.
- He is not against testing new things. He says to allocate only a small percentage of resources to them.

## Shiny object syndrome distracts from the job of marketing

Cody says many marketing teams get shiny object syndrome, which pulls them away from why they exist: "to increase revenue."

- The failure mode is expanding into new channels (his example list: TikTok, Pinterest, SEO) before the current channels are exhausted.
- His rule: "If you have a winning horse, bet on that winning horse until it stops winning."
- Summary principle: do more of what is working and less of what isn't.

## Case study: the 13x ROAS ecom brand that wanted to add channels

An investment banker friend asked Cody about channel expansion for an ecom brand he was working with.

- The brand already had a lot of success on its current channels and wanted to expand into TikTok, Pinterest and SEO.
- The friend mentioned the brand was getting a 13x ROAS on Google Search Ads. Cody called that ROAS "insane."
- Cody asked whether they had exploited that arbitrage as far as it could go. The answer was no.
- His advice: put as much money into that channel as it takes until you see diminishing returns. "Go to that ATM until it quits printing."

## How to decide where the next dollar goes

A practical sequence implied by Cody's argument:

1. Find the channel with the best return (here, 13x ROAS on Google Search).
2. Keep scaling spend into it until marginal returns diminish.
3. Only then look at new channels as the main bet.
4. In the meantime, run new-channel experiments with a small slice of resources (roughly the 20%).

## Winning horses for B2B brands (as of late 2022)

Cody lists what he was seeing work for B2B brands at the time:

- Cold email
- Customer match lists into ad channels
- Remarketing product demos
- Podcasts
- Repurposing podcast content
- Educational content posted to LinkedIn pages
