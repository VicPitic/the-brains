## Email as a cost-per-click channel
- Cody argues email marketing is measured wrongly: "We should be measuring email clicks on a CPC basis," the way search and display are measured.
- Historical framing: advertising started with "eyeballs" (impressions), then added vanity metrics like recall, retention and likeliness to buy. Cost per click (pioneered by Bill Gross at Idealab/goto.com, popularized by Google) shifted the metric from impressions to user actions.
- Simple version: monthly price of the email sender divided by the clicks email creates.
- Better version: compare the conversions that come from email against conversions from paid ads.
- Cost is not only money. Time and mental energy count too, so they belong in the CPC calculation.

## The true email CPC formula
- Formula: Email CPC = (initial labor setup costs + CRM cost x months) / total clicks in the time frame.
- Cody's worked example from client data: ($2,500 setup + $99/month x 24 months) / 2,500 clicks = $1.95 per click.
- Compared with paid ads at about $0.10 per click, email carries a large premium.
- He argues the premium is worth paying because people revisit emails months later, so one send keeps producing clicks long after it goes out.
- The same lens applies to the true cost of email nurture campaigns, which include the build labor, not just the software bill.

## Email as a personal search engine
- While digging through client email data, Cody saw people open an email in the month it was sent, then return months later and click again.
- He connected this to his own habit: he emails himself links all the time, using his inbox as a database and search engine.
- His conclusion: people use their inbox as a "hacked database" they can search later, so sending an email adds your brand, content and ideas to that database.
- He describes email accounts as "personal search engines" for their owners.

## Permission to seed a user's database
- When someone shares their email (a PDF download, a newsletter signup, a product purchase), they give permission to add to their personal database.
- That lets a company seed the inbox with information relevant to the person and related to the brand, which they may return to later.
- Cody frames this as the reason email is "the most valuable marketing asset."
- Email is not a transactional campaign. It is a long-term marketing strategy, similar to SEO.

## Email compounds like SEO
- The more relevant content a company has sitting in people's inboxes, the bigger the network of information that links back to its site.
- Each email is a durable, searchable entry point rather than a one-time blast, so value accumulates over time.
- Practical implication: judge email on lifetime clicks and conversions per dollar and hour invested, not on first-week open and click rates.
- He also notes email still works despite being disrupted by free services like Gmail: it drives traffic, nurtures leads and re-engages audiences. He cites the quote "You'll change more street addresses than you will email addresses in the 21st century."
