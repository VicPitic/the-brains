## Lead validation with GPT-3: prioritizing a large lead database

- Cody says every B2B software company should own a database of all its target customers, sourced from anywhere possible. His examples: directories and scraping Google Maps.
- Investor angle: an early-stage startup that has a list of all its target customers when raising its Series A will make investors "stoked."
- The problem: with, say, 50K collected leads, it is unclear which ones the sales team should work first.
- Traditionally, SDRs comb the database and validate leads manually.
- Keyword filters help only a little. Companies that fit a product often use language that signals it, but keywords are "super limited because people talk about the same thing differently pretty often."
- Cody says GPT-3 removes that limitation, because it can judge fit from the meaning of a company's own site copy instead of matching exact terms.

## The four-step GPT-3 lead validation process

Cody heard about this from a friend who uses GPT-3 for lead validation and thought it solved a massive problem. The process:

1. Use Python to request a potential lead's URL.
2. Ingest all the text on that person's site.
3. Run a GPT-3 prompt asking a qualifying question. His example: "Does this company have multiple mental health professionals or are they a solo practitioner?"
4. Loop this through all the leads.

His summary: "And you just validated your list."

## Writing the qualifying prompt

- The prompt is a plain yes/no or either/or question about a trait that decides whether the lead fits your product.
- His example question separates multi-provider mental health practices from solo practitioners. It is a firmographic distinction that is hard to catch with keywords but easy to read from a site's team pages and service descriptions.
- The same pattern works for any ICP criterion that shows up in a company's website text. Cody only gives the mental health example.

## AI makes strong people far more productive, not redundant

- Cody does not think AI tools like this will replace people. He thinks "it will make 10x people 100x people."
- His stated goal: make teams "super human, so that one really good person can work like they're 100 people."
- Applied to sales: the manual lead-vetting work SDRs used to do is automated, so reps spend their time on leads already validated as good fits.
