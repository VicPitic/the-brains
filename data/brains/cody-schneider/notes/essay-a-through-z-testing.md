## Nature selects by elimination, and Cody argues digital ads should too

- Cody argues that "nature doesn't A/B test." Evolution tries many combinations at once and the last organism standing wins. It is a process of elimination, not a search for the right answer.
- He says digital advertising should work the same way: search for what doesn't work rather than what does.
- He acknowledges this is counterintuitive, since "every digital ad blog, agency and guru" says to A/B test methodically. He calls that approach "utterly archaic."
- His alternative is "A-through-Z testing," which takes A/B testing a step further by testing everything at once.

## Big number theory: use ad volume to find outliers

- Cody's premise is that with enough data, trends emerge. They let you identify independent and dependent variables, see patterns and, most importantly, find outliers.
- He notes that outliers live in the tails, and the tails are typically where the most interesting things happen.
- Print media (magazines, newspapers) limits you to physical ad slots. Digital offers a relatively infinite inventory of ad slots, so you can make tens, hundreds or even thousands of ads.
- Instead of running a single ad to an audience, he says to run every ad creative idea you can think of.
- Trusting big number theory lets you separate the average ads from the ads in the tails, then put your time, money and mental energy behind the ads that create the most organizational impact.

## What A-through-Z testing is and how to run it

- A-through-Z testing combines three ideas: each ad unit is its own entity, big number theory, and letting the algorithm decide the winner.
- The algorithm shows the winner through CPC and conversion data, which reveal the most effective ad units.
- Example setup for Facebook ads: combine 10 photos, 10 headlines and 10 body texts. That creates 1K combinations (1,000).
- Run all of them and let the platform, and the people on it, decide which combination gives the greatest return on ad spend.
- Cody frames this as "teasing a message out of the audience" with the message they want to engage with most, rather than forcing a message on them.

## Why not dissect ad components

- Cody describes working at a previous agency where executives at big public companies would ask how testing worked and try to dissect ad components: the photo, the copy, the call to action. They then looked for trends within each.
- He says this is wrong, because combining creative elements produces a whole new piece of media.
- His analogy: "Hydrogen and oxygen apart are naturally combustible, together they're naturally cool."
- So the unit of analysis is the full ad (the combination), not its individual parts.

## Feeding learnings back into the next round

- Once certain ad units succeed, you use that knowledge when refreshing the ads: which media and copy go well together.
- This shapes which components you use in the next round, but it does not split the ad units back into separate parts.

## The path to AI-based advertising

- Cody concedes that making 1K ad units by hand is probably not a good time investment for most organizations.
- His answer: take the human out of making the ads. He expects companies to have media libraries from which AI-based software generates ad units.
- Firms will then be able to test thousands of ad units and advise creative teams on what media to make next for the library.
- He calls A-through-Z testing "the bridge to the future of AI-based advertising" and says this is what his company, Audience Eye, was pursuing at the time (2020).
