## Why creators beat influencers: the cost-per-view arbitrage

Cody argues that social has shifted from a "who you follow" social graph to "what you're interested in" For You page algorithms (TikTok changed this; Instagram Reels, Twitter, LinkedIn and YouTube now work the same way). Platforms do this because it keeps people on-platform longer, and attention is what they sell. For brands, the value is in "somebody that understands your niche and how to get views and traction within the category", not in follower counts.

His worked example (illustrative numbers):
- **Influencer:** about 100,000 followers (the table he reads out says 10,000), charges $2,000 for one post. Only about 1 in 10 posts goes viral, so one post might get around 30,000 views.
- **Creator:** starts with zero followers, paid $900 a month for one post per day (about 30 posts) across TikTok, Instagram and YouTube Shorts. About 10% of posts outperform, so more chances to hit an outlier.
- Cody's claim: people don't make purchase decisions largely off influencers. They buy from creators who consistently talk about a topic. Influencers only work at massive scale (a Kim Kardashian), and those people make their own products because they know their audience is valuable.
- Because For You page content is shown to whoever the algorithm thinks will stay, a creator with zero followers can reach people they never could before.

## The CPM math versus paid ads

- Average Facebook CPM is "about $6 depending on which category you're in". $900 of Facebook spend buys about 150,000 impressions.
- If a creator gets about 500,000 aggregate impressions for $900, the CPM is roughly $2 (he states the figure loosely), which is cheaper than Facebook.
- His TikTok illustration: an $8 TikTok ad CPM versus about $2 via creator marketing. "That's a better allocation of the limited resources that I have."
- Core framing: this is marketing arbitrage, the same game as in his other videos. Find a channel where attention costs less than the market rate.
- Organic content converts better than paid because people prefer buying from content that feels organic. His analogy: Meta ads are a billboard in a busy town square, while this is handing out value-add flyers (like the top 10 restaurants in the area, one of which is yours).

## The compounding flywheel across 5-10 creators

- Don't hire one creator. Work with 5 to 10, each posting one video a day.
- With 10 videos dropping daily, about one will be an outlier, "based on the math". Share that learning with every other creator so they remix the winning format.
- More creators means more surface area for learning which content types produce reach for the product.
- Promote winners to paid: "If they work organically, they're also going to work in a paid setting." This turns the program into a UGC content engine for ads.
- Keep top performers on retainer, drop the worst, then restart the cycle with new creators.
- Example: his friend's company Focus Tree (a phone-free study/productivity app) uses exactly this strategy across hundreds of TikTok accounts. Cody says it grew to around two million users, but he's unsure of the current figure.
- Format tip: make ads look native, as if recorded on an iPhone and edited in CapCut or in the Instagram app. Brands find this uncomfortable because they want polished, "red camera" production, but a 20-year-old creator paying for school is the model. Creators typically hold deals with several brands (for example five brands at about $1,000 each) and post multiple videos a day.

## The playbook: finding, hiring and managing creators

1. **Find creators already in your category.** Scroll TikTok and watch niche videos until the algorithm silos you into the space, then note the recurring faces. Larger categories (like real estate investing) have more creators than smaller ones (like vibe coding).
2. **Reach out** by DM or the email in their bio: "I want to hire you on a cost per video basis, a video per day for $900."
3. **Creator starts a fresh account** from zero followers, posting in your category.
4. **Keep winners, cut losers** and repeat the cycle.
- Lovable as an example: Cody points to a "coding_dev.app" style account as a likely test of this approach, though it may be run by an in-house team or a clipper. He is skeptical of the "clipper" model: "I think all the data is fake."
- Management is the main pain point. Common fix: make your best performer a manager of the other creators, paid more to ensure the team delivers daily and to share outlier videos with the group.
- Many teams manage creators in a Discord server or WhatsApp group.
- Hiring someone who understands this is hard. The people involved are often flaky 22-year-olds, so expect friction.

## The tool stack

- **Stormy AI:** finds creators by category on TikTok, Instagram and YouTube, DMs them, and negotiates pricing, which removes the biggest time sink.
- **Shortimize and viral.app:** creators submit videos and the tools track views and engagement to measure campaign success.
- **Virlo (pronunciation unsure) and Sandcastles AI:** scrape and aggregate TikToks, Shorts and Reels so you can search for outlier videos in your category, then hand them to creators to remix for your brand.
- Management layer: Discord or WhatsApp group.

## Which products this works for (and which it doesn't)

- **Best fit:** B2C and mobile apps, and products with one killer feature (his example: Incogni, which blocks data brokers from selling your data).
- **Requirement:** an impulse purchase that can happen on a phone, with no learning curve. The viewer can search, install and understand the value instantly.
- **E-commerce:** works at price points around $29. For TikTok Shop, find shop creators, pay per video, and add an affiliate commission on what they drive.
- **Poor fit:** products with a learning curve that need long-form education to use.

## Layering it into B2B and web apps: brand search and "digital gravity"

- Cody's friend's API company uses this approach and it works. It builds top-of-funnel branded search and a discovery flywheel.
- His concept of "digital gravity": create as much surface area for your brand within your category as possible and layer strategies on top of each other.
- TikTok and Instagram reach so many daily active users in the US that some share of your target customers will see the content, which builds mental real estate in your category and lifts branded search.
- Measure it in Google Search Console by filtering queries that contain your brand name. E-commerce brands see branded search on Amazon spike after a TikTok goes viral.
- The ROI is hard to quantify, but it works if the goal is "have an entire industry know that I exist".
- Suggested sequencing: start with the YouTube creator strategy (top of funnel awareness and bottom of funnel conversion), then layer the short-form creator strategy on top.
