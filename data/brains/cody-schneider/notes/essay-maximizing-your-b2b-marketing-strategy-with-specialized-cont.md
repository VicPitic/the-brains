## A hired channel specialist beats rotating UGC creators

- Cody sees brands moving toward one specialist creator per channel instead of rotating UGC models.
- Specialists know the channel, own it entirely, and build consistency and trust because the same person delivers the information every time.
- Rotating UGC models feel unnatural on the platform. His analogy: "imagine if your favorite YouTuber, Tiktoker, or podcast host just switched every upload or episode. Like it would feel strange."
- Specialists are measured on channel growth, unlike social media managers who often keep "posting to no one".
- His advice: mimic what happens naturally on each channel and run a version that serves business goals.
- His 2023 prediction: more companies hiring for titles like "YouTuber", "TikToker" and "Podcaster".

## Case study: $5K/month TikTok hire for a B2B SaaS

- A friend who owns a B2B SaaS company hired one person at $5K per month to run the brand's TikTok.
- Results so far: 375K views for $15K spent, and a ROAS of 3x.
- Cody drew his insights below from conversations about this experiment.

## Make content native, personality-led and consistent

- **Consistency builds trust:** seeing the same face repeatedly makes people more likely to click when it appears in the feed. The more value they keep providing, the smaller the ask feels when it's time to buy.
- **Native content:** content must be built for the specific channel. Each channel has its own nuances, and a one-size-fits-all approach has less impact than a tailored version.
- **Needs a personality:** the friend tested generic product-focused content with no face against content featuring the TikToker. Videos with the person included went viral 10x more.

## Tell stories where the product is part of the journey

- The best performing videos told a story in which the product was included in the journey, rather than the journey being about the product.
- Cody calls this "a really nuanced but important detail and a classic content mistake".
- Focus on the goals people are trying to accomplish, and show the product as an essential tool for getting there.

## Measure ROI with a dedicated link and a self-reported field

- Create a dedicated referral or signup link for each channel. It's an easy way to measure the ROI of hiring these people.
- Combine it with a required "how did you hear about us?" form field to gather enough data to prove the value.

## Post more and double down on winners (80/20)

- In this experiment, 63% of impressions from the organic strategy came from 20% of posts.
- Cody calls it "a numbers game": post more, see what works, make more of what works and less of what doesn't.

## Treat it as an owned media strategy

- Followers gained on the channel give the brand a direct line of communication.
- Cody frames this as building a marketing moat and diversifying the company's marketing bets.
