## Marketing moats and owned media as a portfolio

- Cody argues the most important defensible moats are marketing moats, not the ones usually discussed in business school. He says people keep talking about "the least important moats": product, economies of scale, high NPS.
- Owned media is how he builds a marketing moat. Channels you don't own are variable, higher risk and easier to duplicate.
- His closing frame: think of marketing as "a portfolio of bets with risk premiums." The portfolio holds riskier bets and short and long-term bets, and should adjust to where the company is in its life cycle.
- His one-line summary: "owned media is the bonds of your portfolio."

## Owning every result for the few keywords that drive acquisition

Cody's example is a software product in his portfolio for the Etsy Sellers niche.

- Only 10 keywords create all of its new customer acquisition.
- He works to own the front page for every one of those keywords with several assets at once:
  - Multiple blogs on separate domains ranking for them.
  - YouTube videos from multiple channels ranking for them.
  - Free Chrome extensions that do a lesser version of what the software does.
- The goal is that a searcher sees his assets however they browse the results page.

## Why SERP ownership is still not fully owned

- Cody notes that even a fully owned front page carries risk, because Google and YouTube could change their ranking algorithms.
- His hedge is email collection from the same audience, so he can communicate directly whenever he wants.
- The pattern: rank across many surfaces, then pull the audience into a direct channel (email) that no platform controls.

## The B2B scenario: how rented channels fail

Cody walks through a B2B software company that gets customers through paid ads.

- Say it found two channels that work and raised a Series A on that marketing arbitrage.
- Three months after the raise, Y Combinator accepts two companies doing exactly the same thing.
- CPC doubles and CAC triples.
- Channels you don't own are variable, higher risk and easy to copy, "especially now with ad spy tools."

## Examples of owned media

Cody's list (he notes they all still carry some risk):

- Email newsletters.
- The company website, when people go to it directly.
- SEO content for organic traffic.
- Podcasts.

His observation: they are all old, tried-and-tested technologies. If companies were doing it in 2000 and are still doing it now, there is probably an owned media strategy built around it.

## How to apply this

- Find the small set of queries that drive acquisition, and put multiple assets (blogs, videos, free tools) on each one.
- Add email capture to every owned surface, to protect against algorithm changes.
- Treat paid ads as the risky part of the portfolio and owned media as the stable base, then rebalance as the company matures.
