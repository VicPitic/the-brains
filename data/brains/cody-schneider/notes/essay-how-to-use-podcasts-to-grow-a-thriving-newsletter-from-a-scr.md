## Cold email list → podcast promotion as the first newsletter send

Cody frames the hard part of a scraped email list as "what do I send them?" His answer: promote a podcast.

- Business emails are effectively public now: they sit in directories, can be scraped, or can be found with lookup tools.
- If you have a defined target audience and a product that sells easily to them, email is one of the fastest and easiest ways to reach them, "ESPECIALLY if you're an early-stage startup."
- The problem comes after you have and have validated the emails. Deciding what to send is, in his experience, one of the hardest parts of this channel.

## The process and the results he reports

- For a client, he loaded a cold list of their target audience into an ESP as a sequence.
- The only content sent was promotion of the client's podcast.
- Results on that cold list: a 40% open rate and a 5% click-through rate. He says it "literally blows my mind" and that it is hard to hit those numbers even on a warm list.
- The podcast reached 5K+ downloads per month within 60 days and 8 episodes, which he says puts it in the top 1% of podcasts.

## What the email says

- The email is educational in tone and is essentially "Go learn this today on this podcast."
- It has no direct product pitch. The product ask lives inside the podcast, as a call to action at the start of each episode to sign up for the product.

## Why it works: the psychology

- For the target customer, a podcast is a small lift because it is passive: they can listen without doing anything.
- People don't see podcasts as sales tools yet. They see them as educational vehicles, so the call to action feels soft instead of aggressive.
- Recipients don't find it salesy, even with the signup CTA at the top of the episode.

## Customer interviews as disguised testimonials

- Cody tells clients to interview their own customers on the podcast.
- That makes each episode a customer testimonial packaged as education.
- The content "doesn't have to be perfect, it just has to exist so it can be distributed."

## Where it applies

- He says the process can be done for every industry.
- The takeaway: pair a validated cold list with a podcast so the list has something worth sending, and treat distribution of imperfect content as more important than polishing it.
