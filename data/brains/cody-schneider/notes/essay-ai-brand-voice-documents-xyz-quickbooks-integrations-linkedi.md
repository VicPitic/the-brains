## Brand voice documents for AI-generated content

Cody argues that teams need AI brand voice guides the same way they have brand style guides. A style guide covers fonts, colors and iconography so media looks consistent, and "consistency builds trust, and that's the cornerstone of effective marketing." When teams use AI to create content, outputs often drift and feel off-brand.

His process:
- Collect media the brand has already produced.
- Ask AI to define the brand voice and persona from that content.
- Refine and finalize the document, then share it with the whole team.
- The document contains prompts that keep tone consistent no matter who generates the content.

Pro tip: instruct the AI to pull specific vocabulary from the brand's existing content. This builds a corpus of brand-specific terms so AI output always sounds on-brand. "Each person has words they tend to use more often than others. Brands should have this too to make them feel human."

Related tool he lists: CSS Peeper, a Chrome extension that reads a site's CSS and builds a brand style guide (fonts, hex codes, icons).

## Programmatic SEO agency for Shopify collection pages

Cody's logic: Etsy is a billion-dollar company because it has millions of long-tail landing pages (he cites 13M). "This is how marketplaces actually grow." Previously this required a team of engineers; now AI can do it.

The service: generate programmatic long-tail collection pages for Shopify stores with many SKUs.

How he would find customers:

- Use a Python script to crawl them and count products.
- Cold email owners of stores with high SKU counts.
- Find the emails with Hunter.io.

## LinkedIn polls that turn into email course leads

Cody runs LinkedIn polls built to generate leads:
- Ask a controversial question and hide the results until someone votes. Voting (a click) is needed to see outcomes, which drives engagement.
- More engagement on a post means more reach.
- Everyone who votes gets a push notification when the poll ends, which brings them back to the post for a second round of engagement.
- Before they return, he edits the post to add a call to action to join his 7-day email course. That converts returning voters into leads.

## Cold email as a signup channel (Swell AI test)

Cody sent about 2,000 cold emails to Swell AI prospects, optimizing for opens and signups rather than replies.

- Open rates went above 50%.
- About 1% of the people emailed signed up for Swell AI, which he found by comparing signup data against cold email data for the same period.
- Extrapolation: 100,000 emails would yield around 1,000 signups, and about 5% of signups convert to paying customers.
- He says he doesn't fully believe the math but plans to try scaling it and share the data.

## Newsletter referral reward ideas

Cody shared takeaways from a 45-minute podcast on newsletter growth that he hadn't considered before. Referral reward types:
- Digital products (eBooks, checklists, exclusive articles) as immediate rewards for 1 to 3 referrals.
- Personal shoutouts in the newsletter, such as for a referral or a birthday.
- Swag (stickers, hats, shirts) for higher referral counts, though it may be less effective than it used to be, so keep rewards fresh.
- Access to private groups, extra content or special Q&As, to create exclusivity.
- Giveaways where each referral earns an entry for a desirable item (AirPods, tablets).
- Cross-promotions: promote a subscriber's content or services if they hit a referral count, appealing to creators and businesses in the audience.
