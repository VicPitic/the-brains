## What storytelling marketing is

Cody defines storytelling marketing as "the process of creating story narrative where the product is a character." The product is hidden inside a story rather than pitched directly.

- His example: a rainy camping scene in Glacier National Park where a "whisper light camp stove" warms the group with Guatemalan coffee. The product sits in the narrative without being announced.
- The story casts a vision of the product in someone's life, typically in an aspirational way.
- It lets people "sit in someone else's shoes, in someone else's problem" and see how the product would make their life better.

## Why storytelling works for marketing

- He says storytelling is "a part of the fabric of being human." People connect with stories.
- Marketing is the expression of how a product helps people solve a problem. A story paints a portrait of the moment that problem happens and shows the product as the solution.
- He calls storytelling "a cheat" for three jobs at once: capturing a person's interest, illustrating a problem, and showcasing the product or service as the solution.

## The three-part story structure

Cody says storytelling marketing is always composed of three things:

1. **A problem**
2. **A journey**
3. **A solution** (the product)

- Example: Google's first real commercial, "Parisian Love." It shows someone trying to impress a French girl (problem), their falling in love (journey), and Google Search helping the protagonist (solution).

## Storytelling in Facebook ads

- Facebook is already a place where users tell stories digitally through text, video and images, so he sees it as a strong fit for storytelling marketing.
- Ads that cast a vision of people facing a challenge and overcoming it with a product, using the problem/journey/solution model above, "will get the cheapest traffic from Facebook."

## Storytelling in email marketing

- He notes email is one of the oldest forms of digital marketing, and people are notorious for forwarding stories (his example: a grandmother forwarding an elaborate story that made the reader feel something).
- The tactic is to tap into the natural desire to share stories that make us feel.
- He names three sources of feeling to build emails around:
  - Learning something new.
  - Hearing about someone overcoming an impossible obstacle.
  - Sparking irritation at a frustrating fact of life.

## Books he recommends for marketing storytelling

- **Stories That Stick** (Kindra Hall): the bridge for any gap in a business is a good story. Hall shows leaders the four unique stories they already have to captivate and convert audiences.
- **Brand Storytelling**: using a narrative to connect a brand to customers by linking what it stands for to shared values.
- **Unleash the Power of Storytelling**: a practical roadmap for crafting and delivering more persuasive stories to get more of what you want in career and life.

## How to hire a storytelling marketing agency

- Look for people with "a large volume of empathy." Empathetic people make the best storytellers because they can identify other people's problems and write narratives from those people's perspective.
- The agency must also understand how stories get distributed through social media.
- His rule: if they can't empathize and also distribute those stories with modern media, "don't hire them."
