## Digital gravity: the model

- Cody defines digital gravity as "the sphere of influence you have on the digital world." Like real-world size and reputation, it has to be earned.
- Analogy: gravity is about relative size. The moon orbits the Earth and the Earth orbits the Sun because of relative mass.
- Unlike planets, digital planets can grow. Cody names three growth levers: brand, answering questions about the product, and answering questions about the industry.
- He says it is simple in concept but complex in practice. It takes smart teams, investment in digital marketing infrastructure, an understanding of how the internet works as a whole, and daily discipline. "It's a grueling, multi-year process. But the most impactful things are hard."
- Context: he wrote this in 2019 as Director of Digital Strategy at R+M, which helps large corporations move from traditional to digital media.

## Why big companies have to teach the internet they're big

- Cody came to R+M in early 2018 from bootstrapped startups, where the early problem is finding product-market fit and getting people to trust you.
- Startup formula for looking bigger and more trustworthy: be where your target customers expect to get information about a company like yours. That could be Google, Medium, YouTube or LinkedIn, and every industry has a different mix.
- His rule: your customers spend time somewhere online, so be there as much as you can, "solving problems and providing value."
- He was surprised that R+M's clients (publicly traded, international supply chains, vertically integrated "from mine to table") were at the same starting point online as young startups.
- Core point: a real-world footprint does not translate online. "You have to teach the internet you're big" by growing your digital footprint.

## Step 1: Make the website market the brand

- Cody's rule: a website exists "to market brand. Period."
- Most companies already have a website when they start with R+M. He says these sites are often confused about why they exist and are not optimized for brand.
- First investment: make the website take the real-world brand online.
- The goal is to make search engines, social media and the internet at large understand the who, what and why of the brand, so the brand exists online as it does in the real world.

## Step 2: Answer questions about the product

- After brand, the next phase is answering the questions people ask about the client's products.
- People ask these questions in the real world, so the answers should be published online in a form that both people and search robots can find.
- He notes that robots map the gravity of the web and that human language alone is hard for robots to understand.
- This is the client's first real content. It comes as text, video and sound bites, often several media combined, to create the best answer to each product question.

## Step 3: Answer questions about the industry

- Once brand and product questions are exhausted, move to higher-level industry questions that are not directly about the product but relate to "the DNA of the brand."
- Purpose: signal to the digital world where the company sits in the digital universe.
- Order of work is specific to generic, and brand to industry. Cody says this order lets you "clone the company's real world size online."

## Results and why the early investment matters

- Cody reports that cloning real-world size online had a large effect. Clients needed to add another shift (staffing) and had their best sales quarters ever, after only 6 months of work.
- He shared a graph of one client metric, one of hundreds tracked, showing online gravity growth.
- Why do it: the brand gains "digital mass to throw around online in its industry."
- Early investment in digital footprint puts the brand so far ahead of competitors that, he argues, 20 years later it will be impossible for other firms to catch up. "It is hard to stop a star once it's burning."
