## AI avatar UGC ad agency for supplement brands

- Old UGC process: Cody paid creators about $100 per video and mailed them the product. He got one script read once, with no hook variations. Ten ad variations cost about $1,000, and one in ten hit.
- The new unlock: AI avatar tools (he names make UGC.ai) now let an avatar hold a physical product. You pick an avatar, upload product photos, and the tool composites them. Cody says this wasn't possible until recently.
- He now generates "a thousand different pieces of creative" across ~10 avatars, ~100 hooks and ~100 scripts, with no product shipped.
- Search interest in this category went from 1,600 to 4,300 searches a month over a year.
- Niche: supplement and consumable e-commerce brands, which need someone holding the product. They are high-margin, recurring, and spend a double-digit percentage of revenue on ads.
- Why it fits: the best brands now run broad Facebook targeting with a conversion event and compete on ad creative, testing a hundred pieces a month.
- Offer: about 25 videos a week. You do the hook research, generation and editing, and deliver them with a dashboard of what's working. You don't run the ads, so you don't compete with their ads agency.
- Each week's next batch is built from the prior winners. Once a format wins, the brand can have a human creator remake it better.
- Pricing and scale: about $5K/month per client. 16 clients is roughly $80K/month, or a million a year. Cody says 25 ads take about two hours, so ~32 hours a week for 16 clients. He knows agencies charging about $8K/month for 10 videos.
- The human job is curator: generate 50, deliver the best 25, and add ~5 free extras as a kickback.
- Prospecting: find supplement brands doing ~$10M a year with ~5 people and email all five. Include a Google Drive folder with five example ads already made, built from researched pain points.
- Principle: sell to people "addicted to ads" who already know ads work, not people you must convince to start.
- Niche down further, e.g. creatine gummies for women long-distance runners. Scrape Reddit quotes on their pain points, then write hooks and scripts from those quotes.

## Finding pain points from Reddit and turning them into businesses

- Process: prompt Perplexity with something like "what are the main pain points med spas have in growing the business Reddit". Adding the word "Reddit" is enough, and deep research modes work too.
- Example output for med spas: client acquisition and retention, staffing turnover, over-reliance on discount platforms like Groupon, reputation, facility standards.
- Each pain point maps to a possible business: lead gen, a staffing pipeline, social media management, cleaning.
- He rules out spaces he doesn't want to touch (financing, staffing).
- Math check: med spa customers visit roughly four to eight times a year, and he puts lifetime value around $5K. A paying customer acquired for ~$1,000 is about a 5x return, so you can charge a lot for leads.
- Principle: "A business is two things at its core. You have something people want to buy and you can sell it." Once you find something people hate doing, you do it for pay.
- Online businesses deserve the same mindset as local ones. Tier-one US cities have 80+ window washing companies that all make money at different levels. Likewise there can be a thousand Redfin scrapers. A small, automated product doing a couple grand a month with no staff and Stripe revenue is a real business.

## Med spa lead gen: targeting and local SEO

- Targeting: women ~30 to 50 in roughly the top 5% of income, via Facebook ads promoting individual services.
- Pick the richest neighborhoods in the town and improve Google Maps listings there.
- Local SEO steps:
  - Put keywords in the listing name and description.
  - List the service offerings.
  - Add photos of the interior so it looks great.
  - Submit directory listings with identical name, address and phone to MapQuest, Foursquare, Yelp and similar sites.
- Matching citations help the business rank higher in the Google Maps pack for "med spa near me". The top three organic Maps results get most of the clicks.
- Add-ons: encourage word of mouth with discounts for customers who post on social, and run referral incentives.

## Webinars turned into lead-magnet ebooks

- Market: Cody says over 60% of B2B businesses run a webinar every month. So almost every company hosts one at least yearly, and you can email any marketer.
- Offer: turn each webinar into a written lead magnet (ebook/PDF). The client posts the insights on LinkedIn ("comment for the free guide"), people hand over their email, and the list is used to promote the next webinar. He calls it a flywheel.
- Capturing commenters: a tool like Phantom Buster, or VAs, or a sales rep watching comments. Commenters are buyer signals, and for high-ticket offers they're worth manual outreach.
- Finding leads: on YouTube, search "webinar", filter to this week and over 20 minutes. Don't be put off by videos with ~600 views.
- Outreach: find the company on Apollo, email its marketers, and DM them on LinkedIn: "I saw you posted this webinar last week. I turned it into an ebook lead magnet for you. Here it is for free." Pitch an ongoing service.
- Production: take the transcript, drop it into Claude, ask for an ebook with key insights and takeaways, and finish in Canva.
- Pricing: about $1,000 per webinar (four webinars a month is $4K), or an unlimited plan around $8K/month that also covers the back catalog.
- Proof: at Rupa Health he ran a weekly Wednesday live course series, built from social and an email newsletter. It grew from nothing to 4,000 people per webinar.
- Niche down: pick one industry (biotech, cybersecurity). Cold outreach then name-drops companies the prospect knows, as in "SEO for e-commerce beauty brands".

## City-specific cheap flight deals newsletter

- Critique: Scott's Cheap Flights (now going.com) finds flights that are just cheap, but people want cheap flights from where they live. So unbundle it by city.
- Build: set up Google Flights price tracking with "any dates" on a dedicated Google account, e.g. Dallas to London, Barcelona, Amsterdam, Tokyo. Alerts arrive in an inbox, then an automation rewrites each into your newsletter format with AI and links to the flight.
- Acquire with Facebook ads to a signup form (a Beehiiv newsletter works). Launch in Dallas, get the playbook, then copy it to every major city.
- Monetize: a subscription, kickbacks from partners, and ad slots sold across the whole network. A million travelers across all the city lists is a sellable audience for a hotel chain's promotion.
- Scale reference: Scott's Cheap Flights reported about $3.8M in 2020. Going.com does about $35M a year now.
- He says it can be set up in a weekend and automated end to end. A $20M/year company would take time and some capital.
- He's written about half the script for it but lacks time.

## Nextdoor marketing for a lawn care business

- Tactic from an 18-year-old running a lawn care business entirely on organic Nextdoor posts.
- Mow a lawn, then post before and after photos: "I just did [address] in your neighborhood. If you want this, message me."
- Social proof is built in. A real neighbor lives at that address, the photos match what the house looks like, and neighbors see the result.
- Cody says it generates a ridiculous amount of inbound.
- He says to speed it up by knocking on doors and offering to do a lawn free. Someone will say yes, and the post is the marketing.
