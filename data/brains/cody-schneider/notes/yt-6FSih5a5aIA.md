## Why productized AI services beat AI automation agencies

- Cody says he has watched many friends start AI automation agencies. In his telling, they last 12 to 18 months and then pivot hard into a productized service.
- He thinks the business model is weak:
  - You need strong top-of-funnel lead generation.
  - You need strong sales skills, because the tickets are fairly high and the sale is traditional.
  - Revenue is one-time and project-based, not recurring. It is "a hard business to build and scale."
- His alternative is a productized service with a fixed monthly deliverable, produced with AI tooling. It gives recurring revenue, can run remotely, and removes the constant scramble for lead volume to cover payroll.
- He says a solo operator can plausibly reach $40K to $80K a month within about 6 months with this model.
- Every idea he gives follows the same pattern:
  - a niche with high customer value
  - an AI-automated deliverable on a monthly cadence
  - public case-study content for acquisition
  - reporting tied as close to revenue (usually leads) as possible

## Idea 1: Med spa marketing ($2K-3K/month)

- Med spas average roughly $1.4M to $2M in revenue per location. Customer lifetime value is about $6,000, so clients can afford a retainer.
- Purchase decisions come mostly from paid social (Instagram, Facebook, maybe TikTok) and local SEO, so the service covers two things: Facebook ads and Google Maps optimization.
- **Ad creative (about 10 new pieces a month):**
  - Static images come from the ChatGPT API.
  - Video comes from HeyGen or Arc Ads.
  - Research comes from Perplexity, scraping Reddit and social for the problems, benefits and language around each med spa treatment.
  - Scripts and descriptions are written by AI.
  - Nothing here needs a graphic designer or video editor.
- **Google Maps:**
  - Searching "med spa" in any location shows a map pack, so ranking in the top three matters.
  - Optimize the profile for reviews, keywords in the listing, and images of the physical location.
  - Add directory/citation listings (name, address, phone) on sites like Foursquare. Cody mentions local rank.so for one-click submission to all of them.
- **Reporting:** report on leads and new revenue. This is how you justify the fee. He suggests Looker Studio or Graph, a "chat with data" dashboard tool his team launched that week.
- **Acquisition:**
  - Med spa contact details are public, so you can cold-reach them.
  - Run Facebook ads targeting people who work at med spas, with case studies and a lead magnet PDF that walks through your exact process. Readers see it is painful to do themselves and hire you.
  - Post YouTube case studies with titles like "I grew this med spa by X revenue from Facebook ads" or "I got 100 leads from Google Maps for this med spa." He says "just talk your book."

## Idea 2: Facebook ad creative for beauty e-commerce brands ($3K-5K/month)

- Beauty brands burn through creative fast, because audiences see the same ad too many times. Fresh creative is business-critical, and e-commerce ad agencies are a fast-growing category.
- **AI workflow:**
  - Use a research agent (many n8n-style builds exist on YouTube) to scrape product reviews, extract pain points, find hooks and run competitor analysis.
  - Make the videos with MakeUGC.ai. Its differentiator is that the on-screen person can hold the actual product, which beauty brands need.
  - Deliver something like 20 ads a month, plus analysis of top performers and remixes of them.
- **Where the hooks come from:**
  - Scraping competitors' ad libraries makes everyone's ads look the same.
  - Cody prefers mining Reddit for odd phrasing, for example how someone describes an acne cream. Use Claude to extract the quote and turn it into the hook.
  - Copy competitors' formats (selfie video, static) but not their language, to get pattern disruption in the feed.
- **Pricing context:** traditional human UGC agencies charge about $6,000 for five videos, so an AI offer with more volume at a lower price wins.
- **Strategy:** more creative variations, tested faster, with broad targeting on Facebook, TikTok or Twitter ads. He argues the platform algorithms find buyers better than manual targeting does, so your job is the creative production process.
- **Acquisition:**
  - Publish YouTube breakdowns of how you use the research agent.
  - Post LinkedIn and Twitter breakdowns of ad types and formulas, with a discovery-call CTA.
  - He also endorses "comment to get the asset" posts, such as "I scraped 100 beauty ads, comment 'ads' for the library." It can be a plain digital download or a Google Drive link after an email capture.

## Idea 3: Organic social media management for nutrition coaches (about $2K/month)

- Cody is "obsessed" with nutrition and health coaches. He believes any coaching business is disruptable by AI.
- Coaches serve about 100 clients a month, but lifetime value per client is small (about $1,000). That means they need an organic strategy, because paid ads are hard to justify at that value.
- Searches for "nutrition coach" have grown steadily over the last couple of years.
- **Deliverable:**
  - One post a day.
  - AI-generated food/recipe images (the Pinterest-style approach) combined with client stories.
  - A lead magnet such as a 100-day recipe ebook, which captures emails into a drip nurture.
  - Success is measured in impressions and leads.
- **Tooling:** Assembly (assembly.com) lets you connect many social accounts in one portal. Clients approve each post in a client-facing view, so a solo operator could run the whole business on it. Cody thinks one person can manage 10 accounts.
- **Acquisition:**
  - Cold DMs to nutrition coaches on Instagram, where he sees the ecosystem living.
  - Facebook ads targeting nutrition coaches.
  - An email-gated educational YouTube video.
  - YouTube case studies ("how I grew a client's social account and the leads we got").

## Idea 4: Lead magnets from webinars

- Over half of B2B marketers hosted a webinar in the last year. Their pain points are:
  - getting people to register
  - getting people to show up
  - doing something with the recording afterward
- **The service:** turn a company's back catalog of webinars into ebooks, white papers and other lead magnets, delivered as a monthly quota (for example 4 or 8 a month). Cody describes it as turning "rotting fruit" into juice.
- **Production:** transcribe the webinars with AI tools, turn the transcripts into bolded lists, and format them into branded PDFs.
- **Acquisition:**
  - Do the work first. Reach out to companies running webinars with an ebook you already made from their webinar, then offer the service on a discovery call. You "show your work before you're even working with" them.
  - On LinkedIn, post a "comment to get my webinar marketing guide" lead magnet, since their measure of success is attendance.
- **Why webinars work:**
  - Free educational content builds trust, especially for high-ticket sales.
  - Short-form video will never sell a $3K to $10K+ per month service. Use it to feed top of funnel into long-form content. Webinars can be the bridge.
  - Cody's example is Rupa Health. They grew an email newsletter on the back of the podcast, then promoted weekly webinars through it. About 6,000 people registered and about 1,000 attended each time. The webinar was basically a demo of how to use the product (how to do a lab test the product offers).

## Idea 5: Data analytics as a service for SMBs

- Businesses sit on large amounts of data they cannot interpret, and more founders want to make data-driven decisions but lack the skills.
- **Offer:** "unlimited data analytics" for SMBs at a flat monthly fee.
  - The client asks questions in a Slack channel or message thread, and you answer as fast as possible.
  - You build dashboards and a weekly KPI report.
  - Data sources include the CRM, QuickBooks (cash flow, P&L), customer counts and Google Analytics.
- **Tooling:**
  - Claude or ChatGPT connected to data through MCPs.
  - Looker Studio, Tableau or Power BI (aggressive learning curves, but tutorials are on YouTube).
  - Graph, which he says turns a connected data source or CSV into dashboards and insights.
- **Example customer:** a local operator running 10 children's swimming locations who wants to know cash flow and P&L.

## Cross-cutting content and distribution lessons

- **Faceless video works.** Cody's winery-industry friends run faceless ads: a voiceover monologue over social footage of the tasting room and vineyards. It feels like organic content and performs "absolutely ripping."
- **Static image plus audio works.** One of his Instagram posts, a static image of a tweet with audio, got 195,000 likes. He says the content has to be outstanding for any of these formats to work.
- **Staying current is part of the value.** Formats from six months ago stop working. Part of what clients pay for is that you keep a pulse on what currently works and refresh the content, because you see many clients at once.
- **Case-study content is the main acquisition engine.** Show the work publicly on YouTube (with numbers in the title), then LinkedIn and Twitter, with a discovery call or lead magnet as the CTA.
- **"Comment to get it" posts** on LinkedIn create an engagement flywheel that generates leads.
- **Prove value close to revenue.** The closer your reporting is to leads or revenue, the easier it is to show the work is worth the money.

## Voice agents and agentic browsing

- Cody is most excited about agents that actually finish tasks "to the last mile," after a lot of vaporware. He has been testing which agent platforms really get there.
- He likes Perplexity Comet because it lets you do recurring browser tasks without an automation tool. His example is accepting LinkedIn connection requests every morning and sending a set message.
- He increasingly wants to talk to websites and companies (he uses Superwhisper for most communication) and wonders whether apps become a conversational layer over information.
- **Voice-agent example, medical:**
  - His brother's company in the medical space built a large knowledge document and a voice agent that anyone can phone.
  - It answers every buyer question and is trained to educate rather than sell. Callers stay on for about 20 minutes, then transfer to a human call center to book.
  - A human could never learn that much specific information.
- **Voice-agent example, restaurants:**
  - A friend's company builds voice agents that take restaurant orders, send payment links and enter orders into the POS (such as Toast). It is growing fast.
  - Customers want the agent to sound like the brand. A New York bagel shop wanted the original owner's thick accent, and a New Jersey pizzeria wanted an in-store feel.
- **Business idea:** help companies become voice-first by building branded voice agents with characters, lore and origin stories, using platforms like Vapi.
