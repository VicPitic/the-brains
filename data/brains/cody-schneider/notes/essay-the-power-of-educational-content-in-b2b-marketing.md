## Educational content as the foundation of B2B marketing (digital gravity)

Cody argues that most B2B brands now know they need content, but much of it is "just sales BS in disguise and provides zero value to prospects." His instruction: "Give this up. Do it right now." Instead, make purely educational content with a strong call to action to learn more about the product.

- He calls giving without expecting anything back "digital gravity."
- Increasing your "digital mass" online gives you more influence in your target market.
- Educational content works because it builds trust before a prospect ever talks to sales.

## Start from what the sales team wishes prospects knew

When Cody works with B2B clients, one of the first questions he asks is: what does your sales team wish prospects knew before ever talking to you? He says he already knows the answers, but the exercise is valuable regardless.

Typical answers: the product exists, what the product is, the problem it solves, what it does, who the company is, and that the company and its people can be trusted. In other words, the sales team wants leads to be "buyer ready."

- His closing challenge: talk to your sales teams, ask what they wish leads knew before talking to them, then build marketing initiatives that solve those gaps.
- He pairs two tactics (below) to create buyer-ready leads with "low mental energy input."
- He scores each tactic against the list to see which gaps it closes.

## Tactic 1: Educational content hosted by a team member

The easiest way to make highly educational content, per Cody, is to bring thought leaders from your industry in and have them share their expertise, with someone on your team "hosting."

- **Offboards idea generation.** Your job shifts from creating unique ideas to curating and sharing them.
- **Elevates your brand.** Hosting the content associates you and your brand with it.
- **Builds an owned media library.** It becomes a content engine you can mine whenever you want to repurpose content elsewhere.
- **What it solves on the list:** who the company is, and trust in the company and its people. It does not solve what the product is, the problem it solves, or what it does.

## Tactic 2: Remarketing ungated demos to B2B audiences

Cody says it "literally blows my mind that there are still gated demos in 2022." His view is that B2B customers want to learn everything they can about a product before they talk to anyone, so "give the people what they want."

Steps he lays out:

1. Scrape the emails of your target customers.
2. Import the lists as customer match lists on every platform where people spend time online.
3. Create product demos.
4. Remarket those demos to the customer match audiences, plus anyone who touches your website (install the pixel).

- B2B purchase decisions have a long time horizon, so demos that show how the product improves people's lives educate buyers over time.
- Result: customers who "deeply understand your product before they even talk to your sales team."
- **What it solves on the list:** that the product exists and what it is, the problem it solves, and what it does. It does not build trust in the company or explain who the company is.

## Why the two tactics work as a pair

Cody's scorecard approach shows the tactics are complementary, covering opposite halves of the buyer-ready list:

| Gap | Hosted educational content | Remarketed demos |
|---|---|---|
| Product exists / what it is | Partly | Yes |
| Problem the product solves | No | Yes |
| What the product does | No | Yes |
| Who the company is | Yes | No |
| Trust in company and people | Yes | No |

Together they leave prospects knowing the product, the problem, the solution, and the people behind it before the first sales call.
